Charlie Parker, also known as "Bird" or "Yardbird", was an American jazz saxophonist, composer and bandleader. He was a leading figure in the development of bebop, a style of jazz characterized by advanced harmonies, virtuosic technique and fast tempos.

Charlie Parker, also known as "Bird" or "Yardbird", was an American jazz saxophonist, composer and bandleader. He was a leading figure in the development of bebop, a style of jazz characterized by advanced harmonies, virtuosic technique and fast tempos.

early life and education

Charles Parker Jr. was born on August 29, 1920, in Kansas City, Kansas, to Charles Parker Sr. and Adelaide "Addie" Bailey. He began playing the saxophone at age 11 and joined the Lincoln High School band under bandmaster Alonzo Lewis at age 14. His early musical influence was a young trombone player named Robert Simpson, who taught him basics of improvisation. Parker withdrew from high school in December 1935 to pursue his musical career full-time.

development of bebop

While living in New York City in 1939, Parker achieved a musical breakthrough by developing a new improvisational vocabulary known as bebop. This occurred during a practice session with guitarist William "Biddy" Fleet, while playing the song "Cherokee", where he realized that the 12 semitones of the chromatic scale could lead melodically to any key. He later joined after-hours clubs in Harlem venues such as Clark Monroe's Uptown House, experimenting with other musicians including Thelonious Monk and Dizzy Gillespie.

recording career

Parker primarily recorded for the labels Savoy, Dial, and Verve. He also recorded a series of popular sessions with strings, produced by Norman Granz, which placed him in the context of a {// a small classical string section and a jazz rhythm section rather than his standard bebop quintet. These recordings were his most popular sellers during his lifetime and were admitted to the Grammy Hall of Fame in 1988.
