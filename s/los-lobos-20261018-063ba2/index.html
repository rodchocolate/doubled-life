Los Lobos is an American rock group from East Los Angeles, California. Their music is influenced by various styles including rock and roll, Tex-Mex, country, zydeco, folk, R&B, blues, brown-eyed soul, and traditional music such as boleros, cumbia and norteños.

Los Lobos is an American rock group from East Los Angeles, California. Their music is influenced by various styles including rock and roll, Tex-Mex, country, zydeco, folk, R&B, blues, brown-eyed soul, and traditional music such as boleros, cumbia and norteños.

formation and early career

The group was formed in 1973 by vocalist and guitarist David Hidalgo and drummer Louie Pérez, who met at Garfield High School. They were joined by Conrad Lozano, Cesar Rosas and Frank González. The band's first album, Los Lobos del Este de Los Angeles, was recorded in 1977 over four months across two Hollywood studios. Between 1974 and 1980, the group performed at hundreds of weddings and dances, experimenting with traditional Mexican music. Originally called Los Lobos del Este (de Los Angeles), the name was later shortened to Los Lobos.

commercial success and discography

The band rose to international stardom in 1987 when their version of 'La Bamba' peaked at the top of the Billboard Hot 100, and topped charts in several other countries including the United Kingdom. They released several albums including ...And a Time to Dance (an EP), 'How Will the Wolf Survive?' (inspired by a National Geographic article), 'By the Light of the Moon' and 'La pistola y el corazón'. The album Kiko, produced by Mitchell Froom, was voted 'Album of the year' by the Chicago Sun-Times and Los Angeles Times. In 1993, they released a two-CD collection titled Just Another Band from East L.A.

facts

  • Origin: East Los Angeles, California
