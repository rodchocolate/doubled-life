Charles Lloyd is an American jazz musician and composer born on March 15, 1938. He primarily plays tenor saxophone and flute, though he occasionally uses other reed instruments like the Hungarian tárogató. Since 2007, his primary band has been a quartet consisting of drummer Eric Harland, acoustic bassist Reuben Rogers, and pianist Jason Moran.

early life and education

Born in Memphis, Tennessee, Lloyd grew up exposed to blues, gospel, and jazz. He is of African, Cherokee, Mongolian, and Irish ancestry. After receiving his first saxophone at age nine, he was influenced by 1940s radio broadcasts of artists such as Duke Ellington, Billie Holiday, Lester Young, Coleman Hawkins and Charlie Parker. His early teachers were saxophonist Irvin Reason and pianist Phineas Newborn, Jr. As a teenager, he played jazz with Frank Strozier, Harold Mabern and George Coleman, and served as a sideman for R&B singer Johnny Ace and blues artists B.B. King, Howlin' Wolf and Bobby "Blue" Bland. In 1956, he moved to Los Angeles to study music at the University of Southern California, where he studied with Halsey Stevens. While there, he played in jazz clubs with artists such as Charlie Haden, Don Cherry, Eric Dolphy, Scott LaFaro, Billy Higgins, Ornette Coleman and Bobby Hutcherson, and was a member of Gerald Wilson's big band.

professional career

In 1960, Lloyd became the music director of Chico Hamilton's group, arranging and writing almost all the music for the albums Passin' Thru and Man from Two Worlds. He also collaborated with Nigerian drummer Babatunde Olatunji. In 1964, he joined the Cannonball Adderley Sextet, remaining for two years and crediting Adderley for his development as a leader. He signed with CBS Records in 1964 and recorded Discovery! and Of Course, Of Course, which led to him being voted DownBeat magazine's "New Star." He was also a notable supporting musician for The Beach Boys during their live performances.

the classic quartet and world music

In 1966, Lloyd formed his "classic quartet" with bassist Cecil McBee (later continued by Ron McClure), pianist Keith Jarrett and drummer Jack DeJohnette. The group's 1966 live album Forest Flower, recorded at the Monterey Jazz Festival, was highly successful in the mid-1960s, attracting a diverse audience of jazz and rock fans within the hippie counterculture. In 1967, DownBeat magazine voted him "Jazz Artist of the Year." During a tour of Estonia and Russia, they performed at the Tallinn Jazz Festival and venues in Leningrad with the help of Moscow and Leningrad Jazz Clubs. Lloyd is credited with anticipating world music by incorporating elements from other cultures into his compositions starting in the late 1950s.

later career and ecm records

setelah 1970s, Lloyd practically disappeared from the jazz scene while practicing Transcendental Meditation. During this period, he played extensively with The Beach Boys on studio recordings and as a member of the touring band, recording at Brian Wilson's home studio. He was also a member of the band Celebration. After returning to the jazz world in 1981 with a tour featuring Michel Petrucciani, he rededicated himself to music in 1986 following a nearly fatal medical condition. In 1988, he formed a new quartet with Bobo Stenson. Starting in 1989, he recorded for ECM Records, producing albums such as Fish Out of Water, Canto, Voice in the Night and The Water Is Wide. His later work includes collaborations with Greek singer Maria Farantouri and his New Quartet recordings Mirror and Rabo de Nube.
