Ripuarian dialects are a group of West Central German dialects spoken in the Rhineland south of the Benrath line. They are part of the larger Central Franconian dialect family and exist within a linguistic continuum with Low Franconian languages.

Ripuarian dialects are a group of West Central German dialects spoken in the Rhineland south of the Benrath line. They are part of the larger Central Franconian dialect family and exist within a linguistic continuum with Low Franconian languages.

geographic distribution

The Ripuarian language area extends from northwest of Düsseldorf and Cologne to Aachen in the west and Waldbröl in the east. It also includes the southern edge of the Limburg province of the Netherlands, specifically Kerkrade (Kirchroa), and the north of the German-speaking Community of Belgium. In Kerkrade, it is perceived as a variety of Limburgish and legally treated as such. Speakers are centered on the German city of Cologne (Köln).

varieties and dialects

There are more than one hundred Ripuarian dialects, most of which are bound to a specific municipality or village. These dialects often call themselves 'Platt', such as Öcher Platt (Aachen), Bönnsch Platt (Bonn), Eischwiele Platt (Eschweiler), Kirchröadsj plat (of Kerkrade), and Bocheser plat (of Bocholtz). The most well-known variety is Kölsch, the local dialect of Cologne. Varieties include West Ripuarian around Aachen, East Belgium, and the Netherlands; Central Ripuarian; City Colognian (Stadtkölnisch); and Country Colognian (Landkölnisch).

linguistic features

Ripuarian dialects share a common phonemic pitch accent known as the 'singing' Rhinelandic tone. They are related to Moselle Franconian and Low Franconian Limburgish, with roots in Middle German and influences from Latin, Dutch, French, Low German, and Southern Meuse-Rhenish. Some grammar elements are unique to Ripuarian and do not exist in other German languages. In the particular case of Kölsch, it has a larger vowel system than Standard German and is more non-rhotic, often vocalizing 'r' completely.

speaker population

Approximately one million people speak a variation of Ripuarian dialect, constituting about one quarter of the inhabitants of the area. The number of passive understanders far exceeds the number of active speakers. Usage varies by location; in some areas, only elderly speakers remain, while in other places it remains common in everyday life.

facts

  • Family: Central Franconian
  • Number of Speakers: Approximately one million active speakers
  • Most Well-Known Dialect: Kölsch
  • Origin of Name: Derived from the Ripuarian Franks who settled in the area from the 4th century onward
  • Official Recognition: The European Union, the Netherlands, and the French Community of Belgium officially recognize some Ripuarian dialects as minority languages
