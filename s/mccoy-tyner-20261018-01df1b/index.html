Alfred McCoy Tyner (1938–2020) was an influential American jazz pianist and composer who gained prominence through his work with the John Coltrane Quartet from 1960 to 1965. An NEA Jazz Master and five-time Grammy Award winner, he is recognized as one of the most recognizable and influential jazz pianists of all time.

early life and education

Born in Philadelphia, Pennsylvania, Tyner was encouraged to study piano by his mother. At age 13, he began lessons at the Granoff School of Music, studying music theory and harmony. He was influenced by Thelonious Monk's percussive attacks and bebop pianist Bud Powell. During his teens, he led a group called the Houserockers. At age 17, Tyner converted to Ahmadiyya and changed his name to Suleiman Saud, though he continued performing as McCoy Tyner.

the john coltrane quartet

Tyner joined the John Coltrane Quartet in 1960 after a brief period with The Jazztet. The quartet's classic lineup included bassist Jimmy Garrison and drummer Elvin Jones. Together they recorded several jazz classics, including A Love Supreme (1964) and Crescent (1964). Tyner remained with the group until 1965, leaving because Coltrane's music was becoming more atonal and free, which he felt he could no longer contribute to. He was replaced by Alice Coltrane.

solo career

Following his departure from the Coltrane Quartet, Tyner embarked on a career as a bandleader. After an initial period of struggle where he considered taking a day job as a taxi driver, he established himself through post-bop albums released by Blue Note between 1967 and 1970, including The Real McCoy (1967). This album features Joe Henderson, Ron Carter, and Elvin Jones. He later signed with Milestone Records, where he recorded 19 albums, including orchestral works like Song of the New World (1973) and Fly with the Wind (1976), before moving to Columbia.
