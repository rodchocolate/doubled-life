Sandra Smolen Jaffe (March 10, 1938 – December 27, 2021) was an American businesswoman and arts figure. She co-owned and operated Preservation Hall, a popular jazz club in New Orleans, beginning in 1961.

early life and education

Jaffe was born in Philadelphia, Pennsylvania, to Jacob "Jack" Smolen and Lena Kaplan Smolen. Her family was Jewish; her father was born in Kyiv, then part of the Russian Empire. She graduated from Harcum College in 1958.

career at preservation hall

Beginning in 1961, Jaffe co-owned and operated Preservation Hall with her husband Allan Jaffe. In this role, she handled bookkeeping, press correspondence, publicity, and collected admission at the entrance. She booked "real, old-time New Orleans jazz performers" such as Percy Humphrey, Alcide Pavageau, Sweet Emma Barrett, De De Pierce, and Billie Pierce. Due to the couple's policy of racial integration in the early 1960s, Jaffe was arrested at least once for violating local segregation laws.

later career and recognition

In 1963, she traveled with the Preservation Hall Jazz Band to Hawaii and Japan. In 1987, the Jaffes donated a building in the French Quarter for a music school. In 1993, she retired from running the jazz club and left its management to her son Ben Jaffe. In 2006, she accepted a National Medal of Arts award on behalf of the Preservation Hall Jazz Band.

personal life

Jaffe married Allan Jaffe in December 1960 and had two sons, Russell and Ben. Her husband died in 1987, and she passed away in the2021 at age 83 in New Orleans.

tribute

In February 2023, a tribute concert to the Jaffes was held at Preservation Hall.
