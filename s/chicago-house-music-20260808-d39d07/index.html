Chicago House Music is a type of electronic dance music created in Chicago by DJs and music producers from the city's queer Black underground club culture. It evolved in the early to mid-1980s as DJs began altering disco songs to create a more mechanical beat.

Chicago House Music is a type of electronic dance music created in Chicago by DJs and music producers from the city's queer Black underground club culture. It evolved in the early to mid-1980s as DJs began altering disco songs to create a more mechanical beat.

characteristics

The genre is typically characterized by repetitive 4/4 rhythms, including bass drums, off-beat hi-hats, snare drums, claps, and snaps at a tempo of 120 to 130 beats per minute. These sounds are often provided by electronic drum machines such as the Roland TR-808, TR-909, or TR-707. The music features synthesizer riffs, deep basslines, and may include sung, spoken, or sampled vocals. In early Chicago house, a signature rhythm riff is built on the clave pattern. Additional percussion like congas, bongos, or metallic percussion for a Latin feel can be added. Some subgenres, such as acid house, are defined by the squelchy sounds of the Roland TB-303 bass synthesizer. Production often uses sampled instruments and layering sounds, though the overall texture remains relatively sparse. Unlike pop music, the lower-pitched bass register is most important.

origins and terminology

The term 'house music' may have originated from a Chicago club called the Warehouse, which operated from 1977 to 1982. The club's resident DJ, Frankie Knuckles, is often referred to as the 'godfather of house'. Other theories suggest the name came from a sign in a bar window on Chicago's South Side, which DJ Leonard 'Remix' Rroy claimed he put there because it was music played in one's home. Another theory suggests that records at the Importes Etc. record store were labeled 'As Heard at the Warehouse', which was later shortened to 'House'. The genre evolved from DJs altering disco songs and became mainstream by early 1988.

title

House music expanded from Chicago to New York City and then internationally to cities like London, eventually becoming a worldwide phenomenon. It has had a large influence on pop music, especially dance music, and was incorporated into works by international artists such as Madonna, Lady Gaga, Whitney Houston, and Janet Jackson. Mainstream hits like 'Pump Up the Jam' by Technotronic and 'Show Me Love' by Robin S. were produced in this style. Many house DJs also perform remixes for pop artists.

pioneers

The genre was pioneered by Chicago-based DJs and producers including Frankie Knuckles, Ron Hardy, Jesse Saunders, Chip E., Joe Smooth, Steve 'Silk' Hurley, Farley 'Jackmaster' Funk, Marshall Jefferson, and Phuture.
