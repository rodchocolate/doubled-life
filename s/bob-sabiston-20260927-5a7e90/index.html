Bob Sabiston is an American computer programmer, film art director, and creator of the Rotoshop software program for computer animation. He began his development work as a researcher at the MIT Media Lab from 1986 to 1991.

software development

Sabiston developed Rotoshop, an interpolating rotoscope program designed to automate the interpolation of hand-drawn shapes and lines over video to make rotoscoping easier for artists. The software is proprietary and is currently only available through Sabiston's production company, Flat Black Films. Additionally, he created Inchworm Animation, a paint and animation program for the Nintendo DSi released in 2011, and its successor, Butterfly: Inchworm Animation II for the Nintendo 3DS, which added sound effects and native online sharing functions.

film work

Sabiston served as Head of Animation for Richard Linklater's films Waking Life (2001) and A Scanner Darkly (2006), where he modified the software substantially for the latter. For Waking Life, Sabiston hired thirty graphic artists in Austin, Texas, to assist in production. He also directed a series of shorts for the PBS show "Life360" and a short segment for Lars von Trier's film The Five Obstructions (2003). Other notable short films created by Sabiston include "God's Little Monkey" (1994), which won the Prix Ars Electronica Golden Nica award, and "Snack and Drink" (1999), which is part of the MOMA video collection.

app development

Since 2008, Sabiston has developed several iOS apps, including a drawing keyboard called Jot Keyboard, a modeling/3D-printing app named Voxel, a 3d mind-mapping app called Headspace, the video game Retroid, and in 2015, a tribute to Richard Garriott's Ultima II video game titled Lowlander.
