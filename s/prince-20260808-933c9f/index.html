etymology and historical background

The English word 'prince' derives from the French word prince, which comes from the Latin noun prīnceps, meaning 'the first, foremost, the chief, most distinguished, noble ruler, prince'. The Latin term prīnceps was used as a title for the informal leader of the Roman senate (princeps senatus) and later adopted by Emperor Augustus to establish the formal position of monarch. In medieval and early modern Europe, 'prince' was used as a generic term for any sovereign or quasi-sovereign ruler of a territory, regardless of their actual title or rank, a usage found in Niccolò Machiavelli's work Il Principe and the United States Declaration of Independence.

types of princely titles

The title of prince can be used generically to refer to members of a family that ruled by hereditary right or non-reigning descendants. As a substantive title, it was the lowest rank of monarch in post-Napoleonic Europe, such as the Princes of Monaco and Andorra. Additionally, popes and secular monarchs granted the title substantively to specific individuals and heads of high-ranking European families who did not exercise dynastic sovereignty. In some monarchies, heirs apparent use a specific princely title associated with a territory within the realm, such as the Prince of Wales in the U.K. or the Prince of Asturias in Spain.

the musician prince

Prince Rogers Nelson (1958–2016) was an American singer-songwriter, musician, dancer, actor, and filmmaker who pioneered the Minneapolis sound. He signed with Warner Bros. Records at age 18 and released 39 albums during his life, including For You, Purple Rain, and Sign o' the Times. In 1993, he changed his stage name to an unpronounceable symbol known as the 'Love Symbol', becoming known as the Artist Formerly Known as Prince (TAFKAP). He died in 2016 after accidentally overdosing on counterfeit hydrocodone/paracetamol pills laced with fentanyl.
