Kind of Blue is a studio album by American jazz musician Miles Davis, released on August 17, 1959, through Columbia Records. The album was recorded in New York City at Columbia's 30th Street Studio during two sessions in March and April 1959.

musical approach and influence

Davis departed from his early hard bop style to experiment with musical modes, basing the album entirely on modality. This approach gave performers a set of scales for improvisation and melody, which provided more creative freedom. Influenced in part by pianist Bill Evans and the theorist George Russell's Lydian Chromatic Concept of Tonal Organization, Davis had previously implemented this modal approach on the title track of his 1958 album Milestones. Coltrane later expanded on this modal approach in his own solo career.

recording process

The album was recorded on three-track tape across two sessions. On March 2, 1959, "So What", "Freddie Freeloader", and "Blue in Green" were recorded; on April 22, the tracks "All Blues" and "Lyamenco Sketches" were recorded. Davis provided the musicians with sketches of scales and melody lines rather than extensive rehearsal. While a legend exists that the album was recorded in one pass, only "Flamenco Sketches" yielded a complete take on the first try, though that take was not used as the master. The five master takes issued were the only other complete takes.

title: production and credits

The album was produced by Columbia staff producer Irving Townsend. While Teo Macero has sometimes received partial or full credit, jazz historian Eric Nisenson states that Macero's role was that of an apprentice and observer. Macero's first Davis production was the subsequent record, Sketches of Spain.

critical reception and legacy

Regarded by many critics as Davis's masterpiece and one of the greatest albums of all time, Kind of Blue has influenced jazz, rock, and classical music. In 2002, the Library of Congress selected it for inclusion in the National Recording Registry. In 2003, Rolling Stone magazine ranked it number 12 on its list of the '500 Greatest Albums of All Time', later repositioning it was number 31 in 2020. The RIAA certified the album quintuple platinum in 2019, and it is currently the second-best-selling jazz album in the United States based on RIAA certification.
