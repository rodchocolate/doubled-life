Vybz Kartel (Adidja Azim Palmer) is a Jamaican dancehall artist known by nicknames such as Worl' Boss, Teacha, and King of Dancehall. He has attained folk-hero status in Jamaica through his provocative lyrics and mischievous public persona.

early career and rise to prominence

Palmer began his career as a teenager in 1993 with the recording 'Love Fat Woman' under the moniker 'Adi Banton'. He was later part of a three-member group called Vybz Kartel, eventually keeping the name for himself. After becoming a protege of Bounty Killer, he rose to prominence in 2003 following a string of hit songs and a violent on-stage clash with Ninjaman at the Sting festival in his hometown of Portmore. He released albums through Greensleeves Records and established his own label, Adidjahiem/Notnice Records, with partner Ainsley 'Notnice' Morris. He later joined the Portmore Empire group of dancehall DJs and singers, signing them to his label.

controversies and feuds

Kartel has faced significant controversy over homophobic content in his lyrics, leading to a withdrawal of a UK MOBO Awards nomination in 2004. He was later banned from the airwaves in Guyana by the National Communications Network in 2011 due to 'obscene lyrics'. Additionally, he engaged in a public feud with former collaborator Mavado, which dominated Jamaican media and culture from 2006 to 2009. This conflict resulted in numerous diss tracks and led to street violence between factions known as Gaza and Gully, eventually requiring intervention by Jamaican Prime Minister Bruce Golding.

international success and legal issues

Kartel achieved international success with singles such as 'Romping Shop' (2009), 'Dancehall Hero' (2010), and 'Summer Time' (2011). His 2010 single 'Clarks' became one of his biggest hits, gaining significant radio play in North America. He collaborated with various hip hop and R&B artists including Rihanna, Missy Elliott, Travis Scott, and Major Lazer. In 2014, he was sentenced to life imprisonment for the murder of Clive 'Lizard' Williams. Despite this, he continued releasing music, including over 50 songs in 2016. His conviction was quashed by the UK Judicial Committee of the Privy Council in March 2024, and he was released from prison on July 31, 2024, after the Court of Appeal declined to retry the case.
