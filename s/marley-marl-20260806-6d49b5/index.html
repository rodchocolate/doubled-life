Marley Marl (born Marlon Lu'Ree Williams) is an American record producer, rapper, DJ, and record label founder primarily operating in hip-hop music. He grew up in the Queensbridge housing projects of Queens, New York.

early career and sampling

Marley Marl's career began with an interest in electronica and a stint as an intern at Unique Recording Studios in the early 1980s. While working there, he discovered sampling by accident during a session with artist Captain Rock. He later became the DJ for radio DJ Mr. Magic, after Mr. Magic heard his remix of Malcolm McLaren's "Roxanne's Revenge" (Buffalo Gals), and they together founded the hip-hop collective Juice Crew in 1983. In 1986, he served as an in-house producer for Cold Chillin' Records, where he earned $250,000 per year.

production style and influence

Marley Marl is credited with influencing hip-hop icons such as Pete Rock, Madlib, RZA, DJ Premier, and Biggie Smalls. Producer Madlib stated that Marley was the first producer to inspire him to make beats. He utilized the Roland TR-808 drum machine in inventive ways, often pairing 808 drum sounds with sampled drums to create a 'gritty' feel with a heavy bottom and warm feel. On MC Shan's "The Bridge", he used the 808 pulse to trigger different samplers.

juice crew and cold chillin' records

Marl Marl's big break came in 1984 with Roxanne Shante's hit "Roxanne's Revenge". He produced entire albums for several Juice Crew members, including Big Daddy Kane's Long Live The Kane (1988), Biz Markie's Goin' Off (1988), and MC Shan's Down By Law (1987). In 1988, he released the posse cut "The Symphony", which used a Hal Jackson record from the WBLS record library as a sample source.
