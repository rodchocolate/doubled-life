Michel Petrucciani was a French jazz pianist who lived from 28 December 1962 to 6 January 1999. He suffered from osteogenesis imperfecta, a genetic disease that caused brittle bones and short stature.

early life and education

Petrucciani was born in Orange, Vaucluse, to a musical family with Neapolitan heritage living in Montélimar. His father Tony and brother Philippe played guitar, and his brother Louis played bass. He began learning classical piano at age four, after initially smashing a toy piano with a hammer. He was heavily influenced by the pianist Bill Evans, whose music he started listening to around age ten. By the age of nine, he was making music with his family.

professional career

Petrucciani gave his first professional concert at 13, requiring aids to reach piano pedals. He moved to Paris at age fifteen, where he played with Kenny Clarke and Clark Terry. His breakthrough performance occurred at the Cliousclat jazz festival, where his virtuosity astounded the festival. After a stint in Paris, he moved to New York City in 1984, where he spent the rest of his life and the most productive period of his career. During this time, he recorded the album Live at the Village Vanguard, recorded on 16 March 1984 with bassist Palle Danielsson and drummer Eliot Zigmund.

collaborations and recordings

In 1982, Petrucciani has a successful tour of the West Coast of the United States with saxophonist Charles Lloyd. They won the Prix d'Excellence in 1982 and their performance at the Montreux Jazz Festival was recorded as an album. They also filmed One Night with Blue Note at Town Hall in New York City on 22 February 1985. He also recorded the trio album Power of Three in 1986 at the Montreux Jazz Festival with Wayne Shorter and Jim Hall. He recorded the album Michel Plays Petrucciani, which consists entirely of his own compositions, and worked with musicians such as Gary Peacock, Roy Haynes, Al Foster, Eddie Gómez, and John Abercrombie.
