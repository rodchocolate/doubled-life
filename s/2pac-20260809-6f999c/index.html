Tupac Amaru Shakur, known by stage names 2Pac and Makaveli, was an influential American rapper and actor. He was a prominent political activist for Black America and one of the most best-selling music artists of the 20th century, selling over 75 million records worldwide.

early life and education

Born in East Harlem, New York City, on June 16, 1971, to parents who were Black Panther Party members, Shakur was raised by his mother, Afeni Shakur. In 1984, the family moved to Baltimore, Maryland, where he attended Roland Park Middle School and Paul Laurence Dunbar High School before transferring to the Baltimore School for the Arts. There, he studied ballet, poetry, jazz, and acting, and befriended actress Jada Pinkett. He later moved to Marin City, California, in 1988.

musical career

Shakur's debut album 2Pacalypse Now (1991) established him as a central figure in West Coast hip-hop. He achieved further success with Strictly 4 My N.I.G.G.A.Z... (1993) and Me Against the World (1995). His fourth studio album, All Eyez on Me (1996), was the first double-full-length hip-hop solo studio album released for mass consumption globally and was certified Diamond by the RIAA in 2014. He also formed the group Thug Life and collaborated with artists such as Snoop Dogg and Dr. Dre.

الماضい career and death

During his later career, Shakur experienced legal troubles and served eight months in prison on sexual abuse charges before being released in 1995 after Suge Knight and Jimmy Iovine paid his $1.4 million bail. He signed to Death Row Records and became embroiled in the East Coast–West Coast hip-hop rivalry, including a feud with the Notorious B.I.G. On September 7, 1996, in Paradise, Nevada, Shakur was shot four times by an unidentified assailant in a drive-by shooting; he died six days later.

posthumous work and legacy

Following his death, several albums were released, including the acclaimed The Don Killuminati: The 7 Day Theory (1996) under the name Makaveli and the double-length posthumous album Greatest Hits (1998). The song "Changes" from Greatest Hits was nominated for Best Rap Solo Performance at the 2000 Grammy Awards. In 2002, he was inducted into the Hip-Hop Hall of Fame, and in 2017, he was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame.
