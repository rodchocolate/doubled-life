The Tennessee Three was the backing band for country and rockabilly singer Johnny Cash for nearly 25 years. They provided a unique sound that became recognized by fans as "the Johnny Cash sound".

formation and early history

The band was formed in Memphis, Tennessee, after Johnny Cash moved there in 1954. The original members were Marshall Grant (upright bass), A.W. "Red" Kernodle (six-string steel guitar), and Luther Perkins (Fender Esquire electric guitar). Luther Perkins' performance style on the Fender resulted in the same steady, simple "boom-chicka-boom" or "freight train" rhythm that became famous. In 1955, they auditioned for Sam Phillips at Sun Records; however, Kernodle left the group due to nervousness. Following this, Sam Phillips suggested they call themselves Johnny Cash and the Tennessee Two. They moved to Columbia Records in 1958.

evolution of personnel and sound

In 1960, drummer W.S. Holland joined the group, which was then renamed The Tennessee Three. W.S. Holland is credited as one of the first country music drummers. In 1968, Luther Perkins died from injuries sustained in a house fire. He was replaced by Bob Wootton, who participated in the landmark February 1969 performance at San Quentin State Prison. In 1971, the group recorded an instrumental album titled The Tennessee Three: The Sound Behind Johnny Cash dedicated to Luther Perkins. In 1980, Marshall Grant left the band to manage the Statler Brothers.
