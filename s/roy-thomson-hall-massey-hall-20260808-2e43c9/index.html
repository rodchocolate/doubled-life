Roy Thomson Hall and Massey Hall are two prominent concert halls in Toronto, Ontario, Canada. They are both operated by The Corporation of Massey Hall and Roy Thomson Hall, a non-profit charitable organization.

roy thomson hall

Opened in 1982, Roy Thomson Hall is located downtown in the city's entertainment district. It is home to the Toronto Symphony Orchestra and the Toronto Mendelssohn Choir, as well as the esports team Toronto Defiant. The hall features a circular architectural design with a sloping curvilinear glass exterior, designed by Canadian architects Arthur Erickson and Mathers and Haldenby. Itzhak Perlman served as a special advisor on accessibility for disabled guests and performers. The venue seats 2,630 guests and contains a pipe organ built by Gabriel Kney. Its construction phase was previously known as 'The New Massey Hall' before it was officially named in January 1982 after a C$4.5 million donation from the family of Roy Thomson. Following musician complaints about acoustics, the hall underwent a six-month renovation in 2002. The building's design was influenced by Erickson's relationship with North American Aboriginals and his journeys in Japan. Arthur Erickson described the grey structure as a container for people to fill with their own decorations. It also hosted the state funeral of Jack Layton on August 27, 2011.

history of massey hall

Massey Hall is a performing arts auditorium in Toronto's Garden District, opened in 1894. It was a gift to the people of Toronto from industrialist Hart Massey, who wanted a secular meeting place for choral music that would be accessible to both rich and poor. The building was designed by architect Sidney Badgley with a neoclassical facade and Moorish Revival interior architecture inspired by the Alhambra Palace in Spain and Louis Sullivan's Chicago Auditorium. It features thirty stained glass windows depicting famous composers, created by the Faircloth Brothers glass firm. Completed at a cost of CA$152,390.75, its debut concert was a choral performance of and Handel's 'Messiah' on June 14, 1894. After the 1904 Toronto Great Fire, fire escape staircases were added to the front face of the building. In 1933, the Massey Foundation undertook renovations that simplified the same name to Massey Hall and reduced its capacity from 3,500 to 2,765 patrons. In 1994, for its 100th anniversary, a bar called Centuries is now located in the basement, which has a capacity of 220 people. A major renovation began in 2013, and phase two ('Massey Hall Forever') began in July 2018, which included an expanded stage, a new seven-story addition called the Allied Music Centre, and a new retractable seating system. The project was completed in 2022, with re-opening delayed by the COVID-19 pandemic until 2021.

capacity and designations

Massey Hall was designated as a Heritage Property under the Ontario Heritage Act in 1973 and a National Historic Site of Canada on June 15, 1981. Its current main hall capacity is 2,534 seated and 2,800 standing. The new TD Music Hall within the Allied Music Centre addition has a capacity of 245 seated and 450 standing. The venue is known for its outstanding acoustics and was the long-time hall of the Toronto Symphony Orchestra and the Toronto Symphony Orchestra and the Toronto Mendelssohn Choir until 1984.

notable performers

Massey Hall has hosted a wide variety of performers, including operatic singers such as Maria Callas, Luciano Pavarotti, Enrico Caruso, and Montserrat Caballe, and classical musicians like George Gershwin, Glenn Gould, Vladimir Horowitz, and Arturo Toscanini. It has also been used for public memorials, speaking tours, and boxing matches.
