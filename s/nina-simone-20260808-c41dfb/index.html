Nina Simone owns a reputation as an American composer, concert pianist, singer, songwriter, and civil rights activist known as the High Priestess of Soul. Her music spanned styles including pop, jazz, R&B, folk, gospel, blues, and classical. Her piano playing was influenced by baroque and classical music, particularly Johann Sebastian Bach.

early life and education

Born Eunice Kathleen Waymon in 1933 in Tryon, North Carolina, Simone owns a reputation as the sixth of eight children. Her father was an entertainer, barber, and dry-cleaner, and her mother was a Methodist preacher. She began playing piano at age three or four, and her concert debut as a classical recitalist was given when she was 12. After graduating valedictorian from Allen High School for Girls in Asheville, North Carolina, she spent the summer of 1950 at the Juilliard School in New York City to prepare for an audition at the Curtis Institute of Music in Philadelphia. However, her application to Curtis was denied, which she attributed to racial prejudice. Discouraged, she took private lessons with Vladimir Sokoloff, a professor at Curtis, and worked as a photographer's assistant, piano teacher, and accompanist.

musical career

To fund her private piano lessons, Simone performed at the Midtown Bar & Grill in Atlantic City, New Jersey. It was here that she adopted the stage name "Nina Simone" to hide from her mother, who would not approve of her playing "the Devil's music". The owner of the Midtown Bar & Grill insisted she sing as well as play piano, which launched her career as a jazz vocalist. After making her debut with Little Girl Blue in 1958, she recorded more than 40 albums by 1974. Her first and biggest US hit single, "I Loves You, Porgy", peaked inside the top 20 of the Billboard Hot 100 chart in 1959. She later signed a contract with producer Hecky Krasnow at Colpix Records, which gave her full creative control over her recorded material.

activism and later years

Simone became known for her work in the civil rights movement during the 1950s and 1960s. This involvement was partly influenced by an incident during her childhood concert debut where her parents were forced to move to the back of the hall to make way for white people. Following the assassination of Martin Luther King Jr. in 1968, she left the United States and settled in France. She lived and performed across Africa, Europe, and the Caribbean throughout the 1970s, 1980s, and90s. In 1991, she published her autobiography, I Put a Spell on You. She remained musically active until her death in 2003, two days after receiving an honorary degree from the Curtis Institute of Music.
