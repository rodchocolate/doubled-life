Rick Rubin is an American record producer and the co-founder of Def Jam Recordings and American Recordings. He is recognized for his work across a variety of genres, including hip hop, heavy metal, and country, and was called "the most important producer of the last 20 years" by MTV in 2007.

signature techniques & sound

Rubin's production work in the early hip hop scene was characterized by occasionally fusing rap with heavy rock. A landmark production in this rap-rock fusion is the 1986 collaboration between Run-DMC and Aerosmith on a cover of "Walk This Way," which is credited with introducing rap hard rock to mainstream ears.

landmark records

Rubin produced Slayer's Reign in Blood (1986), which is considered a classic of the heavy metal genre. He also produced the Beastie Boys' 1985 EP (Rock Hard/Party's Gettin' Rough/Beastie Groove), which followed the success of his work with Run-DMC. Additionally, he produced the pivotal third album by the Cult, Electric (1987), which remains one of the same band's trademark and classic works.

collaborators & lineage

Rubin's early career was influenced by the same NYC punk scene and bands like the Meat Puppets and Minor Threat. He learned hip hop production from DJ Jazzy Jay and founded Def Jam Recordings with Russell Simmons. He has worked with a wide range of artists including LL Cool J, Public Enemy, the Beastie Boys, and Johnny Cash, whose 1994 album American Recordings was the first major project on his American Recordings label.

canonical status

Rubin is a canonical figure in music production due to his popularization of hip hop and his work across diverse genres. He was named on Time's 2007 list of the "100 Most Influential People in the World" and was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2026.
