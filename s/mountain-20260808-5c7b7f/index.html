A mountain is an elevated portion of a planet's surface, typically characterized by steep sides and significant exposed bedrock. While there are no universally accepted definitions, they are generally considered to be higher than hills.

definition and classification

Defining a mountain is complex as criteria such as elevation, relief, steepness, and volume have been used. In the United Kingdom and Republic of Ireland, mountains are usually defined as summits at least 2,000 feet (610 m) high. The UN Environmental Programme classifies mountainous environments into seven classes based on elevation and slope, noting that such environments cover 24% of Earth's land mass. In the US, the United States Geological Survey concludes that terms like 'mountain' and 'hill' do not have technical definitions.

geology and formation

Mountains are formed through volcanism, erosion, or tectonic forces acting over tens of millions of years. There are three main types: volcanic, fold, and block mountains. Volcanic mountains form when magma reaches the surface or solidifies below ground to create dome mountains. Fold mountains occur during plate collisions where the crust is overthickened and rock folds into anticlines (upfolds) and synclines (downfolds). Block mountains are caused by faults in the crust where uplifted blocks, known as horsts, rise relative to others; intervening dropped blocks are called graben.

climate and human use

High elevations produce colder climates than at sea level at similar latitudes, which strongly affects mountain ecosystems by creating different plants and animals at different elevations. Due to the less hospitable terrain and climate, mountains are typically used more for resource extraction—such as logging and mining—and recreation, such as skiing and mountain climbing, rather than agriculture.

notable examples

The highest mountain on Earth is Mount Everest in Asia's Himalayas, reaching 8,850 m (29,035 ft) above mean sea level. The tallest mountain from base to peak on Earth is Mauna Kea in Hawaii, which rises 9,330 m (30,610 ft) from the ocean floor. In the Solar System, Olympus Mons on Mars is the highest known mountain at 21,171 m (69,459 ft).
