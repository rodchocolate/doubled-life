John Coltrane was an American jazz saxophonist, bandleader, and composer who is considered one of the most influential figures in 20th-century music and jazz history. Born in North Carolina and raised in High Point, he later moved to Philadelphia to study music and serve in the Navy during World War II.

early life and military service

Coltrane was born on September 23, 1926, in Hamlet, North Carolina. He played clarinet and alto horn in a community band during high school before switching to the saxophone. After graduating from high school, he moved to Philadelphia in 1943, where he took saxophone lessons with Mike Guerra at the Ornstein School of Music. In August 1945, Coltrane enlisted in the Navy to avoid the Army draft, training as an apprentice seaman at Sampson Naval Training Station before being stationed at Pearl Harbor. While there, he joined the Melody Masters, an all-white base swing band, where he served as a guest performer to avoid alerting superior officers. He was discharged from the Navy on August 8, 1946, and received the American Campaign Medal, Asiatic–Pacific Campaign Medal, and the World War II Victory Medal.

professional career and musical development

After returning to Philadelphia in the Navy's G.I. Bill, used Coltrane to enroll at the Granoff School of Music to study music theory with Dennis Sandole. He also took lessons with Matthew Rastelli for several years. After touring with King Kolax, he joined a band led by Jimmy Heath. In 1947, he switched from alto to tenor saxophone. He was heavily influenced by by the Philadelphia pianist Hasaan Ibn Ali and musicians like Coleman Hawkins, Ben Webster, and Tab Smith. Later, he helped pioneer the use of modes and was at the forefront of free jazz. Over time, his music took on a spiritual dimension, as seen in his most acclaimed album A Love Supreme (1965).

titles: recording history and labels

Coltrane's first recordings were informal sessions in Hawaii with Navy musicians on July 13, 1946. He later recorded with Billy Valentine and sat in on sessions with Dizzy Gillespie, Johnny Hodges, and Earl Bostic. In 1955, he joined the Miles Davis Quintet (the "First Great Quintet"), appearing on recordings such as 'Round About Midnight and Milestones. He briefly worked with Thelonious Monk in May 1957. In April 1957, he signed with Prestige Records, where he made his debut album Coltrane (1957) and Blue Train (1958) for Blue Note. After a two-year contract with Atlantic Records starting in April 1959, which produced albums like Giant Steps and My Favorite Things, he became the first artist to sign with Impulse! Records in April 1961, recording until his death in July 1967.
