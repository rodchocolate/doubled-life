John Coltrane (1926–1967) was an American jazz saxophonist, bandleader, and composer who is considered one of the most influential figures in 20th-century music. Born in North Carolina and later moving to Philadelphia, he pioneered the use of modes and was at the forefront of free jazz.

early life and military service

Coltrane was born on September 23, 1926, in Hamlet, North Carolina, and grew up in High Point. He played clarinet and alto horn in a community band before switching to the saxophone. After graduating high school, he moved to Philadelphia in 1943 where he took saxophone lessons at the Ornstein School of Music. In 1945, to avoid the Army draft, he enlisted in the Navy, where he was stationed at Pearl Harbor and played with the Melody Masters swing band. He was discharged from the Navy on August 8, 1946, and received several medals including the World War II Victory Medal.

musical career and development

After his discharge, Coltrane studied music theory with Dennis Sandole at the Granoff School of Music. He switched from alto to tenor saxophone in 1947. Influenced by by musicians such as Charlie Parker, Coleman Hawkins, Ben Webster, and Tab Smith, he became fanatical about practicing. In the early to mid-1950s, he was a member of groups led by Dizzy Gillespie, Earl Bostic, and Johnny Hodges. By 1955, he joined the 'First Great Quintet' of Miles Davis, with whom he played from October 1955 to April 1957.

recording career and labels

Coltrane signed a contract with Prestige Records in April 1957, releasing his debut album Coltrane. He also recorded Blue Train for Blue Note Records in 1958. In April 1959, he signed with Atlantic Records, producing albums such as Giant Steps and My Favorite Things. In April 1961, he became the first artist to sign with Impulse! Records, where he recorded A Love Supreme (1965) and Ascension. He also appeared on several recordings led by Miles Davis and Thelonious Monk.

spiritual dimension and legacy

C و a career, Coltrane's music took on an increasingly spiritual dimension, as seen in A Love Supreme. He was canonized by the African Orthodox Church and posthumously awarded a special Pulitzer Prize. In San Francisco, the St. John Will-I-Am Coltrane African Orthodox Church was founded in 1969 by Franzo and Marina King, who initially viewed him as an incarnation of the Christian god and his album A Love Supreme as a sacred text.
