Quincy Jones was an American record producer, composer, arranger, conductor, and trumpeter with a career spanning seven decades. He is recognized as one of the few producers to have achieved number one records across three consecutive decades, from the 1960s through the 1980s.

signature sound and craft

Jones's craft as a producer and arranger began in the 1950s as a jazz arranger and conductor. His versatility across genres allowed him to produce pop hit records for artists like Lesley Gore in the early 1960s and serve as an arranger and conductor for collaborations between Frank Sinatra and Count Basie. His work as a film composer, composing scores for films such as The Pawnbroker, In the Heat of the Night, In Cold Blood, The Italian Job, and The Color Purple, further expanded his technical range.

landmark records

Jones produced three of the most successful albums by Michael Jackson: Off the Wall (1979), Thriller (1982), and Bad (1987). He also produced and conducted the charity song "We Are the World" (1985), which was created to raise funds for victims of famine in Ethiopia.

collaborators and lineage

Jones's early professional lineage includes his time as a touring member of the Lionel Hampton orchestra in 1953. He was influenced by musicians such as Bunny Campbell and Preston Sandiford. Early in his career, he developed his skills as a trumpeter and arranger in Seattle, playing with Charles Taylor's group, backing Billie Holiday and Billy Eckstine, and meeting Ray Charles.

canonical status

Jones is considered canonical due to his massive commercial success and critical acclaim, receiving 28 Grammy Awards and nominations for seven Academy Awards. He was named one of the most influential jazz musicians of the 20th century by Time magazine and received the same honorary awards such as the Grammy Legend Award, the the National Medal of the Arts, and the recent posthumous Academy Honorary Award in 2025.
