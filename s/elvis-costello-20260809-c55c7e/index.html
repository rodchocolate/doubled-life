Elvis Costello is an English singer, songwriter, record producer, author, and television host born Declan Patrick MacManus on 25 August 1954. He has been described as reinvigorating the literate, lyrical traditions of Bob Dylan and Van Morrison with punk's raw energy and sass.

early life and family background

Costello was born in Paddington, west London, to a musical family. His father, Ross MacManus, was a professional trumpet player and singer who performed in bebop and modern jazz bands as well as the Joe Loss Orchestra. His grandfather, Pat Matthew McManus, was also a professional musician who played trumpet for army bandsmen, ship's musicians for the White Star Line, and orchestra musicians in music halls and theatres showing silent films. Costello has stated that his grandfather's career is the reason he became a musician. He was raised Roman Catholic and served as an altar boy until age 14, spending most of his childhood in Twickenham before moving to Liverpool in 1970.

musical career and style

Costello's professional career began during the rise of punk rock in England. While he initially disguised his musical knowledge due to the primitivism of punk, his stylistic range eventually expanded to include jazz, country, R&B, baroque pop, classical music and Tin Pan Alley. His debut album, My Aim Is True (1977), featured the ballad 'Alison'. His subsequent albums This Year's Model (1978) and Armed Forces (1979) helped define the new wave genre. Between late 1977 and early 1980, all of his singles reached the UK Top 30, including his biggest hit 'Oliver's Army' (1979). In the US, he earned critical praise and high rankings in The Village Voice's Pazz & Jop critics' poll, though commercial success was more modest until the album Spike (1989) and its single 'Veronica', which reached No. 19 on the Billboard Hot 100.

collaborations and other work

Costello's early career was primarily spent performing with his backing band, the Attractions. His current backing band is known as the Imposters. He has collaborated with artists such as Paul McCartney, Burt Bacharach, the hip-hop group the Roots, the songwriter and producer Allen Toussaint, and the classical ensemble the Brodsky Quartet. Costello has also recorded cover versions of songs by Nick Lowe's group Brinsley Schwarz, Sam & Dave, Jerry Chesnut, and Charles Aznavour. His own songs have been recorded by artists including Johnny Cash, Roy Orbison, George Jones, Linda Ronstadt and Alison Krauss. Those outside of music, he was a television host from 2008 to 2010 for the show Spectacle: Elvis Costello with..., and published a memoir, Unfaithful Music & Disappearing Ink, in 2015.

the album spike

Released in 1989 by Warner Bros. Records, Spike is the 12th studio album by Costello. It was his first release since My Aim Is True with no appearance by the Attractions. Produced by T-Bone Burnett, the album was recorded across four locations: London, Dublin, New Orleans and Hollywood. The album includes 'Veronica', a song co-written with Paul McCartney, and 'Tramp the Dirt Down', a protest song targeting the Thatcher government. The album cover artwork, designed by Jeri and John Heiden, depicts an entertainer in captivity.
