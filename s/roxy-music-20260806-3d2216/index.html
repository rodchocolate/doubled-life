Roxy Music were an English rock band formed in 1970 by Bryan Ferry and Graham Simpson. The group pioneered sophisticated elements of glam rock and influenced early English punk music and new wave acts while innovating electronic composition.

musical style and influence

Roxy Music became a successful act in Europe and Australia during the 1970s. The group focused on glamorous fashions and visual sophistication, emphasizing a brand of musical sophistication. Andy Mackay later noted that the band proved rock 'n' roll could accommodate anything.

Their debut studio album, Roxy Music, released June 16, 1972, by Island Records, reached number 10 in the UK Albums Chart. The opening track 'Re-Make/Re-Model' was described as a postmodernist pastiche featuring solos echoing Western music touchstones like Wagner's 'Ride of the Valkyries' and The Beatles' 'Day Tripper'. Some songs on the album were thematically linked to films, such as '2HB', a tribute to Humphrey Bogart in Casablanca, and 'Chance Meeting', inspired by David Lean's Brief Encounter.

career trajectory and legacy

The band split in 1976, reformed in 1978, and split again in 1983. Their final studio album, Avalon (1982), was certified Platinum in the United States. In 2019, Roxy Music were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame.

Outside the band, Bryan Ferry and Brian Eno have had influential solo careers; Eno became one of the most significant British record producers of the late 20th century. In 2001, Ferry, Mackay, Manzanera, and Thompson reunited for a concert tour, and they have toured intermittently since, including a 2022 tour to celebrate the 50th anniversary of their first album.
