10cc are an English art rock band from Manchester formed in July 1972. The group originally featured keyboardist/guitarist Eric Stewart, bassist/guitarist Graham Gouldman, keyboardist/guitarist Lol Creme, and drummer Kevin Godley.

history

The band released their self-titled debut album in 1973 and toured with second drummer Paul Burgess. Between 1972 and 1978, they achieved five consecutive UK top-ten albums: Sheet Music (1974), The Original Soundtrack (1975), How Dare You! (1976), Deceptive Bends (1977), and Bloody Tourists (1978). After Godley and Creme left in November 1976 to focus on electronic music device 'The Gizmo' and video production, the remaining trio recorded Deceptive Bends. Later additions to the lineup including Rick Fenn, Tony O'Malley, Duncan Mackay, Vic Emerson, and Stuart Tosh were added. The group disbanded by the end of 1982. Stewart and Gouldman reunited with Godley and Creme in 1991 to record ...Meanwhile, but the group disbanded again after touring in 1995. Since 1999, Graham Gouldman has led a touring version of the band featuring members such as Rick Fenn, Paul Burgess, Mick Wilson, Keith Hayman, Iain Hornal and Ben Stone.

musical style and recording

The original four members were multi-instrumentalists, singers, writers and producers who worked together in various permutations. Godley and Creme's songwriting was inspired by art and cinema. Most of the records were recorded at their own Strawberry Studios in Stockport and Dorking, with the majority engineered by Eric Stewart. The band had twelve singles reach the UK Top 40, including chart-toppers 'Rubber Bullets' (1973), 'I'm Not in Love' (1975), and 'Dreadlock Holiday' (1978). 'I'm Not in Love' is noted for its innovative backing track.

early days

Before forming 10cc, the members had collaborated in various groups. Lol Creme and Kevin Godley were childhood friends from Manchester; Graham Gouldman had formed The Whirlwinds (later The Mockingbirds) with Godley. Eric Stewart was a member of Wayne Fontana and the Mindbenders. In 1968, Stewart and Peter Tattersall founded Strawberry Studios in Stockport. By 1969, all four future members were working together at the studio. They also produced formulaic bubblegum pop songs for American producers Jerry Kasenetz and Jeffry Katz of Super K Productions, including 'Sausalito' (a No. 86 US hit credited to Ohio Express).
