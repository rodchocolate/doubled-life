Uma is a term used across various contexts including religion, nature, arts and entertainment, and geography. It also appears as an acronym for several scientific, technological, and organizational entities under the UMA abbreviation.

religion and nature

In Hindu religion, Uma is a goddess known as Parvati or Gauri. In nature, Uma refers to the genus of fringe-toed lizards. In terms of geography, there are places named Uma, including a village in North Macedonia (also known as Huma), a district in Ehime, Japan, and Río Uma, a tributary of the uma river in Spain.

arts and entertainment

The term is associated with several films, such as a 1941 Japanese film known as Horse, a 2013 Nepali film by Tsering Rhitar Sherpa, and a 2018 Indian Bengali-language film. It also appears in media franchises like The Witcher 3: Wild Hunt, Descendants, and the children's TV series Oobi. In manga, Miss Uma is a character from Crayon Shin-chan, and in Hawaii, there is a wrestling game called Uma.

science and technology

UMA can stand for various technical terms such as Unified memory architecture (a synonym of integrated graphics), Uniform memory access (a shared memory architecture in parallel computers), User-Managed Access, and Unlicensed Mobile Access. In computer memory management, it refers to the Upper memory area in DOS. In astronomy, UMa is the abbreviation for Ursa Major, a constellation in the Northern Sky.

titles

UMA stands for several organizations including the University of Maine at Augusta, University of Málaga, University of Mannheim, University of Madeira, and University of Mohaghegh Ardabili. It also refers to the political and economic union known as the Arab Maghreb Union (Union du Maghreb Union), and the Argentine Mathematical Union (Unión Matemática Argentina).

ursa major

Ursa Major, abbreviated as UMa, is the third largest of the 88 modern constellations. It is primarily known for the asterism of its main seven stars called the 'Big Dipper' (or 'the Plough' in the UK). The stars Merak and Dubhe are used as pointer stars to find Polaris, the North Star. The constellation covers 1279.66 square degrees of the sky and borders eight other constellations.
