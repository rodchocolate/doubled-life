Virgin Records is a British record label owned by Universal Music Group. It was founded in 1972 as an independent label by entrepreneurs Richard Branson, Simon Draper, Nik Powell, and musician Tom Newman.

history and branding

The label's origins began with a record shop called Virgin Records and Tapes on Notting Hill Gate in London, which specialized in krautrock imports. The name 'Virgin' was suggested by Tessa Watts during a brainstorming session because the founders were new to business. The first release on the label was Mike Oldfield's progressive rock album Tubular Bells in 1973, produced by Tom Newman. This was followed by notable krautrock releases from Tangerine Dream and Faust. The label's original logo, designed by Roger Dean, featured a naked woman in mirror image with a lizard. A red, white, and blue design was introduced in 1975, and the current 'scrawl' logo—a stylized signature created in 1978—was based on a scribble by designer Ray Kyte on a napkin.

evolution and rebranding

In 1977, the signing of the Sex Pistols reinvented the label as a new-wave outpost, moving it into the mainstream of the punk rock era. Under Tessa Watts' guidance, the Pistols brought the label to success. Virgin subsequently signed other new wave groups such as Public Image Ltd, Culture Club, The Human League, Simple Minds, and XTC. The Human League's "Don't You Want Me" became the label's first chart-topping single in 1981.

corporate transitions

Virgin Records was sold by Richard Branson to Thorn EMI in June 1992 for a reported US$1 billion, which Branson used to fund Virgin Atlantic. Following this acquisition, Virgin launched several subsidiaries including Realworld Records, Innocent Records, Pointblank Records, and Hut Recordings. After EMI was acquired by Universal Music Group (UMG) in 2012, UMG created the Virgin EMI Records division. In 2013, Virgin EMI Records was formed through the merger of Mercury Records UK and Virgin Records. In June 2020, Virgin EMI became dormant and was replaced by the relaunch of labels EMI Records and Virgin Records, with Virgin Records operating as an imprint of EMI Records.

north american and classical music operations

Virgin Records America, Inc. was founded in 1986 to handle North American operations. It was headquartered in Beverly Hills from 1986 to 2002, then moved to New York City (2003-2012), and finally to Hollywood, California, under the Capitol Music Group imprint since 2012. Virgin Classics was founded in 1988 as part of Virgin Records. Due to European Commission requirements for divestment during UMG's takeover of EMI, Virgin Classics was sold to Warner Music Group in February 2013 and its roster and catalogue were placed into Erato Records.
