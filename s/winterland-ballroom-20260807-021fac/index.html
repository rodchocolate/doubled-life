Winterland Ballroom was an ice-skating rink and music venue located in San Francisco that operated from 1928 to 1979. It was built at the corner of Post Street and Steiner Street and was demolished in 1986.

history and development

The venue opened on June 29, 1928, as New Dreamland Auditorium, a skating rink that could be converted into a seated entertainment venue. The building cost $1 million, which is equivalent to $18.8 million in 2025. It was built on the site of the Dreamland Rink and Sid Grauman's National Theatre. In the late 1930s, the name was changed to Winterland. The venue successfully operated through the Great Depression and hosted opera, boxing, tennis matches, and the Shipstads and Johnson Ice Follies starting in 1936. In November 1944, impresario Clifford C. Fischer staged an authorized production of the Folies Bergère.

music venue operations

Beginning on September 23, 1966, concert promoter Bill Graham began renting the venue for larger concerts that his nearby Fillmore Auditorium could not accommodate. After closing the Fillmore West in 1971, Graham converted the building for exclusive use as a music venue and took over operations. His related merchandising company, Winterland Productions, sold memorabilia, concert shirts, and sports-team merchandise. The venue had an audience capacity of 5,400.

notable performances and recordings

Numerous popular rock acts played at Winterland, including the Rolling Stones, Jimi Hendrix, Pink Floyd, Queen, and the Grateful Dead. The Grateful Dead made the venue their home base, playing 73 times between March 3, 1967, and December 31, 1978. Peter Frampton recorded parts of his live album Frampton Comes Alive! there. The Band's final show on Thanksgiving Day 1976 was filmed by Martin Scorsese as The Last Waltz. The Sex Pistols played their final show there on January 14, 1978. The Jimi Hendrix Experience recorded six performances between October 10 and 12, 1968, which were posthumously released in the box set Winterland.

closure and demolition

During its final month of existence, shows were booked nearly every night. The venue closed on New Year's Eve 1978 / New Year's Day 1979 with a concert featuring the Grateful Dead, New Riders of the Purple Sage, and the Blues Brothers. This final show lasted over eight hours, and the Grateful Dead's performance began at midnight with "Sugar Magnolia". The building was eventually razed in 1985 and replaced by apartments.
