The Allman Brothers Band was an American rock band formed in Jacksonville, Florida, in 1969. The group incorporated elements of blues, jazz, and country music, with live shows featuring instrumentals and jam band-style improvisation. They were based in Macon, Georgia, and founded by brothers Duane and Gregg Allman.

evolution and personnel changes

Following the motorcycle accident death of group leader Duane Allman in 1971 and bassist Berry Oakley in 1972, the band recruited keyboardist Chuck Leavell and bassist Lamar Williams for their 1973 album Brothers and Sisters. This album featured the hit single "Ramblin' Man" and the instrumental "Jessica", which became classic rock radio staples. The group dissolved in 1976, reformed briefly in 1978, and broke up again in 1982. They re-formed once more in 1989, continuing to tour and heavy touring until they retired in October 2014 after a final show at the Beacon Theatre.

musical style and recognition

The band's music incorporated elements of Southern rock, blues, jazz, and country music. They were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1995 and awarded seven Gold and four Platinum albums by the RIAA. Rolling Stone magazine ranked them 52nd on its list of the All Time Greatest Artists in 2004 (or 2010), and they became renowned for their month-long concert residencies at New York City's Beacon Theatre each spring.
