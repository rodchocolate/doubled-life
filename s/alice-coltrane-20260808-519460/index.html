Alice Coltrane (1937–2007), also known as Swamini Turiyasangitananda or Turiya, was an American jazz musician, composer, bandleader, and Hindu spiritual leader. An accomplished pianist and one of the few harpists in jazz history, she was a foremost proponent of spiritual jazz. Her eclectic music influenced both the world of jazz and beyond.

early life and career

Born Alice Lucille McLeod in Detroit, Michigan, Coltrane grew up in a musical household with a mother who was a church choir member and siblings who became a jazz bassist and songwriter. With her father's encouragement, she performed in clubs around Detroit before moving to Paris in the late 1950s to study classical music and jazz with Bud Powell. In 1960, she worked as an intermission pianist at the Blue Note Jazz Club and appeared on French television. After a brief marriage to Kenny "Pancho" Hagood, she returned to Detroit where she played professionally as a duo with Terry Pollard and in Terry Gibbs' quartet, where she met John Coltrane. She married John Coltrane in 1965 in Ciudad Juárez, Mexico.

musical evolution and solo work

Coltrane replaced McCoy Tyner as pianist in John Coltrane's group in January 1966 and performed with him until his death in July 1967. Following his passing, she began releasing records as a composer and bandleader, starting with her first album A Monastic Trio in 1967. Between 1968 and 1977, she released thirteen full-length records, moving from standard jazz toward a more cosmic, spiritual direction with orchestral approaches and harp glissandos. She recorded for Impulse! Records until 1973 and primarily for Warner Bros. Records from 1973 to 1978.

spiritual leadership and ashram years

In the early 1970s, Coltrane studied Hinduism under Yoga guru Swami Satchidananda. By 1972, she moved to California to establish the Vedantic Center in 1975 and later the Shanti Anantam Ashram in 1983 near Malibu, where she served as spiritual director. She adopted the monastic name Turiyasangitananda. During this period, she recorded Hindu devotional songs, incorporating synthesizers and aspects of Gospel music into her compositions. Her work was influenced by the ISKCON communities and the Sathya Sai Baba movement on the West Coast, leading to the release of Radha-Krsna Nama Sankirtana in 1977.

later career and final works

Coltrane's public musical career slowed in the mid-1970s as she dedicated herself to religious education. Her final jazz-oriented album, Transfiguration, was recorded in 1978 and released by Warner Bros. In the2000s, she returned to spiritual jazz with her final album, Translinear Light, released in 2004. Posthumously, a compilation album titled The Ecstatic Music of Alice Coltrane Turiyasangitananda was released in 2017 by Luaka Bop, drawing from recordings made for members of the Sai Anantam Ashram.
