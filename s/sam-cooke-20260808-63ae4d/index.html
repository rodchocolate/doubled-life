Sam Cooke, known as the "King of Soul," was an influential American soul singer and songwriter. Over his thirteen-year career, he achieved significant commercial success with 29 singles charting in the Billboard Hot 100 Top 40 and 20 singles in the Top 10 of Billboard's Black Singles chart.

early life and gospel career

Born Samuel Cook in Clarksdale, Mississippi, in 1931, Cooke was raised Baptist and sang in his father's church choir. He first gained prominence as the lead singer of the Highway Q.C.'s as a teenager. In 1950, he joined The Soul Stirrers, replacing R. H. Harris. Under his leadership, they recorded songs such as "Jesus Gave Me Water," "Peace in the Valley," and "One More River." Cooke is credited with bringing gospel music to a younger audience, particularly young women. He left the Soul Stirrers in 1956 to pursue a solo career in secular music, which caused a backlash from religious leaders.

solo career and mainstream success

Cooke's transition to pop music began with his first secular song, "Lovable," recorded under the alias Dale Cook. After leaving Specialty Records due to disagreements with Art Rupe over musical direction, he signed with Keen Records in 1957. His first single, "You Send Me," topped the Billboard R&B Best Sellers and Jockeys charts for six weeks and also reached number one on the Billboard Top 100. This success elevated his earnings from $200 to over $5,000 a week. He later signed a lucrative deal with RCA Victor in 1960, after which Keen Records released "Wonderful World" in competition. "Wonderful World," recorded during an impromptu session in 1959, was revised by Cooke to focus more on education, and became one of his biggest hits, reaching number 12 on the Billboard Hot 100.

death and legacy

In 1964, Sam Cooke was shot and killed by a motel manager in Los Angeles. While courts ruled the death a justifiable homicide, his family has since questioned the circumstances. In 2015, Billboard magazine ranked him number 28 on its list of the "35 Greatest R&B Artists of All Time."

wonderful world

The song "Wonderful World" was composed by Lou Adler and Herb Alpert, but revised by Cooke. The recording session for the song featured guitarist Cliff White, bassist Adolphus Alsbrook, and drummer Ronnie Selico. Personnel may have included a quartet of singers including Lou Rawls and George McCurn. The song has since been featured in films such as Animal House (1978) and Hitch (2005), and gained renewed popularity in the 1986 UK release after being featured in a Levi's 501 commercial.

family

Cooke married Barbara Campbell, who is listed on the record labels for hits like "Only Sixteen" and "Caba Cha Cha Cha." He and Barbara had a daughter, Linda Womack (née Cooke), born in 1953. Their same-year marriage occurred in 1958.
