The Toyota 4Runner is an SUV manufactured by the Japanese automaker Toyota and marketed globally since 1984 across six generations. Originally a compact SUV based on the Toyota Hilux pickup truck, it has since evolved into a cross between a compact and mid-size SUV. All 4Runners are built in Japan at the Hino Motors plant in Hamura or Toyota's plant in Tahara, Aichi.

naming and market availability

The name "4Runner" was created by copywriter Robert Nathan of the Saatchi & Saatchi advertising company as a play on the term "forerunner". According to Toyota, the "4" represents the vehicle's 4-wheel drive system and "Runner" refers to its all-terrain capabilities and off-road performance. As of 2021, the 4Runner is sold primarily in North and South America. In markets such as Asia, Europe, and the Middle East, the Land Cruiser Prado is often provided instead. For some markets, the 4Runner was branded as the Hilux Surf, which was later replaced by the Fortuner, a lower-cost alternative based on the the Hilux platform.

development and history

The 4Runner's predecessors were third-party conversions of the Toyota Hilux truck, such as the Toyota Trekker, which was built by Winnebago Industries from 1981 to 1983. The Trekker involved removing the rear cab wall and bed of the donor truck and replacing them with a fiberglass tub and non-removable canopy. The first generation 4Runner (N60) introduced in 1983 was a modification of the existing Hilux short-bed pickup body. It featured a removable fiberglass canopy, rear seats, and a roll bar, and was nearly mechanically identical to the Hilux 4x4. Like the Ford Bronco and Chevrolet K5 Blazer, the 4Runner's design concept was borrowed from these vehicles, which were also based on pickup trucks with removable shells over the rear sections.

findings and reliability

In a 2019 study by iSeeCars.com ranking the longest-lasting vehicles in the US, the 4Runner placed number five. The study found that 3.94% of 4Runners had a mileage of over 200,000 miles (320,000 km).
