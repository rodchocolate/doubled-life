Impressions is an album by American jazz saxophonist and composer John Coltrane, released through Impulse! Records in July 1963. It consists of both live and studio recordings.

recording and personnel

The album's studio tracks were recorded at Van Gelder Studio on September 18, 1962, and April 29, 1963. The live tracks were recorded in November 1961 at the Village Vanguard in Manhattan's Greenwich Village. Personnel for the studio recordings featured the classic Coltrane quartet consisting of McCoy Tyner (piano), Jimmy Garrison (double bass), and Elvin Jones (drums). On the live tracks, they were joined by multireedist Eric Dolphy and bassist Reggie Workman. In some instances, the lapped-up bonus track "Dear Old Stockholm" and the track "After the Rain" featured drummer Roy Haynes instead of Elvin Jones.

reception and influence

DownBeat magazine critic Harvey Pekar gave the album a five-star review in August 1963, stating that while not all music on the album is excellent, some is "more than excellent." In 2000, it was ranked number 687 in Colin Larkin's All Time Top 1000 Albums. The album had a direct influence on popular music; Roger McGuinn of the Byrds, according to which, while touring in late 1965, the band had a tape recording of Impressions and Africa/Brass alongside a Ravi Shankar recording. This led to the 1966 single "Eight Miles High" as a direct homage to Coltrane and specifically to the track "India" on Impressions.

facts

  • Release Date: July 1963
  • Recording Locations: Village Vanguard and Van Gelder Studio
