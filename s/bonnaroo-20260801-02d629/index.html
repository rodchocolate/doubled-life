Bonnaroo, also known as the Bonnaroo Music and Arts Festival, is an annual four-day American music festival. It was founded in 2002 by Superfly Presents, AC Entertainment, and individuals including Coran Capshaw, Chip Hooper, and Ashley Capps.

history and origins

Bonnaroo was developed by Ashley Capps of AC Entertainment following the permanent suspension of the Hot Summer Nights rock music festival in Knoxville, Tennessee. The festival's name is derived from Creole slang for a really good time and was inspired by the 1974 Dr. John album Desitively Bonnaroo. In 1999, the future site of the festival hosted the Itchycoo Park Festival, which is considered its spiritual predecessor. Following a record-breaking sellout in 2019, Live Nation bought out Superfly's share of the festival. Ownership and scheduling changes have occurred over time, with events in 2020, 2021, and 2025 were cancelled due to COVID-19, health concerns, and severe weather.

venue and programming

The festival takes place at Great Stage Park, a farm in Manchester, Tennessee. The venue is divided into Centeroo, which contains stages such as the What Stage, Which Stage, That Tent, This Tent, and The Other Stage, and Outeroo, the campground area where stages like the Where in the Woods Stage have been present since 2018. The What Stage can accommodate up to 80,000 fans. Programming includes stylistically diverse music ranging from indie rock, hip hop, jazz, Americana, bluegrass, country, folk, gospel, reggae, pop, and electronic music, as well as artistic, environmental, and community activities such as yoga, parades, a marriage barn, and sustainability lessons.

economic and environmental impact

In 2023, Bonnaroo contributed $339.8 million to the regional economy, creating or supporting 4,163 full-time job equivalents. The Bonnaroo Works Fund, a 501(c)(3) nonprofit created in 2009, provides grants to local schools and non-profits. Environmentally, the festival supports recycling and sustainability through initiatives like Planet Roo; in 2004, it prevented the collection of 120 tons of trash by encouraging fan recycling.

reception

Bonnaroo has been described as 'the Glastonbury Festival of the United States' due to its similarities to the British festival. It has received praise from publications such as Rolling Stone, which named it 'Best Festival' in 2008 and one of the '50 Moments That Changed Rock & Roll' in 2003, and The New York Times, which stated that Bonnaroo revolutionized the modern rock festival.

notable performances

The Beastie Boys played their final show ever at the festival in 2009. In 2012, R&B singer D'Angelo returned to American stages for the first time in over 12 years with a surprise performance at the annual Superjam.
