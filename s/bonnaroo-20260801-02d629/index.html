Bonnaroo (or the Bonnaroo Music and Arts Festival) is an annual four-day American music festival founded in 2002 by Superfly Presents and AC Entertainment. It takes place at Great Stage Park, a farm in Manchester, Tennessee.

history and origins

Bonnaroo was developed by Ashley Capps of AC Entertainment following the permanent suspension of the Hot Summer Nights rock music festival in Knoxville, Tennessee. The festival's name is derived from Creole slang for a 'really good time' and was inspired by the the same-named 1974 Dr. John album. Its spiritual predecessor was the Itchycoo Park Festival, which took place at the future site of Bonnaroo in 1999. The festival has faced several cancellations: the 2020 event was cancelled due to COVID-19 pandemic health concerns, the 2021 event was cancelled due to torrential rainfall from Hurricane Ida, and the 2025 event was cancelled due to severe weather. In 2019, Live Nation bought out Superfly's share of the festival.

festival layout and activities

The festival takes place at Great Stage Park, which is described as a 650- to 700-acre outdoor event space. The venue area is called 'Centeroo' and the campground area is called 'Outeroo'. Centeroo stages include the What Stage, Which Stage, That Tent, This Tent, and The Other Stage. The What Stage is the largest stage and can accommodate up to 80,000 fans. Outeroo features a variety of stages that vary year-to-year, including the Where in the Woods Stage since 2018 for late-night electronic music. The festival offers stylistically diverse music across multiple stages,-including indie rock, hip hop, jazz, Americana, bluegrass, country, folk, gospel, reggae, pop, electronic, and other alternative music. Beyond music, the festival provides artistic, environmental, and community activities such as yoga, a 5k run, sustainability lessons, and vendor markets.

economic and environmental impact

The Bonnaroo Works Fund, a 501(c)(3) nonprofit organization created in 2009, awards grants to local schools and non-profits. la owns the festival since its inception up to 2013, the direct and indirect economic impact was estimated at $51 million. By 2023, Bonnaroo contributed $339.8 million to the regional economy, including over $5.1 million in tax revenue and supporting 4,163 full-time job equivalents. Regarding environmentalism, Bonnaroo supports recycling and sustainability through initiatives like Planet Roo. In 2004, the festival stated it prevented 120 tons of trash from being collected by encouraging recycling. In 2011, A Greener Festival recognized these efforts with an award.

notable performances

The Beastie Boys played their final show ever at the festival in 2009. R&B singer D'Angelo performed a surprise set at the annual Superjam in 2012, marking his return to American stages for the first time in over 12 years. Recent performances include acts such as Modest Mouse, Noah Kahan, Clipse, and ROLE MODEL on June 14, 2026.
