The Ramones were an American punk rock band formed in 1974 in Forest Hills, Queens, New York City. They are recognized as one of the first bands of the genre and helped establish the punk movement in the United States and elsewhere. Although they never achieved significant commercial success during their existence, they remain highly influential in punk culture.

formation and early years

The band's original members—Joey, Dee Dee, Johnny, and Tommy Ramone—met in Forest Hills, Queens. They began taking shape in early 1974, with Dee Dee Ramone inspiring the group to adopt pseudonyms ending in 'Ramone', a practice inspired by Paul McCartney's use of the name Paul Ramon. The band played their first show on March 30, 1974, at Performance Studios and later made their debut at CBGB on August 16, 1974. They became regulars at the club, playing seventy-four times by the end of the year. In late 1975, they were signed to a recording contract by Seymour Stein of Sire Records.

musical style and debut album

The the Ramones' debut studio album, released on April 23, 1976, was recorded in just seven days on a budget of $6,400. The album is characterized by uptempo tracks, with many songs measuring over 160 beats per minute, and short songs; the longest track, "I Don't Wanna Go Down to the Basement", barely surpassed two and a half minutes. Lyrical themes included violence, drug use, Nazism, humor, and relationship issues. The album was unsuccessful commercially but received glowing reviews from critics and is now regarded as one of the greatest and most influential punk albums of all time.

title

The band experienced several lineup changes over 22 years, with Joey and Johnny Ramone remaining the only constant members. Tommy Ramone left in 1978 to pursue record production, replaced by Marky Ramone, who was later replaced by Richie Ramone in 1983. Dee Dee Ramone left in 1984, replaced by C. J. Ramone. From 1989 to their breakup in in 1996, the lineup consisted of Joey, Johnny, Marky and C. J. Ramone. The band performed 2,263 concerts and released fourteen studio albums before playing a farewell concert in Los Angeles in 1996.

legacy and recognition

The Ramones were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2002, including the original four members and Marky Ramone. In 2011, they were awarded a Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award. They have been ranked highly by publications such as Rolling Stone, Spin, and VH1, with Spin ranking them the second-greatest band of all time in 2002.
