Deep Dish refers to several distinct subjects, including a style of pizza developed in Chicago and an American electronic music duo. It is also the name of a global food documentary series and a 2008 romance novel.

chicago-style pizza

Chicago-style deep-dish pizza was invented at Pizzeria Uno in 1943 by Ike Sewell and Richard Riccardo. The style is characterized by a high edge created by the pan used for baking, which allows for significant amounts of cheese and chunky tomato sauce. Traditional recipes involve layering meats and/or vegetables and mozzarella cheese on the bottom, with crushed tomatoes ladled over the top before baking. Some versions may use cornmeal in the crust for texture or be parbaked. A variation known as stuffed pizza, created by Nancy's Pizza and Giordano's Pizzeria in the mid-1970s, is typically deeper, contains more cheese than standard deep-dish, and has an additional layer of dough covering the toppings.

electronic music duo

Deep Dish is an American electronic music duo consisting of Iranian-American artists Ali "Dubfire" Shirazinia and Sharam Tayebi. Based in Washington, D.C., they are known for their live DJ sets and house or dance remixes of tracks by artists such as Cher, Madonna, Janet Jackson, Gabrielle and Stevie Nicks. The duo released the album Junk Science in 1998 and won a Grammy Award for Best Remixed Recording for their remix of Dido's "Thank You". They disbanded in 2006 but reunited in 2014 to release the single "Quincy" and perform at the Ultra Music Festival. After announcing a permanent return to touring and studio work in 2024, they released the original single "Midnight" in 2025 and continue to tour as a duo in 2026.
