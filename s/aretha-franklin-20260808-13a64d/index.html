Aretha Franklin was an American pianist, songwriter and singer known as the "Queen of Soul". She was twice named by Rolling Stone magazine as the greatest singer of all time.

early life and gospel beginnings

Aretha Franklin was born on March 25, 1942, in Memphis, Tennessee, to Barbara and Clarence LaVaughn "C. L." Franklin. Her father was a Baptist minister and her mother was an accomplished vocalist and piano player. After moving to Detroit, Michigan, where her father took over the pastorship of New Bethel Baptist Church, Franklin learned to play piano by ear. She began singing solos at the church after her mother's death in 1952, and her father managed her early career, taking her on "gospel caravan" tours. Her first recording deal was with J.V.B. Records, where she recorded tracks such as "Never Grow Old" and "Precious Lord".

Franklin's early influences included Ray Charles, Sam Cooke, and James Cleveland, who helped focus her early career as a gospel singer. She also traveled with the Soul Stirrers and spent summers in Chicago with Mavis Staples's family.

recording career

Franklin signed with Columbia Records at age 18, but her career did not immediately flourish. After her contract expired in November 1966, she signed with Atlantic Records in 1966, where she found commercial success and recorded hit albums such as I Never Loved a Man the Way I Love You, Lady Soul, Aretha Now, Young, Gifted and Black, Amazing Grace, and Sparkle.

She later left Atlantic in 1979 to sign with Arista Records, where her career was revived with albums like Jump to It, Who's Zoomin' Who?, Aretha, and A Rose Is Still a Rose. Franklin is one of the best-selling music artists worldwide, having sold more than 75 million records. She had 112 singles on the US Billboard charts, including 73 Hot 100 entries and 20 number-one R&B singles.

the song "respect"

Franklin's rendition of "Respect", a cover of Otis Redding's original song, rearranged and reshaped the lyrics to provide a woman's perspective. Released on April 10, 1967, by Atlantic Records, it became an immediate hit, reaching number one on the Billboard Hot 10 umarestored as a protest anthem for both the civil rights movement of the 1960s and the second-wave feminist movement of the 1970s. It has been heralded as her signature song and was inducted into the Grammy Hall of Fame in 1987 and the Library of Congress' National Recording Registry in 2002.

honors and recognition

Franklin received numerous honors, including 18 Grammy Awards from 44 nominations. She was the first female artist to be inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1987, and was also awarded the National Medal of Arts and the Presidential Medal of Freedom. Posthumously, she was received a special citation from the the Pulitzer Prize jury in 2019 for her indelible contribution to American music and culture.
