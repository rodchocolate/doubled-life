Warner Bros. Records was an American record label that operated as the recorded music division of Warner Bros. Pictures. It later became known as Warner Records after 2019. As of 2026, it is a flagship subsidiary of Warner Music Group.

founding and early years

Warner Bros. Records was established on March 19, 1958, as the recording division of the film studio Warner Bros. Pictures. The label's creation was pushed by board member Serge Semenenko, who argued that an in-house label would create a new income stream and provide a means to promote contract actors. The first artist signed to the label was actor Tab Hunter. To manage the label, the company hired former Columbia Records president James B. Conkling. Founding directors of A&R were Bob Prince, George Avakian, and Harris Ashburn.

early catalogue and strategy

Between 1958 and 1960, the label's early album releases targeted the upscale end of the mainstream audience and the 'hi-fi' market through stereo LPs. The catalogue included film soundtracks, collections of TV themes, novelty and comedy albums by artists such as Bob Newhart and Spike Jones, and 'middle of the road' instrumental albums by artists including Henry Mancini and George Greeley. Some releases featured self-deprecating or jokey titles, such as "Music for People with $3.98 (Plus Tax If Any)" and "But You've도 Not Heard Gershwin with Bongos."

corporate history

Warner Bros. Pictures had previously attempted to enter the record business in 1930 when it acquired Brunswick Records via the Music Publishers Holding Company. However, due to the Great Depression and the rise of broadcast radio, sales crashed and Warner Bros. eventually offloaded Brunswick to the American Record Corporation (ARC) in 1931, and later sold it to Decca Records in 1941. Following this period of absence from the record business, the label was officially founded in 1958. In the mid-2000s, Warner Music Group was spun off as an independent company, and Warner Records has been independently owned since 2004.
