Cab Calloway was an American jazz singer, songwriter, bandleader, and actor whose career spanned over 65 years. He became a popular vocalist of the swing era and was a regular performer at Harlem's Cotton Club.

early life and education

Cabell Calloway III was born in December 1907 in Rochester, New York. He grew up in West Baltimore neighborhood of Druid Hill, where he often skipped school to earn money by selling newspapers and shining shoes. After being caught playing dice on church steps, his mother sent him to Downingtown Industrial and Agricultural School, a reform school in Pennsylvania. Calloway began private vocal lessons in 1922 and performed in nightclubs in Baltimore, mentored by pianist Johnny Jones and drummer Chick Webb. He graduated from Frederick Douglass High School in 1925 and briefly attended law school in Chicago before leaving to pursue performing.

music career

Calloway's music career began in earnest when he joined his sister Blanche Calloway on tour for the musical revue Plantation Days in 1927. He later performed at Chicago's Dreamland Café, Sunset Cafe, and Club Berlin, while also studying scat singing with Louis Armstrong. He relocated to New York in 1929 with the Alabamians band. After the Alabamians broke up, he fronted the Missourians, who became known as Cab Calloway and His Orchestra. In 1931, he was hired as a substitute for the Duke Ellington Orchestra at the Cotton Club in Harlem, which led to a permanent position. He was the first African American musician to sell one million copies of a record with his most famous song, 'Minnie the Moocher', recorded in 1931.

achievements and recognition

Calloway reached the Billboard charts in five consecutive decades from the 1930s to the 1970s. He was the first African American to have a nationally syndicated radio program. He received the National Medal of Arts from the United States Congress in 1993 and posthumously received the Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award in 2008. His song 'Minnie the Moocher' was inducted into the the Grammy Hall of Fame in 1999 and selected for preservation in the Library of Congress' National Recording Registry in 2019.
