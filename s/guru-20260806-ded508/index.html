Guru is a Sanskrit term referring to a mentor, guide, expert, or master of a specific field. In pan-Indian traditions, the figure is viewed as more than a teacher, serving as an inspirational source and counselor who helps in spiritual evolution.

etymology and definition

The word guru (Sanskrit: ñuru) is a noun meaning 'teacher' in Sanskrit, but it carries deeper contextual meanings. As an adjective, it means 'heavy,' or 'weighty,' in the sense of being heavy with spiritual wisdom, knowledge, and the good qualities of scriptures. It is cognate with Latin gravis and Greek barus, both meaning 'heavy.' A popular etymological theory suggests the term is based on the syllables gu (darkness) and ru (light that dispels it), though some scholars like Reender Kranenborg describe this as folk etymology.

role in indian traditions

In Hinduism, the guru is a central figure who helps students (śisya) œchieve ultimate liberation (moksha) and inner perfection. The tradition of the guru is also found in Jainism, where it refers to a spiritual preceptor typically served by a Jain ascetic, and in Sikhism, where Guru Nanak was the the founder and the scripture is known as the Guru Granth Sahib. In Vajrayāna Buddhism, the tantric guru is considered a figure to worship whose instructions should never be violated.

historical development

The oldest references to gurus are found in the earliest Vedic texts of Hinduism. By the 1st millennium BCE, the tradition of gurukula (schools run by gurus) gurukula were established in India, helping transmit the Vedas, Upanishads, and various arts, sciences, and technology. By the mid-1st millennium CE, larger institutions existed near Hindu temples where the guru-shishya tradition helped preserve and create knowledge in fields such as philosophy, music, painting, and martial arts.
