The Mahavishnu Orchestra was a jazz fusion band formed in New York City in 1971 by English guitarist John McLaughlin. The group's music was characterized by a blend of Indian classical music, jazz, and psychedelic rock, blending electric rock, funk, and complex time signatures.

formation and first incarnation

The band's first line-up featured John McLaughlin, drummer Billy Cobham, keyboardist Jan Hammer, violinist Jerry Goodman, and bassist Rick Laird. The group's name originates from Indian spiritual leader Sri Chinmoy, who gave McLaughlin the name Mahavishnu, with 'Maha' meaning 'great' in Sanskrit and 'vishnu' after the Hindu deity Vishnu. After rehearsing for one week in July 1971, they recorded their debut album, The Inner Mounting Flame, released in November 1971. This was followed by Birds of Fire (1973). They also released a live album, Between Nothingness & Eternity. Their first incarnation ended in January 1974 when McLaughlin split the group due to pressures of fame, exhaustion, and lack of communication.

subsequent incarnations

The band reformed in 1974 with a new cast of musicians including Jean-Luc Ponty on violin, Gayle Moran on keyboards, Ralphe Armstrong on bass guitar, and Narada Michael Walden on drums. This version of the orchestra expanded to include various violins, viola, and cello, and recorded Apocalypse (1974) with the London Symphony Orchestra under the direction of Michael Tilson Thomas, and Visions of the Emerald Beyond (1975). In 1976, the band was reduced to a four-piece for Inner Worlds. After a period of dissolution, McLaughlin reformed the group again in 1984 with Bill Evans on saxophones, Jonas Hellborg on bass, Mitchel Forman on keyboards, and original member Billy Cobham on drums. This third incarnation's sound was different from the original, specifically due to McLaughlin's extensive use of the Synclavier synthesizer system.

musical style and discography

The Mahavishnu Orchestra's music was characterized by complex, intense music consisting of a blend of Indian classical music, jazz, and the psychedelic rock.
