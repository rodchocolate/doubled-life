Jazz fusion is a popular music genre that developed in the late 1960s by combining jazz harmony and improvisation with rock music, funk, and rhythm and blues. It is often viewed as a musical tradition or approach rather than a codified style.

Jazz fusion is a popular music genre that developed in the late 1960s by combining jazz harmony and improvisation with rock music, funk, and rhythm and blues. It is often viewed as a musical tradition or approach rather than a codified style.

musical characteristics

Jazz fusion arrangements vary in complexity, ranging from groove-based vamps fixed to a single key or chord with simple melodies to elaborate chord progressions and unconventional time signatures similar to progressive rock. These arrangements typically include improvised sections of varying lengths. While brass and woodwind instruments like the trumpet and saxophone are used, they are often substituted by electric guitars, electric pianos, organs, synthesizers, and bass guitar. Fusion bands are less likely to use acoustic piano and double bass.

origins and early development

The genre emerged in the late 1960s as musicians who grew up listening to rock and roll began incorporating electric instruments and amplifiers popular in rock. Guitarist Larry Coryell, often called the godfather of fusion, recorded Out of Sight and Sound in 1967 with his band the Free Spirits. Other early pioneers emphasized volume, electricity, intensity, and virtuosity. In 1968, music journalist Zaid Mudhaffer coined the term "jazz fusion" in a review of David Axelrod's Song of Innocence.

influence of miles davis

Miles Davis is a central figure in the genre, with his 1969 album In a Silent Way being considered his first fusion album. His subsequent 1969 album Bitches Brew abandoned the swing beat for rock and roll backbeats and bass guitar grooves, mixing free jazz blowing with electronic keyboards and guitar. Despite commercial success, some jazz critics accused Davis of betraying the essence of jazz, as rock music was viewed by some in the jazz community as less sophisticated than jazz.

evolution and later artists

Fusion expanded its experimental approaches through the 1980s, continuing into the 1990s and 2000s. Other influential musicians including Herbie Hancock, who brought funk and disco elements into albums like Head Hunters, and Chick Corea, who combined jazz, rock, pop, and Brazilian music in Return to Forever. Joe Zawinul and Wayne Shorter founded Weather Report in 1970, which remained active until 1986. The American group The Crusaders, formerly the Jazz Crusaders, transitioned from hard bop to a jazz-funk style incorporating electric instruments.

facts

  • Alternative Names: Jazz rock, jazz-rock fusion, or simply fusion
  • First Fusion Album by Miles Davis: In a Silent Way (1969)
  • Godfather of Fusion: Larry Coryell
  • Term Coined By: Zaid Mudhaffer in 1968
  • Parallel Style Development: Smooth jazz (developed during the 1980s)
  • Common Instruments: Electric guitars, electric pianos, organs, synthesizers, and bass guitar
