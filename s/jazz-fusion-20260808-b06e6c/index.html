Jazz fusion is a popular music genre that emerged in the late 1960s by combining jazz harmony and improvisation with rock music, funk, and rhythm and blues. The genre is characterized by the use of electric guitars, basses, amplifiers, and keyboard instruments like electric pianos and organs. It is often viewed as a musical tradition or approach rather than a a codified musical style.

musical characteristics

Jazz fusion arrangements vary in complexity, ranging from groove-based vamps fixed to a single key or chord with simple repeated melodies to elaborate chord progressions and unconventional time signatures similar to progressive rock. These arrangements typically include improvised sections of varying lengths. While brass and woodwind instruments like the trumpet and saxophone are used, they are often substituted by electric guitars, synthesizers, organs, electric pianos, and bass guitar, making the use of acoustic piano and double bass less likely.

origins and early development

The genre's origins trace back to the late 1960s. Guitarist Larry Coryell, often called the godfather of fusion, recorded the album Out of Sight and Sound in 1967 with his band the Free Spirits. Other early pioneers emphasized electricity, volume, intensity, and virtuosity. Charles Lloyd played a combination of rock and jazz at the Monterey Jazz Festival in 1966 and adopted trappings of the California psychedelic rock scene. Flautist Jeremy Steig experimented with jazz in his band Jeremy & the Satyrs. According to music journalist Zaid Mudhaffer, the term 'jazz fusion' was coined in a review of David Axelrod's 1968 album Song of Innocence.

influence of miles davis

Miles Davis played a pivotal role in the development of the genre. His 1969 album In a Silent Way is considered his first fusion album, featuring pioneers such as Chick Corea and Herbie Hancock. In 1969, he recorded Bitches Brew, where he abandoned the swing beat for rock and roll backbeats and bass guitar grooves, using electronic effects and pedals on his trumpet. Despite commercial success, some jazz critics accused him of betraying the essence of jazz, while others viewed rock music as less sophisticated than jazz.

other key musicians and groups

Herbie Hancock brought elements of disco and funk into commercially successful albums like Head Hunters. Chick Corea combined jazz, rock, pop, and Brazilian music in his band Return to Forever. Joe Zawinul and Wayne Shorter started the influential band Weather Report in 1970. The Crusaders (formerly the Jazz Crusaders) were an American group that featured a wide range of genres including R&B, jazz-funk, and jazz-rock, achieving significant commercial success with their 1979 album Street Life.
