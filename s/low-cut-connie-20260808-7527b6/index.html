Low Cut Connie is an American rock and roll band based in Philadelphia, Pennsylvania. The group's only constant member since its formation has been frontman, songwriter, and pianist Adam Weiner.

formation and early years

Adam Weiner performed as a solo artist under the name Ladyfingers while living in New York City, playing in restaurants, karaoke bars, gay bars, and ballet classes. The project that became Low Cut Connie began as an impromptu recording session in 2010 with former members Neil Duncan and Dan Finnemore. The band's name was derived from combining suggestions of "the Low Cut" and "the Connies," and Weiner later created a sketch of a diner waitress from southern New Jersey named Connie, which appeared on the cover of their debut album.

discography and critical reception

The band's debut album, Get Out the Lotion, was recorded in 2010 over four days in Neil Duncan's garage in Gainesville, Florida, and released in 2011. It received favorable reviews from critics such as Robert Christgau and Ken Tucker, with some comparing the music to Jerry Lee Lewis. Their second album, Call Me Sylvia, released in September 2012, was included by Rolling Stone on its list of the 100 Best Albums of the 2010s. Subsequent albums include Hi Honey (2015), Dirty Pictures (Part 1) (2017) and Dirty Pictures (Part 2) (2018), which debuted on Billboard's Independent albums chart. Their sixth album, a double-length 17-song record titled Private Lives, was released on October 16, 2020.

notable endorsements and performances

Low Cut Connie has received high-profile endorsements from Elton John, who called the band one of his favorites, and former President Barack Obama, who included the song "Boozophilia" on his 2015 Spotify Summer Playlist. The band also made their network television debut on Late Night with Seth Meyers on August 13, 2018, performing the song "All These Kids Are Way Too High."
