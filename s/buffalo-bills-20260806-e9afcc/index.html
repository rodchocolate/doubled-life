The Buffalo Bills are a professional American football team based in the Buffalo–Niagara Falls metropolitan area. They compete in the National Football League (NFL) as a member of the American Football Conference (AFC) East division and play home games at Highmark Stadium in Orchard Park, New York.

overview

The Bills are the only NFL team that plays home games in the state of New York. The franchise is owned by Terry Pegula and a coalition of private equity funds and investors who purchased a minority stake in 2024. In 2024, they became one of the first NFL teams to sell part of their franchise to outside private equity investors, selling 20.6% of the team interest at a valuation of $5.6 billion, including 10% to Arctos Partners LP.

history

Founded in 1959 as a charter member of the American Football League (AFL), the Bills joined the NFL in 1970 after the AFL–NFL merger. The team's name is taken from an All-America Football Conference (AAFC) franchise from Buffalo that was named after western frontiersman Buffalo Bill. They won two consecutive AFL titles in 1964 and 1965, which are the only major professional sports championships for a team representing Buffalo. After a period of mediocrity, they became perennial contenders in the late 1980s through the mid-1990s under head coach Marv Levy, winning four consecutive AFC Championships but losing all four subsequent Super Bowls.

recent performance and coaching

Following a 17-year playoff drought between 2000 and 2016, the team returned to consistent postseason contention under head coach Sean McDermott. During this era, they earned division championships and playoff wins since 1995 during the 2020 season, and won the AFC East in 2021 and 2022. However, Sean McDermott was fired on January 19, 2026, following a defeat in the2025 season's Divisional round. Joe Brady, who previously served as offensive coordinator, was promoted to head coach on January 27, 2026.
