Bill Graham (1931–1991) was an American concert promoter who had a profound influence on the musical renaissance of the 1960s from its epicenter in San Francisco. He is known for making the Fillmore and Winterland Ballroom famous as venues for rock bands and acts of the Bay Area.

early life

Born Wulf Wolodia Grajonca in Berlin, Germany, Graham was the son of lower middle-class Jewish parents. Due to Nazi persecution, he and his sister Tanya "Tolla" were placed in a Berlin orphanage and sent to France as part of a pre-Holocaust exchange of Jewish children for Christian orphans. After the fall of France in 1940, he was among a group of Jewish orphans spirited out of France to North America as one of the One Thousand Children (OTC). His mother died on the way to Auschwitz concentration camp and his sister Tolla did not survive the journey to the United States. He settled into a foster home in the Bronx, New York, where he changed his name to sound more "American" after being taunted for his immigrant accent. He graduated from DeWitt Clinton High School and earned a business degree from the City College of New York. In 1951, he was drafted into the United States Army and served in the Korean War, where he was awarded the Bronze Star and Purple Heart.

career in san francisco

Graham moved to San Francisco in the early 1960s. He began his career as a promoter by organizing a benefit concert for the San Francisco Mime Troupe after their leader, R. G. Davis, was arrested on obscenity charges. This success led him to team up with Chet Helms and Family Dog projects to promote concerts that served as social meeting places and forums for various ideologies, including civil rights and peace movements. Graham eventually secured a contract for open dates at the Fillmore Auditorium, which he used as a caminho to establish permanent locations of his own. Despite facing challenges with City Hall regarding dance hall permits, he eventually obtained a permit after an editorial in the San Francisco Chronicle on April 21, 1966, which he described as a big turning point. He also worked with Charles Sullivan, an entrepreneur who owned the master lease on the Fillmore Auditorium.

title la own

Graham died in 1991 in a helicopter crash. In 1992, the San Francisco Board of Supervisors voted to rename the San Francisco Civic Auditorium as the Bill Graham Civic Auditorium, a multi-purpose arena that holds 8,500 people.
