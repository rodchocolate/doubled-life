Massey Hall is a performing arts auditorium located in Toronto, Ontario, Canada, within the Garden District. Opened in 1894, it is recognized for its outstanding acoustics and served as the long-time hall of the Toronto Symphony Orchestra and the Toronto Mendelssohn Choir.

history and architecture

Massey Hall was a gift to the people of Toronto from industrialist Hart Massey, who sought to create a secular meeting place for choral music in memory of his son Charles. Designed by architect Sidney Badgley and completed in 1894 at a cost of CA$152,390.75, the building features a Palladian exterior with a neoclassical facade preferred by Massey's daughter Lillian. The interior is Moorish Revival architecture inspired by the Alhambra Palace in Spain and Louis Sullivan's Chicago Auditorium, featuring Moorish arches and thirty stained glass windows depicting famous composers created by the Faircloth Brothers glass firm. The foyer is finished in Art Deco style. After the 1904 Toronto Great Fire, fire escape staircases were installed along the front face of the building, which later became iconic before being removed. In 1933, the Massey Foundation undertook renovations that reduced the capacity from 3,500 to 2,765 and simplified the venue's name from Massey Music Hall to Massey Hall.

renovations and facilities

The hall closed in July 2018 for a two-year renovation and restoration project titled "Massey Hall Forever," which included an expanded stage, a new retractable seating system, and the addition of the Allied Music Centre. The Allied Music Centre is a seven-storey building containing a performance studio on the seventh floor, a smaller theatre space on the sixth floor, and a new concert space on the fourth floor (TD Music Hall). The hall re-opened in 2021 after delays caused by the COVID-19 pandemic. Current capacity for the main hall is 2,534 seated and 2,800 standing, while TD Music Hall has a capacity of 245 seated and 450 standing. In 1994, the basement was refurbished to include Centuries, a bar with a capacity of 220 people that hosts post-show parties and CD releases, featuring decor consisting of hundreds of photos and autographs of artists who have performed there.

performances and events

While primarily used for musical performances, the hall has hosted public memorials, boxing matches, speaking tours and political speeches. Notable performers include operatic singers such as Luciano Pavarotti, Maria Callas and Enrico Caruso, and classical musicians like Arturo Toscanini and Glenn Gould. Until 1984, the Toronto Symphony Orchestra and the Toronto Mendelssohn Choir were the primary performers. The hall also hosted a May 15, 1953 concert by 'the Quintet' (Dizzy Gillespie, Charlie Parker, Bud Powell, Charles Mingus, and Max Roach), which was recorded as the album Jazz at Massey Hall. This recording was later inducted into the Grammy Hall of Fame in 1995.
