Clube de Regatas do Flamengo is a Brazilian multi-sports club based in Rio de Janeiro's Gávea neighborhood. Founded in 1895 and named after the Flamengo neighborhood, it is most famous for its professional football team.

establishment and early history

The club was founded on 17 November 1895 by a group of rowers at Nestor de Barros's manor on Flamengo Beach. Initially established as a rowing club, the football department was only created on 24 December 1911 after ten dissatisfied players from Fluminense joined the rest of the club. The team's first official match took place on 3 May 1912, where they defeated Mangueira 16-2, marking the largest margin of victory in the club's history.

football success and popularity

Flamengo is one of two clubs to have never been relegated from Brazil's top division, along with São Paulo. It is considered the most popular football club in Brazil, with over 46.9 million fans, representing 21.9% of the population that supports a team in Brazil. The club has amassed a considerable array of national and international titles and is described as the most successful Brazilian team of the 21st century so far.

title

Flamengo is Brazil's richest and most valuable football club, with an annual revenue of R$1.2 billion (€218 million) and a valuation of over R$5,096 billion (€800 million). Its training center, 'Ninho do Urubu', located in Vargem Grande, Rio de Janeiro, houses the professional teams and youth academy. The club's mascot is the vulture (urubu), which has been used since 1969.

national team representation

Flamengo players have been well represented in the Brazil national team. Mário Zagallo scored Brazil's third goal in the 1958 World Cup final, and forward Leônidas da Silva won both the Golden Boot and Golden Ball at the 1938 FIFA World Cup. Additionally, Zizinho, a midfielder for Flamengo, won the Golden Ball at the 1950 World Cup.
