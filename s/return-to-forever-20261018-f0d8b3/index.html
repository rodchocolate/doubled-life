Return to Forever is an American jazz fusion band founded by pianist Chick Corea in 1972. The group is often cited as one of the core groups of the 1970s jazz-fusion movement alongside Weather Report, The Headhunters, and Mahavishnu Orchestra.

formation and early period

Founded in 1972 by Chick Corea after his time with Miles Davis and the avant-garde jazz band Circle, Corea owns a more accessible style of music than avant-garde jazz. The first edition of the group performed primarily Latin-oriented music featuring singer and percussionist Flora Purim, husband Airto Moreira on drums and percussion, Joe Farrell on saxophone and flute, and Stanley Clarke on bass. Their debut self-titled album was recorded for ECM Records in 1972 and initially released only in Europe. This was followed by the same lineup performing on Stan Getz's Captain Marvel album (1972) and their second album, Light as a Feather (1973), which included the well-known song "Spain".

jazz rock era

Between 1973 and 1976, the band's sound shifted to jazz-rock or jazz fusion, moving away from vocals and becoming entirely instrumental. This era was characterized by over-driven, distorted guitar and electric bass, featuring members such as Bill Connors, Lenny White, and later Al Di Meola. Their music remained melodic with strong themes but incorporated rock-oriented approaches and instrumental improvisation. During this period, they released several albums including Hymn of the Seventh Galaxy (1973), Where Have I Known You Before (1974), No Mystery (1975)—which won a Grammy Award for Best Jazz Performance by a Group—and Romantic Warrior (1976), their best-selling album which reached gold disc status.

later years and reunions

Return to Forever first disbanded in 1977 after five years and seven studio albums. While the band never released another studio album, they occasionally reunited for live performances until Chick Corea's death in 2021.
