The Patti Smith Group is an American musical ensemble featuring singer, songwriter, and poet Patti Smith. The group emerged from the New York City punk rock movement of the 1970s, fusing rock music with spoken poetry.

formation and early career

In 1973, Patti Smith teamed up with musician and rock archivist Lenny Kaye, later adding Richard Sohl on piano. The ensemble expanded into a full band including Jay Dee Daugherty on drums and Ivan Král on guitar and bass. Financed by Sam Wagstaff, the group recorded their first single, "Hey Joe/Piss Factory" in 1974. In March 1975, the Patti Smith Group began a two-month weekend set of shows at CBGB in New York City alongside the band Television.

discography and commercial success

The group's debut album, Horses, was recorded in later 1975 and produced by John Cale. The follow-up album, Radio Ethiopia, featured a rawer sound and was influenced by the band MC5. In March 1978, the group released Easter, which is regarded as their commercial breakthrough and most commercially accessible record. Produced by Jimmy Iovine, Easter included the diversity of musical styles including folk, straightforward rock, and spoken word. The album's successful single "Because the Night", co-written with Bruce Springsteen, reached number 13 on the Billboard Hot 100 and number five on the UK Singles Chart.

crossover and later years

The group worked together through the late 1970s until Patti Smith's tour in support of Radio Ethiopia ended with a severe neck injury in Tampa, Florida, on January 23, 1977. Despite this injury, the group produced two further albums: Easter and Wave (1979). By the mid-1980s, Smith was semi-retired from music. In 1996, she worked with her long-time band to record Gone Aga.
