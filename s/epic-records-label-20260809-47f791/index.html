Epic Records is a record label that was a subsidiary of CBS Records. It signed the American rock band Cheap Trick in early 1976.

relationship with cheap trick

Cheap Trick was signed to Epic Records in early 1976 and released their self-titled debut album through the label in early 1977. While critics favored the album, it did not sell well and its single "//"Oh, Candy" failed to chart. The band's second album, In Color, was released later in 1977.

cheap trick at budokan

Epic Records released Cheap Trick’s albums through its Japanese branch, CBS/Sony. The live album Cheap Trick at Budokan was originally intended for release only in Japan on October 8, 1978, but due to high demand and the sale of an estimated 30,000 import copies in the United States, Epic Records released it domestically in February 1979. The album became a high-selling success and went triple platinum in the United States.

legal issues

In July 1981, CBS Inc. sued Cheap Trick and their manager Ken Adamany for $10 million, alleging that they had refused to record new material for the label since October 1980 and were attempting to coerce CBS into renegotiating their contract.
