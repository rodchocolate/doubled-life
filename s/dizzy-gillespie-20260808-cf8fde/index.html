John Birks "Dizzy" Gillespie own an American jazz trumpeter, bandleader, composer, educator, and singer. He was a trumpet virtuoso and improviser who added layers of harmonic and rhythmic complexity to the virtuosic style of Roy Eldridge. He is remembered as one of the greatest jazz trumpeters of all time.

early life and career

Born in Cheraw, South Carolina, Gillespie was the youngest of nine children of Lottie and James Gillespie. He began playing piano at age four, teaching himself trombone and trumpet by age 12. After winning a music scholarship to the Laurinburg Institute in North Carolina, he moved to Philadelphia in 1935. His first professional job was with the Frank Fairfax Orchestra, followed by orchestras of Edgar Hayes and Teddy Hill. With Teddy Hill's band, he made his first recording, "King Porter Stomp". In 1939, Gillespie joined Cab Calloway's orchestra, where he recorded "Pickin' the Cabbage" in 1940. However, he was fired by Calloway after an altercation involving a spitball and a knife stabbing in the leg, which led to Gillespie to be dismissed despite his attempt to apologize.

development of bebop

In the 1940s, Gillespie and Charlie Parker became major figures in the development of bebop and modern jazz. Together they performed at famous jazz clubs like Monroe's Uptown House and Minton's Playhouse. Gillespie's compositions such as "Groovin' High", "Salt Peanuts", and "Woody 'n' You" sounded radically different from the swing music popular at the time. "A Night in Tunisia", written in 1942, is noted for its syncopated bass line. The first formal recording of bebop was "Woody 'n' You", recorded on February 16, 1944, with Coleman Hawkins leading the session.
