Robert Fripp is an English musician, composer, record producer, and author. He is most recognized as the founder and only constant member of the progressive rock band King Crimson.

early life and education

Born in Wimborne Minster, Dorset, England, Fripp was the second child of a workingen-class family. He received a guitar at age eleven in 1957, which he describes as the beginning of his musical life. After taking lessons from Don Strike and Kathleen Gartell, he was inspired by Scotty Moore to play rock and roll, later moving into traditional and modern jazz. Influences include Charlie Parker and Charles Mingus. At fifteen, he joined his first band, the Ravens, and later played in The Douglas Ward Trio and The League of Gentlemen. He studied economics, economic history, and political history for his A-levels at Bournemouth College, while playing light jazz in the Majestic Dance Orchestra. During this workday, he met future collaborators such as Greg Lake, Richard Palmer-James, and John Wetton.

musical career and king crimson

In 1967, Fripp joined Peter and Michael Giles in the trio Giles, Giles and Fripp, releasing one studio album in 1968. In mid-1968, he formed King Crimson with Michael Giles and Ian McDonald, recruiting Greg Lake as lead singer and bassist and Peter Sinfield as lyricist. Their debut album, In the Court of the Crimson King (1969), is considered one of the most influential albums in progressive rock history. Following a series of lineup changes and various albums including Lizard and Islands, Fripp took over de facto leadership of the band in 1971 after ousting Peter Sinfield. He remained the only constant member of the the same band throughout its various iterations.

solo work and collaborations

Fripp has worked extensively as a session musician and collaborator, notably with artists such as David Bowie, Blondie, Blondie, Brian Eno, Peter Gabriel, Daryl Hall, Talking Heads, and David Sylvian. He also composed the startup sound for Windows Vista in collaboration with Steve Ball and Tucker Martine. His discography includes contributions to over 700 official releases. In 1979, he released his debut solo album, Exposure, which focused on the rock song format and grew out of collaborations with Peter Gabriel and Daryl Hall. He also developed a tape-delay/looping system known as 'Frippertronics' (later superseded by the digital 'Soundscapes' system in the 1990s) and New Standard Tuning.

technical innovations

The term 'Frippertronics' was coined by his partner at the time, Joanna Walton. He developed this tape-delay/looping technique with Brian Eno. He later transitioned to a digital system called 'Soundscapes' in the 1990s. Fripp's compositions often feature unusual asymmetric rhythms influenced by folk and classical traditions.
