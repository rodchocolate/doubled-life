Robert Fripp is an English musician, composer, record producer, and author. He is most recognized as the founder and only constant member of the progressive rock band King Crimson.

early life and education

Born in Wimborne Minster, Dorset, England, Fripp was the second child of a working-class family. He received a guitar at age eleven and studied under Kathleen Gartell and Don Strike. His early musical influences included rock and roll via Scotty Moore, traditional jazz at thirteen, and modern jazz at fifteen, with Charlie Parker and Charles Mingus as key figures. In 1961, he joined his first band, the Ravens, before briefly working as a junior negotiator in his father's estate agency business. At seventeen, he became a professional musician, playing with The Douglas Ward Trio and The League of Gentlemen. He later attended Bournemouth College to study economics, economic history, and political history for his A-levels.

career with king crimson

Fripp founded King Crimson in 1968 with Michael Giles, Greg Lake, Ian McDonald, and Peter Sinfield. The band's debut album, In the Court of the Crimson King (1969), is considered one of the most influential albums in progressive rock. Fripp remained the only constant member throughout the band's history, leading the group through various incarnations, including a 'Double Trio' sextet and a 'Double Duo' quartet. The band drew from diverse influences such as classical, jazz, folk, heavy metal, gamelan, and industrial music. After several hiatuses, King Crimson toured as an octet from 2014 to 2021, with Fripp stating that the band had 'moved from sound to silence' following their final show.

solo work and innovations

Fripp has contributed to over 700 official releases and has composed the startup sound for Windows Vista. He developed a tape delay system known as 'Frippertronics', which was later superseded by a digital system called 'Soundscapes' in the 1990s. His solo debut album, Exposure (1979), grew out of collaborations with artists like David Bowie, Peter Gabriel, and Daryl Hall. Fripp also introduced New Standard Tuning for his guitar. His compositions often feature unusual asymmetric rhythms influenced by classical and folk traditions.
