Swizz Beatz, born Kasseem Daoud Dean, is an American record producer, rapper, and DJ. Born and raised in the Bronx, New York City, he began his musical career as a DJ in 1994.

early life and career beginnings

Dean spent much of his childhood in the North East Bronx. After obtaining equipment from his stepfather and uncles, he began DJing as a teenager. Due to repeated involvement in violent behavior at Harry S. Truman High School, he relocated to Atlanta, Georgia, where he worked for his uncles, Joaquin (Waah) and Darrin (Dee) Dean, the co-CEOs of Ruff Ryders Entertainment. At age 16, he began producing tracks and was signed as a producer and songwriter to Ruff Ryders Productions. He sold his first beat to rapper DMX, resulting in the chart-topping single "Ruff Ryders' Anthem" on the 1998 album It's Dark and Hell is Hot.

production career

With a career spanning over two decades, Dean has contributed production to over 160 albums across hip-hop, pop, soul, rock, and R&B. He has produced 81 singles, several of which have received gold certification or higher from the RIAA. His works include "Ruff Ryders' Anthem", "Party Up (Up in Here)" for DMX, "Upgrade U" and "Ring the Alarm" for Beyoncé, "Fancy" for Drake, and "Famous" for Kanye West. He has won a Grammy Award and was named the inaugural "Producer in Residence" at New York University in 2010.

record labels and solo work

In 2001, Dean established Full Surface Records as a subsidiary of J Records in a joint venture with Clive Davis. The label signed acts such as Cassidy, Eve, Mashonda, and Bone Thugs-n-Harmony. As a recording artist, he released the compilation album Swizz Beatz Presents G.H.E.T.T.O. Stories (2002) and his debut studio album One Man Band Man (2007), which peaked at number seven on the Billboard 200. In 2018, he signed with Epic Records to release his second album, Poison.

other ventures

Aside from music, Dean has involved himself in fashion design, art collection, and board directing. He served as a creative director for Reebok and Monster Cable. Since 2015, he has been a member of trustees at the Brooklyn Museum. He and his wife, Alicia Keys, jointly won the Producers & Engineers Wing Award by The Recording Academy in 2018.
