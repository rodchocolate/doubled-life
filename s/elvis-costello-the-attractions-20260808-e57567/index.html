Elvis Costello & The Attractions were an English new wave musician and his backing band who performed together from 1977 to 1986, and again from 1994 to 1996. The group consisted of Steve Nieve, Bruce Thomas, and Pete Thomas, who backed Costello's vocals and guitar. The ensemble has been described as one of the best backing bands in rock history.

formation and early history

The Attractions were formed on the eve of the release of Elvis Costello's debut album, My Aim Is True (1977), replacing the American West Coast band Clover. The original lineup consisted of Steve Nieve on keyboards, Bruce Thomas on bass guitar, and Pete Thomas on drums. According to Tyler Wilcox of Pitchfork, the band provided a 'minimal, hard-edged sound' that suited the punk scene in England at the time. The group made their live debut on 14 July 1977 and appeared on Saturday Night Live in December 1977 as a last-minute replacement for the Sex Pistols, an event that resulted in Costello being banned from the show until 1989.

musical career and discography

The Attractions backed Costello on all of his albums and singles until 1984, except for the solo single 'New Amsterdam' (1980). Their first album together was This Year's Model (1978), and they did not receive official co-billing credit until Armed Forces (1979). They recorded several albums including Get Happy!! (1980), Trust (1981), Almost Blue (1981), Imperial Bedroom ((1982), Punch the Clock (1983), and Goodbye Cruel World (1984). In 1986, they appeared on only one track of King of America and the B-side 'Baby's Got A Brand New Hairdo'. Later that year, they recorded Blood & Chocolate (1986), which was their final release for several years. They reunited for tracks on Brutal Youth (1994) and toured together from 1994 to 1996, before splitting for good in 1996.

npp independent work

In 1980, The Attractions recorded an independent album titled Mad About the Wrong Boy, featuring original compositions by all three members. This album was produced by Roger Bechirian and included singles such as 'Single Girl' and 'Arms Race'. Due to their use of pseudonyms and association with Bechirian, they were erroneously rumored to be behind the synthpop trio Blanket of Secrecy (BoS), whose lone album Walls Have Ears (1982) was produced by Bechirian.
