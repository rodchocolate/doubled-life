Johnny Cash own an American singer-songwriter known for his deep, calm, bass-baritone voice and trademark all-black stage wardrobe, which earned him the nickname "Man in Black". His music often features themes of sorrow, moral tribulation, and redemption.

early life and military service

Born to poor cotton farmers in Kingsland, Arkansas, Cash grew up on gospel music and played on a local radio station in high school. He enlisted in the Air Force on July 7, 1950, and served as a Morse code operator in West Germany with the 12th Radio Squadron Mobile of the U.S. Air Force Security Service. While there, he formed his first band, "The Landsberg Barbarians". After being honorably discharged as a staff sergeant on July 3, 1954, he returned to Texas.

musical career and style

Cash rose to fame in the mid-1950s within the Memphis, Tennessee rockabilly scene. His music spanned genres including country, rock and roll, rockabilly, blues, folk, and gospel sounds. He is one of the best-selling music artists of all time, having sold over 90 million records worldwide. He was inducted into the Country Music, Rock and Roll, and Gospel Music Halls of Fame. His signature songs include "I Walk the Line", "Ring of Fire", "Get Rhythm", and "Man in Black". He also recorded railroad songs such as "Rock Island Line" and humorous numbers like "A Boy Named Sue".

later career and covers

During his final years, Cash covered songs by contemporary rock artists including Depeche Mode's "Personal Jesus" and Soundgarden's "Rusty Cage". He also covered "Hurt" by Nine Inch Nails for his final album during his lifetime, American IV: The Man Comes Around. This cover of "Hurt" is widely considered one of his best works and has sold over 2 million downloads in the United States as of March 2017.
