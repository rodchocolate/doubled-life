Pete King (1929–2009) was a British jazz tenor saxophonist and co-founder of Ronnie Scott's Jazz Club in London. He served as the club's manager for nearly fifty years.

musical career

King began his professional work in 1947 with Jiver Hutchinson, later joining bands led by Leon Roy, Teddy Foster and Kenny Graham. In 1948, he served as first tenor in George Evans' Saxes ‘n’ Sevens. He also played with Kathy Stobart from 1950 to 1952 and Oscar Rabin between 1948 and 1950. In September 1952, he recorded with the Ronnie Scott Quintet. During the latter half of the 1950s, King was a member of Jack Parnell's band and later joined a nine-piece orchestra led by Scott that featured leading jazz musicians such as Benny Green, Ken Wray and Derek Humble. In 1956, he was also a member of the Victor Feldman Big Band.

ronnie scott's jazz club

King co-founded Ronnie Scott's Jazz Club with Ronnie Scott on 30 October 1959. After the break-up of The Jazz Couriers, which King had managed, he effectively gave up playing to run the club. He continued managing the club until several years after Scott's death in 1996, eventually selling it in June 2005 to Michael Watt and theatre impresario Sally Greene. In addition to day-to-day operations, King was instrumental in negotiations between the Musicians' Union and the American Federation of Musicians to lift a ban on American musicians, which allowed for more regular exchanges of British and American players.
