Darius Milhaud (1892–1974) was a prolific French composer, conductor, and teacher who is considered one of the key modernist composers. He was a member of Les Six and made extensive use of polytonality in his compositions. His work was heavily influenced by jazz and Brazilian music.

life and career

Born in Marseille, Milhaud grew up in Aix-en-Provence. He studied at the Paris Conservatory under Charles-Marie Widor, André Gedalge, and privately with Vincent d'Indy. After serving as secretary to Paul Claudel in Brazil from 1917 to 1919, he composed works influenced by Brazilian popular music, such as Le Bøef sur le toit and Saudades do Brasil. In 1922, a trip to the United States where he heard 'authentic' jazz in Harlem, influenced his composition of La création du monde. Due to the Nazi invasion of France in 1940, Milhaud emigrated to the U.S., where he secured a teaching post at Mills College in Oakland, California. He taught there and at the Paris Conservatoire from 1947 to 1971 until poor health forced his retirement. He died in Geneva and was buried in the Saint-Pierre Cemetery in Aix-en-Provence.

musical style and influences

Milhaud's compositions are characterized by the use of polytonality and influences from jazz and Brazilian music. He encouraged his students, such as Burt Bacharach, to embrace melody. He was influenced by Arnold Schoenberg, dedicating his Fifth String Quartet to him and conducting premieres of Pierrot lunaire. He also incorporated ideas and idioms from jazz into works like La création du monde.

works and legacy

Milhaud was extremely prolific, with an opus list ending at 443. His work spans a wide range of genres including operas (such as Bolivar and David), ballets (such as Le bøef sur le toit and La création du monde), and orchestral works (including twelve symphonies).

His legacy continues through his students, who include Dave Brubeck, Steve Reich, Philip Glass, Karlheinz Stockhausen, and Iannis Xenakis. There are archival collections of his papers at Mills College, the New York Public Library for the Performing Arts, and Cleveland State University.
