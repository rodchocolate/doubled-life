The Fillmore East was a rock music venue located on Second Avenue near East 6th Street in the Lower East Side of Manhattan, New York City. It was operated by rock promoter Bill Graham from March 8, 1968, to June 27, 1971. The venue served as an East Coast counterpart to Graham's Fillmore Auditorium and Fillmore West in San Francisco.

history and facility

The theater at 105 Second Avenue was originally built as a Yiddish theater in 1925–1926, designed by Harrison Wiseman in the Medieval Revival style. It was first called the Commodore Theater, then became the Loews Commodore movie theater and later the Village Theatre. When Bill Graham took over the unused and dilapidated theater in 1968, it had a capacity of almost 2,700 people despite its small front marquee and façade. After opening as the Fillmore East, it became known as "The Church of Rock and Roll," featuring two-show, triple-bill concerts several nights a week, typically on Friday and Saturday nights at 8 pm and 11 pm.

notable performances

A wide array of rock music acts played the venue, including Jimi Hendrix, The Kinks, Ike & Tina Turner, and Led Zeppelin. The Grateful Dead played a total of 43 concerts at the theater from June 1968 through April 1971, and Jefferson Airplane performed thirty-six shows. The Allman Brothers Band were sometimes called "Bill Graham's house band" due to the frequency of their performances. John Lennon and Yoko Ono sat in with Frank Zappa and the Mothers of Invention on June 6, 1971. Additionally, the Joshua Light Show and later the Pig Light Show became integral parts of the performances, providing psychedelic art lighting.

live album recordings

The venue was a favorite spot for recording live albums due to its excellent acoustics and attentive audiences. The Allman Brothers Band recorded their breakthrough double album, At Fillmore East, which was recorded over two nights in March 1971 (March 12 and 13) and released by Capricorn Records. This album is regarded as one of the greatest live albums of all time and was selected for preservation in the Library of Congress by the National Recording Registry. The Allman Brothers Band also had recordings from February 1970 and February 1971, and later releases like Eat A Peach.
