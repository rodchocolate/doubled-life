The Royal Albert Hall is a concert hall located on the northern edge of South Kensington, London, England. It has a seating capacity of 5,272 and was opened by Queen Victoria in 1871.

overview

The hall is the venue for the BBC Proms concerts, which have been held there every summer since 1941. It hosts more than 390 shows in its main auditorium annually, including rock and pop concerts, classical music, opera, ballet, and charity performances. Additionally, 1,000 events are held each year in non-auditorium spaces. Over its history, the hall has hosted a wide variety of events such as speeches by Albert Einstein and Winston Churchill, meetings of suffragettes, and boxing matches featuring Muhammad Ali and Lennox Lewis.

history

Originally intended to be called the Central Hall of Arts and Sciences, Queen Victoria changed the name to the Royal Albert Hall of Arts and Sciences in 1867 to memorialize her husband, Prince Albert. The hall was designed by civil engineers Major-General Henry Y. D. Scott and Captain Francis Fowke of the Royal Engineers and built by Lucas Brothers. Its construction used Fareham Red brick and terra cotta block decoration. The dome, made of wrought iron and glazed, was designed by Rowland Mason Ordish. The official opening ceremony took place on 29 March 1871.

the grand organ

The hall's Grand Organ, known as 'The Voice of Jupiter', is the second largest pipe organ in the United Kingdom. Originally built by Henry 'Father' Willis in 1871, it has undergone several rebuilds by Harrison & Harrison and Mander Organs. Since its restoration in 2004, it features 9,999 pipes and 147 stops. Anna Lapwood became the hall's first official organist in 2025.
