The Roxy Theatre is a nightclub located on the Sunset Strip in West Hollywood, California. It is currently owned by Lou Adler and his son, Nic.

history

Prior to 1969, the venue was known as the Largo, an upscale burlesque club. The Roxy Theatre opened on September 20, 1973, founded by Elmer Valentine and Lou Adler, along with original partners David Geffen, Elliot Roberts, and Peter Asher. In 1974, it hosted the first American run of the stage play The Rocky Horror Show. In January 2014, Goldenvoice became the exclusive promoter for the theatre, bringing in high-profile acts such as Foo Fighters and U2.

notable performances and recordings

The venue has hosted a wide array of performances and recordings. Neil Young recorded his live album Roxy: Tonight's The Night Live during the first three days of the theatre's opening in 1973. Bruce Springsteen & the E Street Band recorded tracks for their Live/1975-85 album here, and Frank Zappa & The Mothers of Invention recorded most of the Roxy and Elsewhere (1974) album in December 1973. Bob Marley & The Wailers played several shows including a 1976 recording session for Live at the Roxy. The Ramones played their first California concert here on August 11, 1976, and filmed scenes for their movie Rock 'n' Roll High School in December 1978. Genesis played six shows over three days in December 1973 and again in May 1980. Other notable recordings include live albums by The Tragically Hip (1991), NOFX (1995), and Social Distortion (1998). More recent performances include Adele's debut concert tour 'An Evening with Adele' on May 21, 2008.

comedy and other events

The Roxy Theatre has also served as a venue for comedy and residency-style runs. Paul Reubens introduced his Pee-wee Herman character in a revue in 1981, and Tom Eyen's hit comedy Women Behind Bars enjoyed a long extended run. The theatre was the site of the first AIDS benefit held in Los Angeles on July 27, 1983.
