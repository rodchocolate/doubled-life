Keith Jarrett is an American pianist and composer born on May 8, 1945. He has performed as a group leader and solo performer in jazz, classical music, and jazz fusion since the early 1970s. His improvisations are influenced by gospel, blues, ethnic folk music, Western classical music, and jazz traditions.

early life and education

Born in Allentown, Pennsylvania, Jarrett possesses absolute pitch and began piano lessons before his third birthday. At age five, he appeared on a television talent program hosted by Paul Whiteman. By age seven, he performed his first formal recital featuring works by Mozart, Beethoven, Bach, and Saint-Saëns, as well as two original compositions. He attended Emmaus High School in Pennsylvania, where he became proficient in jazz. Although invited to study classical composition in Paris with Nadia Boulanger, he declined the invitation to pursue jazz. After graduating in 1963, he moved to Boston to attend Berklee College of Music and play cocktail piano in local clubs.

professional career

Jarrett's commercial recording debut occurred in 1966 on the album Buttercorn Lady with Art Blakey's The Jazz Messengers. He later joined the Charles Lloyd Quartet, where he recorded the successful 1966 album Forest Flower and toured internationally, including performances in Moscow and Leningrad. In 1968, Jarrett joined Miles Davis's group, playing electronic organ and Rhodes piano, alternating with Chick Corea. Despite a dislike for amplified music, he remained with the group out of respect for Davis and cited him as a vital influence on his thinking about improvisation. He left the group in December 1971 after Jack DeJohnette departed.

group leadership and solo work

Since the early 1970s, Jarrett has led several ensembles, as a composer and improviser. He formed the 'American Quartet' with Charlie Haden, Paul Motian, and Dewey Redman, which recorded over a dozen albums over five years. He also led a 'European' or 'Scandinavian' Quartet featuring Jan Garbarek, Palle Danielsson, and Jon Christensen. He is well known for his solo piano recordings, including the best-selling piano recording in history, The Köln Concert (1975). He also recorded with the Standards Trio consisting of Gary Peacock and Jack DeJohnette from 1983 to 2014. He has expressed a dislike for studio recordings, preferring live performances because he feels the vibe is wrong in studios.

awards and health

In 2003, Jarrett received the Polar Music Prize for both contemporary and classical music, and in 2004, he received the Léonie Sonning Music Prize. In 2008, he was inducted into the DownBeat Jazz Hall of Fame. His career as a performer has ended due to health issues; in February 2018, he suffered a stroke, followed by a second stroke in May 2018 that left him partially paralyzed and unable to play with his left hand.
