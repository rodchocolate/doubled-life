A name is a term used for identification by an external observer. It can identify a single thing uniquely or within a given context, or it can identify a class or category of things.

naming conventions

A naming convention is a set of generally accepted standards, criteria, or stipulated norms for naming things. These conventions are used across various fields such as astronomy, biology (binomial nomenclature), chemistry (chemical nomenclature), and computer programming. Products like automobiles typically follow binomial names consisting of a make and model, while school courses often use an abbreviation for the subject area followed by a number indicating difficulty level. Additionally, many identifiers such as bank accounts, government IDs, and flight numbers have internal structures and conventions.

personal names

A personal name is an identifying word or words used to designate an individual. In many countries, it is traditional for individuals to have a given name (or first name) and a surname (also called a family name). Some people may have two surnames inherited from each parent. Other identifiers include middle names, nicknames, aliases, or titles designating professional roles or authority.

etymology and religious significance

The word 'name' comes from Old English 'nama', which is cognate with Latin 'nomen', Greek 'onoma', and Sanskrit 'naman'. In the ancient near-east, names were thought to be powerful manifestations of a person or deity. This belief led to a reluctance to use the proper name of God in Hebrew writing and speech, and the common understanding that magical rituals had to be carried out 'in [someone's] name'. In the Old Testament, changing a name indicated a change of status, such as when Abram and Sarai were renamed Abraham and Sarah.
