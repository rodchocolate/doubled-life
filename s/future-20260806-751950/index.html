The future is defined as the time that occurs after the past and the present. Its arrival is considered inevitable due to the laws of physics and the existence of time. In a linear conception of time used in the Occidental view, it is the portion of the projected timeline anticipated to occur.

physics and time

In physics, physics treats time as the fourth dimension and spacetime as a stretchy fabric that bends due to forces such as gravity. In classical physics, the future is simply half of the timeline for all observers. However, in special relativity, the flow of time is relative to the observer's frame of reference, meaning the future is no longer an objective notion. Modern notions include absolute future or the future light cone. While many physicists argue that one may only move forward in time, some claim theoretical possibilities for time travel through cosmic strings or wormioholes, with Michio Kaku stating that powering a hypothetical time machine to punch a hole into the fabric of space-time would require the energy of a star.

philosophy and religion

Philosophical views on the future vary. Presentism is the belief that only the present exists, while the past and future are unreal or logical constructions. Eternalism is the opposite, believing things in the past and yet to come exist eternally. The 'growing block' theory suggests the past and present exist, but the future does not. Saint Augustine proposed the present as a knife edge between the past and the future. In religion, the future is addressed through eschatologies, karma, and life after death. Some religious figures, such as diviners and prophets, claim to see into the future, while major prophets are said to have the power to change it.

psychology

Human behavior encompasses the anticipation of the future through psychological outlooks. Optimism is a view of the world as a positive place where most situations work out for the best, while pessimism—the opposite of optimism—is the tendency to emphasize only bad or undesirable outcomes. Hope is an emotional state and belief in a positive outcome despite evidence to the contrary, which differs from optimism, which some theories point to as a conclusion reached through a deliberate thought pattern.

cultural production and study

The concept of the future has been explored in art movements like 20th-century futurism. The science, art, and practice of postulating possible futures is known as futurology or future studies. Modern practitioners of this field stress plural and alternative futures over a monolithic one, emphasizing the creation of possible and preferable futures rather than just prediction and probability.

other uses

The term 'future' is used in various other contexts, including computer science (programming), grammar (future tense and future perfect), and music. For example, the American rapper Future has a self-titled fifth studio album released in 2017, which was certified Platinum by the RIAA and debuted at number one on the Billboard 200.
