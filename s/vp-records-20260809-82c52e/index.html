VP Records is an independent record label based in Queens, New York, specializing in Caribbean music such as reggae, dancehall, soca, and reggaeton. The label is known for releasing music by notable artists in these genres. It has established a presence in Australia and New Zealand and maintains offices in New York City, Miami, London, Kingston, Tokyo, Johannesburg, Rio de Janeiro, and Toronto.

history

The label was founded in 1979 by Vincent "Randy" Chin and Patricia Chin, who owned Studio 17 recording studios and the Randy's Records store in Kingston, Jamaica. The founders moved to New York City in the mid-1970s and opened a record store in Brooklyn in 1975, later relocating it to Jamaica, Queens in 1979. Following the success of the retail store, the record label was expanded in 1993. The name 'VP' is derived from the first letters of the founders' names. After the acquisition of Greensleeves Records in 2008, which included a catalogue of over 12,000 songs, VP became the largest reggae label and publisher in the world. In 2007, the label began re-issuing classic albums from the 1970s and 1980s on the 17 North Parade label.

operations and recognition

VP Records operates as part of the VP Music Group, which owns and distributes four labels: VP Records, Greensleeves Records, Dub Rockers, and 17 North Parade. It also has a subsidiary called VP Associated Label Group (VPAL), which provides independent artists with access to its distribution channel for broader visibility. The label has been featured in publications such as Time magazine, Billboard, and The New York Times. It was awarded Billboard's Best Independent Label for two consecutive years (2002 and 2003) and received the award for Best Reggae Imprint Label for three consecutive years.

artists and releases

The label's artists include Shaggy, Spice, Beres Hammond, Richie Spice, and Christopher Martin. It has released a series of Riddim Driven albums and the compilation series Strictly the Best, which has reached over 50 volumes. The label achieved worldwide success for artists like Sean Paul through deals with Virgin Music Canada and Warner Music Group's Atlantic Records. It is also credited with increasing the popularity of Elephant Man (the 'Energy God'), via a deal with Bad Boy Records. Additionally, producer Kemar McGregor began licensing albums to VP Records in 2006, producing hit singles for albums such as Richie Spice's "Gideon Boot" and Beres Hammond's A Moment in Time.

founders and leadership

Vincent Chin died in 2003, and the label is now run by the Chins' sons, Randy and Christopher, with Patricia Chin continuing to assist in the maintenance of the company.
