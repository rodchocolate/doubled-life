Paul van Dyk is a German DJ, record producer and musician. He was the first artist to receive a Grammy Award nomination for Best Dance/Electronic album with his 2003 release Reflections. By 2008, he had sold over 3 million albums worldwide.

early life and career

Matthias Paul (born 16 December 1971) grew up in East Berlin in a single parent household. He worked as a broadcast technician and trained to be a carpenter before his musical education came from listening to radio stations like Sender Freies Berlin and Radio in the American Sector. His first acquired album was Organisation by Orchestral Manoeuvres in the Dark. Shortly before the fall of the Berlin Wall, he and his mother moved to Hamburg, then back to Berlin in 1990. He began DJing at the club Tresor in March 1991 and later performed at Andre'Hoche's Dubmission parties in the Turbine club. He collaborated with Cosmic Baby as The Visions of Shiva, releasing singles such as "Perfect Day" and "How Much Can You Take?" through the Berlin independent label MFS.

professional success and recognition

Van Dyk achieved early popularity with his remix of Humate's "Love Stimulation" and the hit single "For an Angel". He was voted World No. 1 DJ by DJ Magazine in 2005 and 2006, and was the first DJ to be named number one by Mixmag in 2005. He took a controlling share in Vandit Records in mid-1998. He also hosted the radio show "VONYC Sessions", which reached 1000 episodes on January 4, 2026.

musical works and collaborations

Van Dyk has produced several studio albums including 45 RPM, Seven Ways, Out There and Back, Reflections and In Between. His work with U.S. producer BT produced tracks like "Flaming June" and "Forbidden Fruit". He also collaborated with Saint Etienne for the hit single "Tell Me Why (The Riddle)" and Hemstock & Jennings for "Nothing But You". He won a Mexican Oscar for composing the soundtrack to the film Zurdo. He released several DJ mix albums such as The Politics of Dancing and Vonyc Sessions.
