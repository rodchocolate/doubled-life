The Olympia Brass Band is an American jazz brass band from New Orleans. It has existed in multiple iterations, with a first version active from the late 19th century to around 1914.

history and evolution

The original Olympia Brass Band, known as 'The Olympia', was active from the late 19th century to approximately 1914. In 1958, saxophonist Harold Dejan formed a new version of the band. reorganized as the Olympia Brass Band. The group became a staple at Preservation Hall, where they performed weekly Sunday night gigs for many years and influenced future Preservation Hall musicians. They were also featured in the 1977 dance musical 'Fat Tuesday and All That Jazz!'

performances and media

The band has toured Europe and Africa (for the U.S. State Department), and performed for Pope John Paul II and conducted a BBC Radio broadcast for Queen Elizabeth's 25th wedding anniversary in 1972. The group appeared in the 1973 James Bond movie 'Live and Let Die', where they play a band leading a funeral march; trumpeter Alvin Alcorn plays the 'baby-faced killer'.

personnel and legacy

The band served as a training ground for musicians such as Joseph Torregano, Byron "Flea" Bernard, Tanio Hingle, Kerry Hunter, Kenneth Terry, and "Kid" Mervin Campbell. Notable members over the years included leader Harold "Duke" Dejan, Milton Batiste, Emanuel Paul, and Tuba Fats (Anthony Lacen). The band's history is considered to have ended with the death of grand marshal Richard "King" Matthews in 2010.

discography

The band has left numerous recordings, including 'The Westlawn Funeral Dirge' featuring Emanuel Paul. Their discography includes the recording 'New Orleans Street Parade (1968)' and 'Dejan's Olympia Brass Band of New Orleans' (1974).
