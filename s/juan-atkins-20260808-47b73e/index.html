Juan Atkins is an American record producer and DJ from Detroit, Michigan, known as the original pioneer of Detroit techno. He has been a member of the Belleville Three, Cybotron, and Borderland.

early life and influences

Born on September 12, 1962, in Detroit, Atkins grew up in a musical family with a father who was a concert promoter. He initially played guitar and bass guitar in funk/garage bands before moving to Belleville, Michigan, where he bought his first synthesizer, a Korg MS-10, at age 15. His musical awakening was influenced by George Clinton's Parliament and Funkadelic, as well as Kraftwerk and the radio shows of The Electrifying Mojo. He also drew upon dystopian science fiction narratives and the techno-futurist possibilities described by sociologist Alvin Toffler to construct his sonic landscapes.

career and collaborations

In 1980, Atkins formed Cybotron with Richard Davis. The duo released the studio album Enter in 1983 and singles such as "Korg MS-10" and "Clear". After splitting in 1985, Atkins founded the record label Metroplex, where he began recording solo under the alias Model 500. As Model 500, he released landmark tracks like "No UFO's" (1985), which is frequently described as the first techno record. He later collaborated with Moritz von Oswald to form the duo Borderland, releasing albums such as Borderland (2013) and Transport (2016). He has also worked with Thomas Fehlmann and Moritz von Oswald as 3MB Featuring Magic Juan Atkins.

detroit techno and the belleville three

The birth of Detroit techno was closely associated with the Belleville Three—Juan Atkins, Kevin Saunderson, and Derrick May. These high school friends from Belleville, Michigan, synthesized dance music with mechanical sounds to reflect post-industrialist Detroit. Their work contributed to the discourse of Afrofuturism by repurposing technology to create a new form of music for marginalized underground populations. They also DJed at The Music Institute in downtown Detroit.
