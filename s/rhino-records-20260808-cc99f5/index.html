Rhino Entertainment Company, formerly known as Rhino Records Inc., is an American specialty record label and production company founded in 1978. It currently serves as the catalog division for Warner Music Group.

origins and early history

Rhino began as a record shop on Westwood Boulevard in Los Angeles in 1973, run by Richard Foos, and became a record distributor five years later thanks to the effort of then-store manager Harold Bronson. The label's first single was released in 1975, featuring Wild Man Fischer's "Go to Rhino Records". The company's mascot character Rocky—a cartoon greaser rhinoceros wearing a black leather jacket—was designed by William Stout and later Scott Shaw.

evolution of business model

During the 1970s and 1980s, Rhino was a novelty and reissue label. It released compilation albums of pop, rock & roll, and rhythm & blues successes from the 1950s through the1980s, as well as retrospectives of comedy performers like Spike Jones, Tom Lehrer, Stan Freberg, and Richard Pryor. By the mid-1980s, most releases were re-issues of recordings licensed from other companies. For superior sound quality, audio mastering was done under the direction of Bill Inglot. In the late 1980s, the company transitioned into a complete entertainment company specializing in home video reissues of television programs such as The Monkees and Mystery Science Theater 3000, alongside compact disc releases of movie soundtracks and select artists.

corporate history and ownership

In 1985, Rhino signed a distribution agreement with Capitol Records. In 1989, Rhino and EMI jointly acquired Roulette Records, receiving the U.S. rights to its catalog (excluding jazz). In 1992, Rhino signed a new distribution deal with Atlantic Records, at which time Time Warner bought a 50 percent stake in the company. By 1998, Time Warner became the wholly owned unit of Time Warner. In 2004, Time Warner spun off its music divisions and Rhino is now part of the Warner Music Group. The label's current CEO is Mark Pinkus.

artists and releases

Rhino has reissued material from artists such as the Beach Boys, the Doors, the Ramones, the Bee Gees, and the Grateful Dead. It also produced soundtracks for films like Casablanca, The Wizard of Oz, and Gone with the Wind. In 1999, it started 'Rhino Handmade' for limited-edition releases. While Gary Stewart signed new music artists such as NRBQ and Steve Wynn, these albums typically generated more critical acclaim than public interest, making re-issues the primary revenue stream. One exception was the success of "At This Moment" by Billy Vera and the Beaters.
