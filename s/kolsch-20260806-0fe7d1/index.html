Kölsch refers to several distinct subjects, including a style of beer originating in Cologne, Germany, a Ripuarian dialect spoken in and around the city, and various individuals with the surname Koelsch. It also refers to a house musician/DJ and a US Navy vessel, USS Koelsch.

beer style

Kölsch is a pale gold, bright and clear top-fermenting beer from Cologne. Since 1997, it has held a protected geographical indication (PGI) within the European Union, meaning it must be brewed according to the Kölsch-Konvention as defined by the Cologne Brewery Association. The style is warm fermented with top-fermenting yeast and then conditioned at cold temperatures like a lager. It is traditionally served in a 200-millilitre cylindrical glass called a Stange, which is replaced immediately by a server known as a Köbes who marks a tick on the coaster under the glass.

colognian dialect

Kölsch (or Colognian) is a set of closely related Ripuarian dialects from the Central German group, spoken in an area reaching from Neuss in the north to south of Bonn, west to Düren and east to Olpe. In Cologne, it is actively spoken by approximately 250,000 people. The dialect has been influenced by New High German since the 17th century and French during the Napoleonic occupation between 1794 and 1815. It is characterized by a phonemic pitch accent known as the 'singing' Rhinelandic tone.

history of kölsch beer

Cologne brewers began using a hybridized brewing process—brewing with top-fermenting yeast and aging in cold cellars—around 1750 to compete with bottom-fermented beers. The beer was first called 'Kölsch' in 1918, developed from a cloudier variant called Wieß. By the start of World War II, Cologne had over forty breweries, but only two remained by the end of the war. In 1986, 24 brewers agreed to the Kölsch-Konvention to restrict the use of the name 'Kölsch' for breweries in Cologne and certain authorized breweries outside the city.
