Sonny Rollins is an American jazz tenor saxophonist widely recognized as one of the most influential and important jazz musicians. Over a seven-decade career, he has recorded more than 60 albums as a leader.

early life and education

Walter Theodore "Sonny" Rollins was born on September 7, 1930, in Harlem, New York City. He grew up in a musical family where his father played the clarinet, his sister played piano, and his older brother played violin. His parents, Walter and Valborg, were from the Danish Virgin Islands. Rollins began taking piano lessons at age nine and switched to alto saxophone at eleven after being inspired by Louis Jordan. In 1946, influenced by Coleman Hawkins, he switched to the tenor saxophone, which became his signature instrument. He attended Edward W. Stitt Junior High School and Benjamin Franklin High School in East Harlem.

professional career

Rollins began performing professionally after graduating high school in 1948, making his first recordings in early 1949 as a sideman with Babs Gonzales. He recorded with several jazz legends such as Miles Davis, Thelonious Monk, and Charlie Parker. A significant breakthrough occurred in 1954 when he recorded "Oleo", "Airegin", and "Doxy" with a quintet led by Miles Davis; these compositions became jazz standards. In 1955, Rollins entered the Federal Medical Center, Lexington, to break his heroin habit through experimental methadone therapy. After sobriety, he briefly joined the Miles Davis Quintet and the Clifford Brown–Max Roach quintet.

musical style and key works

Rollins Rollins's signature contributions to jazz include the use of calypso rhythms, often performing traditional Caribbean tunes like "Hold 'Em Joe" and "Don't Stop the Carnival". His 1956 album Saxophone Colossus was recorded at Rudy Van Gelder's studio in New Jersey and includes his best-known composition "St. Thomas". This album was selected for preservation by the National Recording Registry of the Library of Congress in 2016. Other notable works include Tenor Madness, recorded in 1956 with Miles Davis's group, and compositions such as "Strode Rode" and "Moritat".
