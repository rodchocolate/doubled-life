Thelonious Monk was an American jazz pianist and composer known for his unique improvisational style and significant contributions to the standard jazz repertoire. He is recognized as one of five jazz musicians featured on the cover of Time magazine.

musical style and compositions

Monk's playing style combined a highly percussive attack with abrupt, dramatic use of switched key releases, silences, and hesitations. His compositions and improvisations feature angular melodies and dissonances, often utilizing flat fifths, flat ninths, unexpected chromatic notes, low bass notes, stride, and fast whole tone runs. He composed numerous jazz standards, including "'Round Midnight", "Blue Monk", "Straight, No Chaser", "Ruby, My Dear", "In Walked Bud", and "Well, You Needn't". As of 2009, he was the second-most-recorded jazz composer in history after Duke Ellington. He also created the tune "52nd Street Theme", which he claims was the origin of the name 'bebop'.

early life and education

Born on October 10, 1917, in Rocky Mount, North Carolina, Monk began studying the trumpet before switching to piano at age nine. He took lessons from neighbor Alberta Simmons, who taught him the stride style of Fats Waller, James P. Johnson, and Eubie Blake. He attended Stuyvesant High School in Manhattan but did not graduate. For two years, he studied classical piano under Simon Wolf, learning pieces by composers such as Bach, Mozart, Beethoven, Liszt, Chopin, and Rachmaninoff.

career development

In the early to mid-1940s, Monk was the house pianist at Minton's Playhouse in Harlem, where he participated in cutting contests that were crucial to the formulation of bebop. He recorded his first commercial recordings with the Coleman Hawkins Quartet in 1944. Later, in 1947, he met Lorraine Gordon and Alfred Lion, co-founder of Blue Note Records, which led to his first recordings as a leader for the label between 1947 and 1952.
