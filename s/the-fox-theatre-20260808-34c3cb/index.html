The Fox Theatre, often marketed as the Fabulous Fox, is a performing arts venue and former movie palace located at 660 Peachtree Street NE in Midtown Atlanta, Georgia. It serves as the centerpiece of the Fox Theatre Historic District.

The Fox Theatre, often marketed as the Fabulous Fox, is a performing arts venue and former movie palace located at 660 Peachtree Street NE in Midtown Atlanta, Georgia. It serves as the centerpiece of the Fox Theatre Historic District.

architecture

The theater's design is divided into two primary architectural styles: Islamic architecture and Egyptian architecture. The 4,665-seat auditorium replicates an Arabian courtyard with a ceiling featuring 96 embedded crystal "stars," some of which flicker, and a projection of drifting clouds. The Egyptian Ballroom is modeled after a temple for Ramses II at Karnak, while the mezzanine Ladies Lounge features makeup tables with tiny Sphinxes and a replica of King Tut's throne chair. Throughout the building, trompe-l'œil is used extensively; plaster beams appear as wood, paint appears as gold leaf, and a giant Bedouin canopy in the auditorium is made of plaster and steel rods to funnel sound. The Islamic sections include ablution fountains that are currently kept dry.

history

Originally planned as the Yaarab Shrine Temple for a 5,000-member Shriners organization, the project exceeded its budget, leading the Shriners to lease the auditorium to movie mogul William Fox. The theater opened on December 25, 1929, shortly after the stock market crash. By 1932, William Fox was bankrupt and the Yaarab Temple could not meet mortgage payments, causing the venue to close only 125 weeks after opening. Following a foreclosure in December 1932, it was purchased by Paramount Pictures and Lucas & Jenkins. In the 1940s, the theater became one of Atlanta's finest movie theaters and its Egyptian Ballroom became a popular public dance hall for big bands and country and western swing bands.

title

The Fox Theatre was notable in the 1940s for being the only theater in Atlanta allowing both black and white patrons, though it remained segregated by separate box office, entrance, and seating. It was integrated in 1962. In the 1974, Southern Bell attempted to purchase the site to demolish the building for a parking deck. This led to a public outcry and a campaign involving celebrities like Liberace and Lynyrd Skynyrd, who recorded their first live album there. Following this effort, the city refused to issue a demolition permit. The venue was placed on the National Register of Historic Places in May 1974 and later named a National Historic Landmark on May 26, 1976.

current use

The theater currently hosts a variety of cultural and artistic events, including the Atlanta Ballet, national touring companies of Broadway shows, occasional concerts by popular artists, and a summer film series.
