The Preservation Hall Jazz Band is a New Orleans jazz band founded in the early 1960s by tuba player Allan Jaffe. The ensemble derives its name from Preservation Hall, a music venue located in the French Quarter of New Orleans.

origins and early years

Preservation Hall began as an art gallery called Associated Artists managed by Larry Borenstein in the 1950s. To attract customers, Borenstein invited local jazz musicians to play for tips. In May 1961, management was turned over to Ken Grayson Mills and Barbara Reid, who renamed the venue 'Preservation Hall'. Allan Jaffe and his wife Sandra, from Pennsylvania, took over management in September 1961. Jaffe hired local musicians ranging in age from 60s to 90s, many of whom struggled with poverty, racism, and illness. Initially, the Jaffes served no alcohol, used no amplification, and did not advertise. Starting in 1963, Allan Jaffe began touring with bands in the U.S. and other countries, featuring popular figures such as clarinetist George Lewis, pianist Sweet Emma Barrett, trumpeter Kid Thomas Valentine, and others.

evolution of membership

In the 1970s, brothers Percy Humphrey and Willie Humphrey replaced Sweet Emma Barrett as leaders after her health declined. The lineup included musicians such as Frank Demond, James Prevost, James "Sing" Miller, Cie Frazier, Jim Robinson, Narvin Kimball, and Allan Jaffe on tuba. During the late 1960s and early 1970s, many touring members were hired by Harold Dejan for his Olympia Brass Band, which influenced both the Preservation Hall Jazz Band and future musicians. In 1987, Allan Jaffe died of cancer; his son Ben Jaffe, a double bass and tuba player, became artistic director in 1993. By 1999, most older generation musicians had passed away, and the band members were a mixture of younger African-American players and white musicians from overseas, including Michael White (ii), Wendell Brunious, Freddie Lonzo, and Walter Payton, as well the Swedish pianist Lars Edegran and English trumpeter Clive Wilson.

collaborations and recognition

The band has collaborated with musicians from various genres, including a Grammy Award winning album with the Blind Boys of Alabama called Down in New Orleans. They also recorded American Legacies, a collaboration uniting bluegrass music and New Orleans jazz with the Del McCoury band. They made a guest appearance on the Foo Fighters' album Sonic Highways in 2014 and collaborated with the Trey McIntyre Project dance ensemble for contemporary dance works Ma Maison and The Sweeter End. In 2006, the Preservation Hall Jazz Band was awarded the National Medal of Arts, the nation's highest honor for artistic excellence, presented to Benjamin Jaffe and Sandra Jaffe.

crossover and later years

title own: The band continues to tour nationally and internationally. It was led by trumpeter Wendell Brunious, later replaced by his brother John Brunious. In 2018, the documentary A Tuba to Cuba was released. In 2025, their album For Fat Man (with Preservation Brass) was nominated for a Grammy Award for Best Regional Roots Music Album. During their Golden Anniversary from 2011 to 2012, they performed at Carnegie Hall and the New Orleans Jazz & Heritage Festival, and were the first band to perform at both the Newport Jazz Festival and the Newport Folk Festival in the same year.
