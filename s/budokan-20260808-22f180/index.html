The Nippon Budokan is an indoor arena located in Chiyoda, Tokyo, Japan. Originally built for the 1964 Summer Olympics judo competition, it serves as a primary venue for Japanese martial arts and has become one of the world's most outstanding musical performance venues.

history and construction

The Nippon Budokan was completed on September 15, 1964, and opened on October 3 in the presence of Emperor Showa and Empress Kojun. It was built following an initiative by the Diet Members' Association for the Construction of the Martial Arts Hall, which was formed in June 1961 after judo was selected as an official sport for the 1964 Tokyo Summer Olympics. The building is an octagonal structure modeled after Yumedono (Hall of Dreams) in Hōryū-ji in Nara and stands 42 metres high. The arena holds 14,471 people.

martial arts and sporting events

The Budokan's primary purpose is to host Japanese martial arts contests, including annual national championships for judo, kendo, karate, and aikido. It has hosted the 1967 Women's Volleyball World Championship and the 2020 Summer Olympics judo and karate competitions, as well as the 2020 Summer Paralympics judo events. The arena has also been associated with professional wrestling shows from Pro Wrestling Noah and All Japan Pro Wrestling, though regular shows have ceased due to declining audiences. Additionally, a 1976 hybrid rules fight between Muhammad Ali and Antonio Inoki is considered a forerunner to mixed martial arts.

musical performances

The Beatles were the first rock group to perform at the Budokan in June and July 1966. Other notable acts have played there, including ABBA, who held their final live performance there in March 1980. cheap trick at budokan

The first live album by American rock band Cheap Trick is their best-selling recording. Released in Japan on October 8, 1978, and in the US in February 1979 through Epic Records, it became a high-selling success that kickstarted the band's popularity. In the U.S., it peaked at number four on the Billboard 200 and sold over three million copies. The album was selected by the Library of Congress for preservation in the United States National Recording Registry in 2019.
