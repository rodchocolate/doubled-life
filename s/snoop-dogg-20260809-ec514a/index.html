Snoop Dogg is an American rapper, singer, record producer, songwriter, and actor known as a key figure in West Coast hip-hop. He helped define gangsta rap and G-funk, often regarded as one of the greatest rappers of all time. His lyrics frequently address social issues such as gun violence and recreational drug use.

early life and musical beginnings

Born Calvin Cordozar Broadus Jr. on October 20, 1971, in Long Beach, California, Snoop Dogg was raised Baptist and began singing and playing piano at Golgotha Trinity Baptist Church. In sixth grade, he began rapping in school hallways, which drew large crowds. After graduating from Long Beach Polytechnic High School in 1989, he was arrested for possession of cocaine. Together with cousins Nate Dogg and Lil' ½ Dead, and friend Warren G, he recorded homemade tapes and a four-song demo at VIP Records store. A mixtape featuring one of his early solo freestyles over "Hold On" by En Vogue was eventually heard by producer Dr. Dre, who called him to audition for Death Row Records in 1991.

music career and commercial success

Snoop Dogg rose to prominence in 1992 through collaborations with Dr. Dre on the single "Deep Cover" and the album The Chronic, including the track "Nuthin' but a 'G' Thang". His debut album, Doggystyle (1993), debuted at number one on the Billboard 200, selling 806,000 copies in its first week and spawning hit singles such as "Gin and Juice" and "What's My Name?". Following his departure from Death Row Records, he signed with Master P's No Limit Records, releasing albums like Da Game Is to Be Sold, Not to Be Told (1998) and No Limit Top Dogg (1999). In 2004, the single "Drop It Like It's Hot" featuring Pharrell became his first Billboard Hot 100 number one. He later adopted the alias Snoop Lion for a reggae album, Reincarnated (2013), and released gospel album Bible of Love (2018).

accolades and other ventures

Snoop Dogg has sold over 35 million records worldwide and has received seventeen Grammy Award nominations, two Sports Emmy Awards, and a star on the Hollywood Walk of Fame. In 2022, he co-headlined the Super Bowl LVI halftime show, for which he earned a Primetime Emmy Award. Outside of music, he he has appeared in films such as Training Day and television series like The L Word, and served as a coach on The Voice.
