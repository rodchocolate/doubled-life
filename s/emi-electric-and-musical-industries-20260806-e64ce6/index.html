EMI (Electric and Musical Industries) was a British transnational conglomerate founded in March 1931 in London. It was once one of the "Big Four" record companies and the fourth largest business group and record label conglomerate in the music industry at the time of its acquisition by Universal Music in 2012.

electronics research and development

EMI's research teams developed several technological advancements in broadcasting and medical imaging. In 1934, a team led by Sir Isaac Shoenberg developed the electronic Marconi-EMI system for television broadcasting. EMI also manufactured broadcast television cameras, including the most commercially successful EMI 2001 colour television camera. In the early 1970s, electrical engineer Godfrey Hounsfield developed the first CT scanner with financial support from EMI and the UK Department of Health and Social Security. For this invention, Hounsfield won the Nobel Prize in 1979, and EMI was awarded a Queen's Award for Technological Innovation in 1973. Additionally, EMI engineer Alan Blumlein received a patent for stereophonic sound in 1931, and during World War II, EMI Laboratories in Hayes developed radar equipment, microwave devices, and guided missiles employing analogue computers.

music industry influence

EMI's music operations spanned globally, with the same as well as branch offices in many countries. In 1931, the company opened the legendary Abbey Road recording studios in London. During the 1930s and 1940s, its roster of artists included Otto Klemperer, Sir Edward Elgar, and Arturo Toscanini.
