Eric Clapton is an English rock and blues guitarist, singer, and songwriter who is regarded as one of the most successful and influential guitarists in rock music. He has sold 100 million records worldwide and is a three-time inductee to the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame.

early life and education

Born on 30 March 1945 in Ripley, Surrey, England, Clapton grew up believing his grandmother Rose and her second husband Jack Clapp were his parents. He received an acoustic Hoyer guitar for his thirteenth birthday, though he briefly lost interest before returning to play consistently two years later. Influenced by blues music from an early age, he practiced long hours playing along to records using a portable Grundig reel-to-reel tape recorder. After leaving Hollyfield School in Surbiton, he studied at the Kingston College of Art but was expelled at the end of the academic year because his focus remained on music rather than art.

musical career and band memberships

Clapton's professional career began with performances as a duo with Dave Brock in pubs around Surrey, followed by by joining the Roosters from January to August 1963. He then joined the Yardbirds from 1963 to 1965, where he developed a distinctive style synthesizing Chicago blues and influences from guitarists like B.B. King and Freddie King. Following this, he joined John Mayall & the Bluesbreakers from 1965 to 1966. He later formed the power trio Cream with Jack Bruce and Ginger Baker, which lasted until November 1968. Subsequent bands included Blind Faith (with Baker, Steve Winwood, and Ric Grech) and Derek and the Dominos, who recorded the album Layla and Other Assorted Love Songs.

solo career and notable works

Clapton has released numerous solo albums, including his self-titled debut in 1970, a 1974 cover of Bob Marley's "I Shot the Sheriff", and the country-infused Slowhand (1977). His 1992 live album Unplugged, recorded at Bray Studios for MTV, became the best-selling live album of all time and sold 26 million copies worldwide. This album features the song "Tears in Heaven", written as an expression of grief following the death of his son Conor in 1991. His latest studio album, Meanwhile, was released in 2024.

accolades and philanthropy

Clapton has received 18 Grammy Awards and the Brit Award for Outstanding Contribution to Music. He is the only three-time inductee to the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame, having been inducted as a solo artist and as a member of both Cream and the Yardbirds. In addition to receiving four Ivor Novello Awards and a CBE for services to music in 2004, he has sold 100 million records worldwide. In 1998, recovering from addiction himself, he founded the Crossroads Centre on Antigua, a medical facility for those recovering from substance abuse.
