Funk is a music genre that originated in African-American communities in the mid-1960s. It was created as a rhythmic, danceable new form of music through a mixture of various genres popular among African-Americans in the mid-20th century.

origins and history

Funk originated in the mid-1960s with James Brown's development of a signature groove that emphasized the downbeat, known as "The One," along with syncopation on guitar riffs, drum patterns, and basslines. In the late 1960s, musicians such as Parliament-Funkadelic, Sly and the Family Stone, and The Chambers Brothers introduced more eclectic examples of the genre. During the 1970s and 1980s, other groups including Earth, Wind & Fire, Kool and the Gang, Chic, and Bootsy Collins further developed these innovations.

musical characteristics

The genre deemphasizes melody and chord progressions to focus on a strong rhythmic groove created by an electric bassist and percussionist. Funk typically features complex percussive grooves with interlocking rhythm instruments that create a hypnotic, danceable feel, often at slower tempos than other popular music. It utilizes richly colored extended chords similar to those found in bebop jazz, such as dominant ninth chords and minor eleventh chords.

etymology

The term 'funk' originally referred to a strong odor, derived from the Latin fumigare. In African communities, however, the term had a positive connotation, associating a musician's physical exertion and sweat with an exquisite performance. In early jam sessions, musicians would encourage one another to "get down" or "put some stank on it."

derivatives and influence

Funk has produced several derivatives including G-funk, a mix of psychedelic funk and gangsta rap; boogie, a hybrid of electronic music and funk; and others such as funk metal, avant-funk, and Timba. Additionally, funk samples and breakbeats have been used extensively in electronic dance music and hip-hop.

facts

  • Origin: African-American communities, mid-1960s
  • Rhythmic Focus: The One (emphasis on the first beat of every measure)
  • es: Slower, sexier and more syncopated than disco
  • Key Figures: James Brown, Parliament-Funkadelic, Sly and the Family Stone
  • Etymology: Derived from Latin fumigare meaning 'to smoke'
