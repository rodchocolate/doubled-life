Stax Records is an American record company originally based in Memphis, Tennessee. Founded in 1957 as Satellite Records, it became influential in the creation of Southern soul and Memphis soul music.

origins and name change

Stax was founded in 1957 by Jim Stewart, initially operating out of a garage. In 1958, his sister Estelle Axton became a financial partner, investing $2,500 to purchase an Ampex 350 mono console tape recorder. The label was originally named Satellite Records and focused on country music, rockabilly, and pop. By 1960, the uma-integrated team of racially integrated staff and artists worked from a converted garage near National Cemetery in Brunswick, Tennessee, before moving to the former Capitol Theatre at 926 East McLemore Avenue in South Memphis. The name was changed to Stax Records in September 1961 after a conflict with another company called Satellite Records in California. The name 'Stax' is a portmanteau of Stewart and Axton.

musical style and influence

The label's sound was based on black gospel, country, and earlier forms of rhythm and blues (R&B), which became known as southern soul music. This identifiable sound was created by using one studio, one equipment set-up, and a small group of songwriters and musicians, including the house band Booker T. & the M.G.'s. Stax also released gospel, funk, and blues recordings, and featured ethnically integrated bands and staff during a time of racial strife in Memphis and the South.

growth and decline

Following the death of Otis Redding in 1967 and the severance of its distribution deal with Atlantic Records in 1968, the label was expanded by co-owner Al Bell. To compete with Motown Records, Bell expanded operations significantly over five years. However, in the mid-1970s, a problematic distribution deal with CBS Records caused the label to slide into insolvency and forced its closure in late 1975. In 1977, Fantasy Records acquired the post-1968 catalogue, and by the early 1980s, it became strictly a reissue label. After Concord Records acquired Fantasy in 2004, the Stax label was reactivated to issue both new recordings and catalog material.

associated artists and media

Stax Records featured artists such as Otis Redding, Isaac Hayes, The Staple Singers, Sam & Dave, and the house band Booker T. & the M.G.'s. It also had a sister label, Volt Records, and an offshoot called We Produce Records, which signed the soul vocal trio The Temprees in 1970. A documentary series titled 'Stax: Soulsville U.S.A.' was produced by Jamila Wignot for HBO, winning a Peabody Award.
