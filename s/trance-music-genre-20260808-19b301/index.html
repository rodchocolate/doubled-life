Trance music is a genre of electronic dance music that emerged from the techno and EBM scene in Frankfurt, Germany, in the late 1980s and early 1990s. It is typically characterized by a tempo between 120 and 150 beats per minute (BPM) and repeating melodic phrases.

Trance music is a genre of electronic dance music that emerged from the techno and EBM scene in Frankfurt, Germany, in the late 1980s and early 1990s. It is typically characterized by a tempo between 120 and 150 beats per minute (BPM) and repeating melodic phrases.

musical characteristics

Trance music often utilizes a musical form that builds tension and elements throughout a track, culminating in one to two peaks or drops. It incorporates influences from classical music, film scores, house, techno, chill-out, ambient, and tech house. Modern trance typically features a mid-song climax followed by a soft breakdown where beats and percussion are removed entirely, leaving the melody or atmospherics to stand alone before building up again. Tracks are often lengthy to allow for this progression and have sparse opening and closing sections to facilitate DJ mixing. While mostly instrumental, vocals can be mixed in, typically performed by mezzo-soprano to soprano female soloists without a traditional verse/chorus structure. Structured vocal form is the basis of the vocal trance subgenre, described as 'grand, soaring, and operatic'. Male singers such as Jonathan Mendelsohn are also featured.

history and development

The genre's early years were defined by Frankfurt labels like Eye Q, Harthouse, and Force Inc. Producers such as Rolf Ellmer, Oliver Lieb, and Pete Namlook created noteworthy tracks. Much of the development was traced to Sven Väth, who was influenced by his experiences in Goa, India, where DJs used psychedelic rock and other sounds to induce trance states at beach parties. Väth launched Eye Q in 1991 and Harthouse in 1992. In Berlin, MFS Records signed artists like Paul van Dyk, Cosmic Baby, and Mijk van Dijk. In the UK, labels such as Platipus Records and Hooj Choons were influential in progressive trance. In Australia, Christopher J. Dolan (Quench) released 'Dreams' in 1993, which sold over a million copies worldwide by October 2000.

subgenres

Various subgenres emerged from the same roots. Goa trance, with an organic sound and oriental aesthetic, emerged as German trance moved back to Goa. This evolved into psychedelic trance (psytrance), characterized by high tempo riffs and a layered melodies. Psytrance tempos generally range from 125 to 150 BPM and use a a constant pounding bass beat. Subgenres of psytrance include dark psytrance, which is heavier with tempos starting at 150 bpm and often samples horror films; full-on, high energy for peak moments with tempos usually between 140–148 bpm; and Suomisaundi, originating in Finland during the mid-1990s. Other trance subgenres mentioned include hard trance, which introduced the breakdown-build-anthem template, and epic trance, influenced by classical and film music.
