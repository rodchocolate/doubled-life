The Jazz Messengers were a jazz combo that existed for over thirty-five years starting in the early 1950s. The group was led or co-led by founding drummer Art Blakey until his death in 1990.

overview

The Jazz Messengers served as a proving ground for young jazz talent, with each iteration of the group typically including new young players. Having the group on one's resume was considered a rite of passage in the jazz world and provided immediate bona fides. Many former members established careers as solo musicians, including Lee Morgan, Wayne Shorter, Freddie Hubbard, Wynton Marsalis, and Keith Jarrett. Some members, such as Gregory Charles Royal and Clarence Seay, played in the group but did not record with them.

history

The group's origins trace back to 1947 when Art Blakey led 'Art Blakey's Messengers' for a Blue Note Records session. He also led a financially unstable big band called the 'Seventeen Messengers' around 1947 or 1949. The Jazz Messengers formed as a collective in 1954 or 1955, nominally led by Horace Silver or Blakey. This original group disbanded in 1956, but Blakey retained the name for future iterations. A 'second' lineup formed in 1956-1958, which included Jackie McLean and later evolved into a more stable version of the group. By 1958, a lineup featuring Lee Morgan, Benny Golson, Bobby Timmons, and Jymie Merritt began what is described as perhaps the most fruitful period of the Jazz Messengers.

discography and labels

The group's discography includes 47 studio albums, 21 live albums, 6 compilations, 2 soundtracks, and one boxed set. They recorded for various labels including Blue Note Records, Columbia Records, RCA's Vik Records, Pacific Jazz, Elektra, Cadillac, Jubilee, Bethlehem, Atlantic, Impulse! Records, Riverside Records, Limelight Records, Mercury's sub-label, Prestige Records, Sonet Records, Roulette Records, Timeless Records and Concord Jazz. In 1958, they returned to Blue Note for the album Moanin', which became one of the archetypal hard bop albums of the era. The group did not release recordings between 1966 and 1972, except for a 1970 release on Catalyst.

findings

The album Moanin' is noted for its combination of gospel and blues influences with modern jazz sensibility. It features the track 'Moanin',' composed by Bobby Timmons, and originals by Benny Golson such as 'Along Came Betty' and 'Blues March'. The recording was produced by Alfred Lion and engineered by Rudy Van Gelder in his Hackensack studios.
