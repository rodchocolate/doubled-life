The Grand Ole Opry House is the venue where the Grand Ole Opry radio broadcast has been based since 1974. It is located east of downtown Nashville, Tennessee, and serves as the home for the longest-running radio broadcast in U.S. history.

The Grand Ole Opry House is the venue where the Grand Ole Opry radio broadcast has been based since 1974. It is located east of downtown Nashville, Tennessee, and serves as the home for the longest-running radio broadcast in U.S. history.

overview

The Grand Ole Opry House hosts a regular live country-music radio broadcast that occurs between two and five nights per week. The program showcases a mix of contemporary chart-toppers, famous singers, and comedic performances or skits, performing genres such as gospel, Americana, folk, Bluegrass, and country music. It attracts millions of internet and radio listeners and hundreds of thousands of visitors from around the world.

venue history

The Opry has moved through several venues before settling in its current home. In October 1934, it moved to the Hillsboro Theatre (now the Belcourt), the Dixie Tabernacle in East Nashville on June 13, 1936, and then to the War Memorial Auditorium. In June 1943, it moved to the Ryman Auditorium. Since 1974, the show has been broadcast from the Grand Ole Opry House, though it maintains winter residencies at the Ryman Auditorium (from 1999 to 2020 and again starting in 2023).

membership

Membership in the Grand Ole Opry is considered one of country music's crowning achievements. Just over 225 acts have been members throughout its history, with approximately 75 members currently active as of 2024. The Grand Ole Opry House maintains a member gallery backstage featuring an engraved brass nameplate for every act that has ever been a member.
