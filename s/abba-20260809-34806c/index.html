ABBA is a Swedish pop music group formed in Stockholm in 1972 by Agnetha Fältskog, Björn Ulvaeus, and Benny Andersson and Anni-Frid Lyngstad. They are among the most renowned and commercially successful musical groups in history.

overview

ABBA is one of the best-selling music acts of all time, with sales exceeding 150 million. They were the first group from a non-English-speaking country to achieve consistent success on the charts in English-speaking countries such as Canada, Ireland, New Zealand, South Africa, the United States, Australia, and the United Kingdom. The group achieved eight consecutive number-one albums in the UK and recorded hit songs in Spanish for Latin American audiences. Their compilation album ABBA Gold is certified platinum over 128 times across more than 28 countries/territories worldwide as of April 2026 and has been in the UK top 100 album chart for a record 1,248 weeks.

history

The group formed in 1972 from members who had already established solo or group careers. Agnetha Fältskog was a talented singer/songwriter of schlager-style songs with several successful singles on the Swedish charts. Björn Ulvaeus began his career as a singer and guitarist for the Hootenanny Singers, while Benny Andersson was a member of the Hep Stars, known as 'the Swedish Beatles'. In 1974, ABBA won the Eurovision Song Contest for Sweden with the song 'Waterloo', which was later chosen as the best song in the competition's history during its 50th anniversary celebration in 2005. The group comprised two married couples: Fältskog and Ulvaeus, and Lyngstad and Andersson. However, as their fame grew, their personal lives suffered, leading to the dissolution of both marriages. These changes were reflected in later music with darker and more introspective lyrics.

tours and performances

ABBA performed sporadically over eight years since forming. They embarked on a European & Australian Tour in 1977 and a North American and European Tour from 1979 to 1980, which supported the album Voulez-Vous and later Greatest Hits Vol. 2. The tour closed in Tokyo, Japan, at the Nippon Budokan in March 1980, which was ABBA's final live performance before unofficially disbanding in late 1982. Following their breakup, Andersson and Ulvaeus continued writing music for films, musicals, and stage, while Fältskog and Lyngstad pursued solo careers. In 2016, the group reunited and began working on a digital avatar concert tour. This led to the release of the album Voyage in November 2021 and the opening of the ABBA Voyage residency in London in May 2022, which features virtual avatars of the group.

legacy and media

The group's music was adapted into the global stage musical Mamma Mia!, which toured globally and remains one of the ten longest-running productions on Broadway (closed 2015) and the West End (still running as of October 2024). A film version of Mamma Mia! released in 2008 became the uma highest-grossing film in the United Kingdom that year. A sequel, Mamma Mia! Here We Go Again, was released in 2018. In May 2023, ABBA received the BRIT Billion Award for surpassing one billion UK streams.
