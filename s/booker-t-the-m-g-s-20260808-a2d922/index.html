Booker T. & the M.G.'s were an American instrumental, R&B, and jazz band formed in Memphis, Tennessee, in 1962. The group helped shape the sound of Southern soul and Memphis soul, becoming one of the most prolific and imitated bands of its era. They were one of the first racially integrated rock groups during a time when the Memphis music scene was generally considered the preserve of Black culture.

formation and early years

The band formed as the house band for Stax Records, consisting of original members Booker T. Jones (organ, piano), Steve Cropper (guitar), Lewie Steinberg (bass), and Al Jackson Jr. (drums). In 1962, while backing Billy Lee Riley in a Memphis studio, the group began jamming with a bluesy organ riff that was recorded by Stax president Jim Stewart. This led to the release of their hit single "Green Onions", which reached number 1 on the US Billboard R&B chart and number 3 on the pop chart, selling over one million copies. The band continued to issue instrumental singles and albums throughout the uma 1960s, including the album Green Onions and later Soul Dressing in 1965.

role as house band

As members of the Mar-Keys, the rotating slate of musicians that served as the house band of Stax Records, the M.G.'s played on hundreds of recordings for artists such as Otis Redding, Wilson Pickett, and Bill Withers. They are considered to have originated much of the sound of soul music—specifically Southern soul—where "the groove" is paramount. Along with Motown's Funk Brothers, they set a standard for soul music, and Stax Records was known as "Soulsville U.S.A." while Detroit's Motown Records was known as "Hitsville U.S.A."

title

In 1965, Lewie Steinberg was replaced by Donald "Duck" Dunn, who remained with the group until his death in 2012. Al Jackson Jr. was murdered in 1975, after which the band reunited on various occasions using different drummers such as Steve Jordan and Anton Fig. Booker T. Jones eventually moved to California in 1970, leaving Stax due to frustration with how the group was treated as employees rather than musicians. The band was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1992, and later into the Musicians Hall of Fame and Museum (2008), the Memphis Music Hall of Fame (2012), and the Blues Hall of Fame (2019).
