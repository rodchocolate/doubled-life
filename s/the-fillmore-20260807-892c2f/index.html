The Fillmore refers to both a historical neighborhood in San Francisco and a historic music venue located on the edge of that district and the Upper Fillmore neighborhood.

fillmore district

The Fillmore District is a historical neighborhood in San Francisco, generally treated as either a synonym or subneighborhood of the Western Addition. It rose to prominence after the 1906 earthquake because it was not affected by the earthquake or subsequent fires. This led to an influx of diverse ethnic populations, including African Americans, Jews, and Japanese residents. The district was known for having the largest jazz scene on the west coast of the United States until its decline in the 1970s. However, redevelopment programs in the late 1960s and 1970s led to the forced removal of African American residents and a decline in the jazz scene, a process some call 'Negro Removal'. City planners claim this was done to combat crime and reinvigorate the economy.

the fillmore music venue

Built in 1912 as Majestic Hall, the venue became the Fillmore Auditorium in 1954 when it was taken over by Charles Sullivan, an African-American businessman who introduced integrated dances and major Black music acts. Following Sullivan's unsolved murder in 1966, Bill Graham took over management. Under Graham, the venue became a focal point for psychedelic music and counterculture, hosting acts like the Grateful Dead, Jimi Hendrix, and Pink Floyd. The building was damaged by the Loma Prieta earthquake of 1989 and closed, but it was reopened in 1994 after structural retrofitting. Since 2007, it has been operated by Live Nation.

title history and other venues

The venue's name changed several times before becoming the Fillmore Auditorium; it was known as the Majestic Hall, Ambassador Dance Hall, and Ambassador Roller Skating Rink. For a period from 1969 to 1970, it was operated as the New Old Fillmore. In the early 1980s, it became The Elite Club, hosting punk rock shows. As a result of its cultural impact, Bill Graham expanded the concept to other locations including Fillmore West and Fillmore East in New York City. Live Nation has since further expanded the brand into clubs in cities such as Denver, Detroit, Philadelphia, and Miami.
