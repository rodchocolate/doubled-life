Jungle is a genre of electronic music that emerged from the UK rave scene and Jamaican sound system culture in the 1990s. It developed out of breakbeat hardcore and is characterized by rapid breakbeats, syncopated percussive loops, and deep basslines.

origins and characteristics

Jungle music's foundations were laid by tracks like "We Are I.E." by Lennie De Ice, which featured a ragga bassline. The genre emerged as the breakbeat hardcore scene fragmented in the early 1990s, incorporating influences from dub, reggae, dancehall, hip-hop, and funk. Producers frequently sampled the "Amen break" or other breakbeats from jazz and funk recordings. Musically, it is described as a postmodern dub music on steroids with spatialised production and bass quake pressure.

rise and popularity

The genre reached its peak popularity between 1994 and 1995, achieving UK top 40 hits such as "Incredible" by M-Beat featuring General Levy. During this period, it was proliferated by pirate radio stations like Kool FM—regarded as the most instrumental station in the development of the jungle scene—as well as Don FM, Rush and Rude FM. Legal stations like Kiss 100 and BBC Radio 1 also began providing platforms for the genre from 1994 and 1995 respectively.

subgenres and evolution

Ragga jungle, a fusion of jungle and heavy reggae influence, became a major subgenre during 1994 and 1995. Jump-up emerged in 1995 from hardstep, focusing on sound experiments with the bass line. By 1996 and 1997, the sound shifted toward a darker, grittier soundscape with more hip-hop and jazz influences, which instrumentalized the transition of jungle into drum and bass by the end of 1998.
