Berkshire Hathaway Inc. is an American multinational conglomerate holding company based in Omaha, Nebraska. Originally a textile manufacturer, it transitioned into a conglomerate under the management of Warren Buffett and Charlie Munger. Greg Abel succeeded Buffett as CEO at the start of 2026.

company overview

Berkshire Hathaway is ranked 5th on the Fortune 500 and 9th on the Fortune Global 500. It is one of the ten largest components of the S&P 500 and among the largest employers in the United States. The company's Class A shares have the highest per-share price of any public company globally, reaching $700,000 in August 2024 due to the board's historical opposition to stock splits. As of March 31, 2026, the company held $373.5 billion in cash, cash equivalents and short-term investments in U.S. treasury bills.

history and evolution

The company originated as a textile manufacturer formed in 1955 from the merger of Hathaway Manufacturing Company and Berkshire Fine Spinning Associates. Warren Buffett began buying stock in 1962, eventually taking control of the company in 1965 to replace manager Seabury Stanton. Buffett has described this acquisition as his biggest investment mistake, noting that it had denied him compounded returns of approximately $200 billion over 45 years. The last textile operations were shut down in 1985.

acquisitions and subsidiaries

Under Buffett's control, Berkshire Hathaway expanded into insurance and other businesses through acquisitions such as National Indemnity Company (1967), See's Candies (1972), and Nebraska Furniture Mart (1983). The company also had significant investments in GEICO (1976) and a diverse portfolio of media properties, including the Omaha World-Herald and The Buffalo News. Additionally, Berkshire Hathaway Energy (BHE), a wholly owned subsidiary headquartered in Des Moines, Iowa, operates utility and energy businesses across the U.S., Canada, and the UK, owning companies such as PacifiCorp and NV Energy.
