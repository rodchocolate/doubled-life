Everyone thinks Takashi Murakami's masterpieces painting.

Most artists, simple images,

Murakami does that too. But his real genius is that he samples systems.

He collects and recombines management processes

the exact same way other artists might collect visual references.

His studio isn't just where the art gets made.

The studio is the art. In his own words,

maybe my path as an individual painter was a vocation,

The painting was the calling he was handed.

The machine, the hundred hands,

the coordination, the group effort.

That was the part he actually love.

So the management isn't the boring part

It's the actual creative act,

deciding what can be made,

who makes it, how it gets paid for,

how it gets explained, where it ends up living.

Most artists make an object and hope the world understands it.

Murakami builds the conditions that make understanding unavoidable.

He's not making artworks. He's designing a machine that makes them.
