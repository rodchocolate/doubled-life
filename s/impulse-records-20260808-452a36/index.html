Impulse! Records is an American jazz record label established by Creed Taylor in 1960. The label became known as "the house that Trane built" due to the consistent sales and positive critiques of recordings by John Coltrane.

history and leadership

Impulse! was established in 1960 as a jazz subsidiary of Am-Par Record Corporation, which was part of the parent company ABC-Paramount Records. Creed Taylor served as the initial producer and A&R manager; he chose the name "Pulse" but added a prefix after discovering another label already used that name. Taylor left the label in 1961 to become the head of Verve Records. He was succeeded by Bob Thiele, who produced most of the albums in the 1960s and remained with the label until 1969. Thiele's departure was partly due to a breakdown in his relationship with Larry Newton, president of ABC Records.

visual design

The label's albums are noted for their visual appeal, featuring a black, orange, and white livery devised by Fran Attaway. The logo featured the Impulse! name in a heavy, sans-serif, lower-cased font with an exclamation mark mirroring the lower-case "i". For most of the 1960s, this logo was placed in an orange letters in a white circle. In 1968, this was replaced by a one-color design featuring the Impulse! logo and ABC Records logo side by side. Album covers often used glossy laminated stock with large-format photographs or paintings that bled to the edges of the cover. Many of these were designed by art director Robert Flynn and photographed by individuals such as Pete Turner, Arnold Newman, and Joe Alper.

artists and releases

Early success was achieved through signings like Ray Charles, whose album Genius + Soul = Jazz provided the first hit. Other early releases included Out of the Cool by Gil Evans and The Blues and the Abstract Truth by Oliver Nelson. Under Bob Thiele, Impulse! became a key outlet for free jazz and movements spearheaded by John Coltrane, McCoy Tyner, Archie Shepp, and Freddie Hubbard. Notable recordings also include collaborations between Coltrane and Duke Ellington or Coleman Hawkins, and works by Charles Mingus. The 1965 LP A Love Supreme by John Coltrane sold over 100,000 copies on its first release and more than half a million by 1970.
