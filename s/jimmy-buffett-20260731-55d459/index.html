Jimmy Buffett own an American singer-songwriter, author, and businessperson who was known for his tropical music sound blending country, rock, folk, and calypso. He promoted a lifestyle of "island escapism" and recorded many hit songs, including "Margaritaville".

early life and education

Jimmy Buffett was born on December 25, 1946, in Pascagoula, Mississippi. He was the son of Mary Lorraine and James Delaney Buffett Jr., a marine engineer and sailor who worked for the United States Army Corps of Engineers. Influenced by his father and grandfather—a steamship captain from Newfoundland—Buffett was exposed to sailing as a child. He was educated by Jesuits as a Catholic and served as an altar boy. In 1961, after seeing a folk music ensemble perform in Biloxi, Mississippi, Buffett realized he wanted to be a musician. After attending St. Ignatius Catholic School in Mobile, where he played trombone in the school band, he graduated from McGill Institute in 1964. He later enrolled at Auburn University, but flunked out after a year in April 1966 due to his interest in music and girls. He eventually earned a bachelor's degree in history in 1969 from the University of Southern Mississippi after attending Pearl River Community College. During college, he worked as an electrician and welder in a shipyard.

music career

After graduating in 1969, Buffett moved to New Orleans, where he performed for tourists on Decatur Street and at the Bayou Room nightclub. In 1970, he moved to Nashville to pursue a country music career, where he worked as an editorial assistant for Billboard. He signed a two-album contract with Barnaby Records, releasing his first album, Down to Earth, in August 1970. After an impromptu audition, he was hired as the opening act for Dianne Davidson at the Exit/In club in Nashville. In November 1971, he moved to Key West, Florida, where he became involved in the literary scene and worked as a first mate on a yacht. As part of terms of a recording contract with ABC/Dunhill Records in 1973, he was promoted as a replacement for Jim Croce. After releasing A White Sport Coat and a Pink Crustacean in June 1973, he formed the Coral Reefer Band in 1975. He recorded several hit songs known as "The Big 8", which include "Margaritaville" (1977), "Come Monday" (1974), "Fins" (1979), "Volcano" (1979), "A Pirate Looks at Forty" (1974), "Cheeseburger in Paradise" (1978), "Why Don't We Get Drunk" (1973), and "Changes in Latitudes, Changes in Attitudes" (1977). He released over 30 albums, with eight certified gold and nine certified platinum or multi-platinum by the RIAA. He was posthumously inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2024 in the Musical Excellence category.

business ventures

Buffett parlayed his "island escapism" lifestyle into several business ventures, including hotels, casinos, liquor, retirement communities, and a bestselling author career. He founded the Jimmy Buffett's Margaritaville restaurant chain, and the now-defunct Cheeseburger in Paradise restaurant chain. The brand is owned by Margaritaville Holdings LLC. The Margaritaville hospitality company manages and franchises casual dining American restaurants, retail stores selling merchandise, and themed hotels. This includes various locations across the United States, Canada, Mexico, and the Caribbean, as well as Sydney, Australia. Specific ventures include Landshark Bar & Grill, Air Margaritaville airport lounge restaurants, and vacation clubs in Puerto Rico and Rio Grande, St. Thomas, Virgin Islands. He also partnered with Outback Steakhouse to develop the first Cheeseburger in Paradise restaurant in 2002.
