ECM Records (Edition of Contemporary Music) is an independent record label founded in Munich in 1969 by Manfred Eicher, Karl Egger, and Manfred Scheffner. While primarily known for jazz music, the label has released a variety of recordings across different genres.

history and production

The first ECM release was Mal Waldron's 1969 recording Free at Last, produced by Manfred Scheffner. Manfred Eicher continues to act as the producer for the vast majority of its recordings, with other producers including Steve Lake, Robert Hurwitz, and Sun Chung. A typical ECM session consists of three days: two for recording and one for mixing. Many albums have been recorded with sound engineer Jan Erik Kongshaug in Oslo, Norway.

the new series

Created in 1984 to document Western classical works, the ECM New Series focuses on composers from early music (such as Thomas Tallis and Carlo Gesualdo) to contemporary figures like John Cage and Steve Reich. The series was initiated for Arvo Pärt's record debut Tabula Rasa. Since then, a strong relationship has developed between Eicher and Pärt, where all of the same composer's works premiere on recordings for the label and are recorded in his presence.

genre blending and distribution

ECM's artists often refuse to acknowledge boundaries between genres, combining jazz and classical music. For example, Jan Garbarek's Officium (1994) features saxophone solos over Gregorian chant. In 2017, ECM announced that its entire catalog would be made available for streaming on platforms such as Spotify, Apple Music and Amazon to combat unauthorized streaming and piracy, though physical recordings remain the preferred medium.
