Luciano 'Chano' Pozo González was a Cuban jazz percussionist, singer, dancer, and composer who played a major role in the founding of Latin jazz. Despite living to only 33 years old, he is credited with shaping Latin music. He was the first Latin percussionist in Dizzy Gillespie's band.

early life and santería

Born in Havana to Cecelio González and Carnación Pozo, Chano Pozo grew up in poverty. After his mother died when he was 11, he lived with his father's mistress, Natalia. He showed an early interest in drums and performed in Afro-Cuban religious ceremonies. While living in the Africa neighborhood (El África Solar), a former slave quarters, he became involved in petty criminal activities and spent time in a youth reformatory in Guanajay, where he learned reading, writing, and auto body repair while honing his drumming skills. During this time, he became a devotee of Santería (La Regla de Ocha), an Afro-Caribbean religion derived from Yoruba beliefs of Nigeria. He pledged allegiance to the Catholic Saint Barbara/Shango, the god of fire and thunder, often carrying a red scarf to signify this allegiance. After his release, he worked as a bootblack and later as a newspaper seller for El País, which led to him being hired as a personal driver and bodyguard for businessman Alfredo Suárez.

musical career in cuba

Pozo became well-known in Havana for his compositions for Carnival, where he was a sought-after rumbero. He led his own comparsa (street troupe) щотen winning top cash prizes for his compositions. In 1940, he and fellow musicians wrote 'La Comparsa de los Dandys', a conga music composition that earned first prize in the city of Santiago de Cuba's carnival and is considered an unofficial theme song of Santiago de Cuba. He also performed as a dancer in a troupe known as The Dandy. To overcome racial marginalization, he worked as a bouncer for the radio station Azul, where he used his imposing size to keep crowds in check while befriending musicians who worked there.

career in new york and latin jazz

In 1947, encouraged by Mario Bauza and Miguelito Valdés, Pozo immigrated to the United States. He was introduced to Dizzy Gillespie, who was looking for a conga player. The collaborations between Gillespie and Pozo brought Afro-Cuban rhythms and instruments like the bongo and tumbadora into the East Coast jazz scene. They co-wrote Latin-flavored compositions such as 'Manteca' and 'Tin Tin Deo'. Their work contributed to the early form of Latin jazz known as 'Cubop' (Cuban bebop), a mix of Afro-Cuban clave-based rhythms with jazz harmonies and improvisation techniques.
