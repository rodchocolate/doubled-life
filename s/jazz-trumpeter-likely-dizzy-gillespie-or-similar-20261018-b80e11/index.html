Dizzy Gillespie is a bebop trumpeter who collaborated with percussionist Chano Pozo in 1947 to bring Afro-Cuban rhythms and instruments like the bongo and tumbadora into the East Coast jazz scene. He participated in unrehearsed jam sessions with the Machito orchestra between 1948 and 1949 at venues such as Birdland, Bop City, and Royal Roots.

afro-cuban jazz influence

Dizzy Gillespie's work in 1947 collaborations with percussionist Chano Pozo brought Afro-Cuban Afro-Cuban rhythms and instruments, such as the tumbadora and the bongo, the East Coast jazz scene. Early combinations of jazz with Cuban jazz music, such as 'Manteca' and 'Mangó Mangüé', were commonly referred to as 'Cubop' for Cuban bebop.

collaborations and jam sessions

Between 1948 and 1949, Dizzy Gillespie sat in with the Machito orchestra, sitting in with Charlie Parker, Howard McGhee, and tenor saxophonist Brew Moore. These sessions were unrehearsed and uninhibited jam sessions that were described by master of ceremonies Symphony Sid as Afro-Cuban jazz.
