Devo is an American new wave band from Akron, Ohio, formed in 1973. The group's classic line-up included drummer Alan Myers and two sets of brothers: the Mothersbaughs (vocalist Mark and lead guitarist Bob) and the Casales (bassist Gerald and rhythm guitarist Bob). They are best known for their 1980 single "Whip It".

concept and formation

The band's name is derived from the concept of "de-evolution," a tongue-in-cheek social theory suggesting that mankind has begun to regress rather than evolve, evidenced by the dysfunction of American society. This idea was developed as a joke by Kent State University art students Gerald Casale and Bob Lewis in the late 1960s. The formation of the band became serious following the May 4, 1970, Kent State massacre. Early work was marked by experimental and dissonant art punk merging rock music with electronics, while their output in the 1980s shifted toward synth-pop and a more mainstream style.

early career and recording

Devo's first form was the "Sextet Devo," which performed at the 1973 Kent State performing arts festival. After several fluid lineups, they secured a recording contract with Warner Bros. in 1978 following recommendations from David Bowie and Iggy Pop. Their debut studio album, Q: Are We Not Men? A: We Are Devo!, was produced by Brian Eno and recorded primarily in Cologne, West Germany. The album peaked at No. 12 in the UK and No. 78 in the US. They followed this with Duty Now for the Future in 1979, which moved the band toward electronic instrumentation.

commercial success and later years

The band's highest selling album was Freedom of Choice (1980), which went Platinum in the US and Gold in Canada. Their 1981 follow-up, New Traditionalists, peaked at No. 23 on American charts. After a period of decline in chart success, they released their apparently final studio album, Smooth Noodle Maps, in 1990. From 1990 through 2009, no new albums under the Devo name were released, until Something for Everybody was released on June 15, 2010.

influence and style

Devo's music and visual presentation—including costumes and stage shows—combine kitsch science fiction themes, deadpan surrealist humor, and satirical social commentary. The band was a pioneer of the music video format, with Gerald Casale being particularly enthusiastic about this. Their music has influenced subsequent movements such as industrial, alternative rock, and new wave, as well as an internet microgenre known as "devo-core".
