Jeff Buckley was an American musician who released one studio album, Grace, in 1994. He gained a following in the East Village of Manhattan during the early 1990s performing at venues like Sin-é.

early life and musical influences

Born in Anaheim, California, Buckley was the only son of Mary and singer-songwriter Tim Buckley. He was raised by his mother and stepfather, Ron Moorhead, and was known as Scott "Scottie" Moorhead as a child. Buckley began playing guitar at age five and decided to become a musician at age 12. His musical influences included Led Zeppelin, Queen, Jimi Hendrix, the Who, and Pink Floyd, as well as progressive rock bands Rush, Genesis, and Yes, and jazz fusion guitarist Al Di Meola. He also admired Joni Mitchell, the Smiths and Siouxsie and the Banshees. After graduating high school, he attended the Musicians Institute in Hollywood to study music theory.

career development

In Los Angeles, Buckley spent six years playing guitar in various bands across styles from jazz, reggae, and roots rock to heavy metal. He toured with dancehall reggae artist Shinehead and collaborated with producer Michael J. Clouse to form X-Factor Productions. From 1988 to 1989, he played in a band called the Wild Blue Yonder, which included future Tool member Danny Carey. He moved to New York City in February 1990, where he became an impassioned fan of Nusrat Fateh Ali Khan's Qawwali music. He later performed at a tribute concert for his father, Greetings from Tim Buckley, in April 1991, which served as his public singing debut.

grace and recording process

Buckley's only studio album, Grace, was released in 1994 by Columbia Records. Produced by Buckley and Andy Wallace, it was recorded at Bearsville Studios in Woodstock, New York. The album includes covers of the jazz standard "Lilac Wine", the hymn "Corpus Christi Carol" and Leonard Cohen's "Hallelujah". Buckley experimented extensively with arrangements and recording techniques, though he found theen recording process contrary to his improvisational performance style. The album's cover photograph, taken by Merri Cyr, depicts Buckley in a women's sequinned jacket from a thrift store.
