Ronnie Scott's Jazz Club is a jazz club located in Soho, London, that has been operating since 1959. It was co-founded and managed by musicians Ronnie Scott and Pete King.

history

The club opened on 30 October 1959 at 39 Gerrard Street in London's Soho district. In 1965, it moved to a larger venue at 47 Frith Street, while the original location continued as the 'Old Place' until its lease expired in 1967. Ronnie Scott served as the club's Master of Ceremonies and was known for his repertoire of jokes and one-liners. Following Scott's death in 1996, Pete King managed the club for another nine years before selling it to theatre impresario Sally Greene and philanthropist Michael Watt in June 2005.

house musicians and record label

The club employed house musicians to accompany visiting soloists or members of larger bands. House drummers have included Phil Seamen, Allan Ganley, Tony Oxley, Martin Drew, Mark Fletcher, Chris Dagley, Pedro Segundo, and Chris Higginbottom. House pianists include Eddie Thompson, Stan Tracey, and John Critchinson. House bassists include Sam Burgess, and house guitarists include Ernest Ranglin. The club also established its own record label, Ronnie Scott's Jazz House, in 1978, which released live performances from the club and new recordings.

birmingham offshoot

A franchised offshoot of the club was opened at 258 Broad Street in Birmingham in October 1991. This venue hosted notable musicians and bands such as Coldplay, Robert Fripp, Charlie Watts, Scotty Moore, Helen Shapiro, and Kenny Baker's Dozen. The Birmingham location closed on 2 February 2002 after facing mounting debts of §1.5m and declining footfall.
