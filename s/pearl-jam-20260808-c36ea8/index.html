Pearl Jam is an American rock band formed in Seattle, Washington, in 1990. As one of the key bands in the grunge movement of the early 1990s, they are considered one of the most influential bands of that decade.

formation and early history

Pearl Jam was formed after the dissolution of Stone Gossard and Jeff Ament's previous bands, Green River and Mother Love Bone. Following the death of vocalist Andrew Wood in 1990, Gossard began practicing with guitarist Mike McCready. The trio then recorded a five-song instrumental demo tape to find a singer and drummer. This demo was passed to Eddie Vedder, who recorded vocals for three songs and was flown to Seattle for an audition. With the addition of Dave Krusen on drums, the band initially took the name Mookie Blaylock before signing to Epic Records and renaming themselves Pearl Jam.

commercial success and discography

The band's debut album, Ten (1991), became one of the highest-selling rock albums ever, being certified 13× platinum by the RIAA. Their second album, Vs. (1993), set a record for most copies sold in its first week of release at the time, and their third album, Vitalogy (1994), became the second-fastest-selling CD in history at that time. By 2012, Pearl Jam had sold more than 100 million albums worldwide. The band's discography includes 12 studio albums, 23 live albums and 3 compilation albums. Their most recent studio album mentioned is Gigaton (2020).

industry practices and advocacy

Pearl Jam members often avoided popular music industry practices such as interviews and music videos. In 1994, the band unsuccessfully sued Ticketmaster for monopolizing the concert-ticket market. The band and vocalist Eddie Vedder have also promoted social and political issues, including opposition to George W. Bush's presidency and abortion rights sentiments.
