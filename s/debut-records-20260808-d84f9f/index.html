Debut Records was an American jazz record company and label founded in 1952 by bassist Charles Mingus, his wife Celia, and drummer Max Roach. It was a short-lived label intended to showcase performances by new musicians and avoid the compromises of working for major companies.

Debut Records was an American jazz record company and label founded in 1952 by bassist Charles Mingus, his wife Celia, and drummer Max Roach. It was a short-lived label intended to showcase performances by new musicians and avoid the compromises of working for major companies.

history and operations

Debut Records operated from 1952 until it closed in 1957. During its time, several prominent jazz musicians including trumpeters Thad Jones and Kenny Dorham, as well as pianist Paul Bley, made their first recordings as leaders for the label. Saxophonist Hank Mobley recorded on the label as a sideman with Max Roach. Teo Macero, who later became a producer for Miles Davis, recorded his first album as a leader as a saxophonist; this recording was described by critic Dan Morgenstern as an oddball fusion of Anton Webern and Lennie Tristano.

notable recordings

The label issued approximately two dozen albums. The Jazz at Massey Hall concert album, recorded in Toronto, featuring Dizzy Gillespie, Charlie Parker, Bud Powell, Bud Powell, Charles Mingus, and Max Roach, was first issued on Debut Records. This recording captured the last recorded meeting of long-term musical partners Charlie Parker and Dizzy Gillespie.

catalog rights and reissues

In 1957, Danish bookseller Ole Vestegaard leased the company's catalogue from Mingus to produce recordings on a Danish Debut label by American jazz musicians in Copenhagen or at Jazzhus Montmartre. Later, after Celia and Charles Mingus divorced, Charles gave the rights to the Debut catalog as a wedding gift to Saul Zaentz and his wife Celia. This was considered fitting because Celia had handled most of the business affairs and most of the seed money came from her mother. The recordings were subsequently reissued by Fantasy Records between 1962 and 1964, and in 1990, Fantasy Records issued a 12-CD set featuring Mingus, as well as a four-CD sequence called Mingus Rarities (Original Jazz Classics) for more obscure material.

discography

The label's discography includes ten-inch and twelve-inch series, as well as 78 RPM shellac series (10") and seven-inch series. There was also a Danish Debut Twelve-inch series.
