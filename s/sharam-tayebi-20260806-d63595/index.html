Sharam Tayebi, known professionally as Sharam, is an Iranian techno and house DJ and producer. Born in Tehran, Iran, he emigrated to Washington D.C. at age 14, where he became a mainstay of the underground dance music scene.

early life and career beginnings

Sharam Tayebi was born in Iran and settled in Washington D.C. with his family at age 14. In this city, he discovered dance music and developed his skills as a musician, DJ, and producer. During the 1990s, he met Ali "Dubfire" Shirazinia, another Iranian immigrant, and together they formed the duo Deep Dish in 1992. Their sound was characterized by a mix of deep house and techno with influences from rock and industrial music. Together, they established the eponymous record label Deep Dish and Yoshitoshi Records, and released their music through the Tribal America label after meeting Danny Tenaglia.

deep dish era

As part of Deep Dish, Sharam and Dubfire released two albums: Penetrate Deeper (1995) and Junk Science (1998). The duo's work was highly acclaimed, winning a Grammy Award for Best Remixed Recording for their remix of Dido's "Thank You" and receiving nominations for the album George Is On (2005), including Best Electronic Album. They were known for the house or dance remixes of tracks by artists such as Madonna, Janet Jackson, Cher, Stevie Nicks, and Gabrielle. They released six DJ mix compilations between 1999 and 2003, and their final album together before a hiatus, George Is On, featured the single "Flashdance," which reached number 3 in the UK.

solo career

Following a split from Dubfire in 2006, Sharam launched his solo career with the Global Underground mix compilation Dubai GU29. He gained significant success with his cover of Eddie Murphy's "Party All the Time," released as "P.A.T.T." featuring P. Diddy, which peaked at number 8 in the UK charts. His solo albums include Get Wild (2009), a concept album inspired by Sergio Leone spaghetti westerns, and Retroactive (2016), which paid homage to his musical history. He also released the techno-influenced album Collecti (2017). Other notable solo works include the mix compilation Night and Day (2012) and singles such as "She Came Along" featuring Kid Cudi, and tracks from his Mach EP series including "M.I.T.T." with Shakira and "Fun" written by Chris Martin of Coldplay.

reunions and recent work

In 2014, Sharam and Dubfire reunited as Deep Dish, releasing the single "Quincy" and performing at the Ultra Music Festival. After another hiatus, they announced a permanent return to touring and the studio in 2024 with "The Future" tour. In 2025, they released their first original single in over a decade, "Midnight," featuring Eynka and Wrabel, accompanied by several sold-out residencies in London at venues such as fabric and The Cause.
