Elektra Records (or Elektra Entertainment) is an American record label owned by Warner Music Group. Founded in 1950 by Jac Holzman and Paul Rickolt, it played a significant role in the development of contemporary folk and rock music from the 1950s to the 1970s.

pop and psychedelic rock

Elektra's entry into pop music began with a short-lived joint venture called Bounty Records in 1965. After Bounty folded, the Paul Butterfield Band was moved to Elektra. Between 1966 and 1967, the label gained prestige by signing leading acts from the American psychedelic rock wave, including The Doors, Love, the Chicago-based Paul Butterfield Blues Band, and Detroit bands MC5 and The Stooges. Other signings included Tim Buckley and Bread. In 1968, rock guitar soloist Lonnie Mack was signed to a three-album deal.

modern era

In 2004, Elektra was consolidated into WMG's Atlantic Records Group. After five years of dormancy, it was revived as an imprint of Atlantic in 2009. In October 2018, it was reorganized into Elektra Music Group as an independently managed frontline label of Warner Music. In June 2022, Elektra Music Group merged with 300 Entertainment to create 300 Elektra Entertainment (3EE), and in October 2024, 300 Elektra Entertainment merged with Atlantic Music Group.
