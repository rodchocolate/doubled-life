UK garage (UKG) is a genre of electronic music that originated in England during the early to mid-1990s. It is characterized by syncopated hi-hats, cymbals, and snares with shuffled rhythms and percussive rhythms. The genre typically features a tempo around 130 BPM and often uses pitch-shifted or time-stretched vocal samples.

origins and influences

UK garage emerged in London during the early 1990s from a blend of New York garage house, R&B, Jamaican ragga and dancehall music, and jungle. It was influenced by rave culture, gospel, Baptist churches, the Black diaspora, and the pirate radio scene. According to MJ Cole, London's multicultural nature as a 'melting pot of young people' reflected in the music. In the UK, garage was initially played in second rooms at jungle events, providing a more soulful and sensual sound at 130bpm compared to the same-time popular 170bpm jungle basslines. This led to the creation of the 'Sunday Scene,' as promoters could only hire venues on Sunday evenings because Friday and Saturday nights were reserved for faster, more popular jungle and drum and bass events.

evolution and subgenres

In 1994, DJ EZ started playing American DJ Todd Edwards' garage house tracks at a speedier 130bpm to match the tempo of UK hardcore and jungle music, which raised the visibility of garage house and created what became known as speed garage. Speed garage was characterized by reverse-warped basslines, rolling snares, and a sped-up house-style beat. By 1997, Armand van Helden's remix of 'Spin Spin Sugar' helped popularize the genre into the mainstream. As British producers began creating original tracks incorporating rave music and elements of Black British and Caribbean music, they called their work 'UK garage'. The sound evolved further into 2-step garage, which added contemporary R&B-styled vocals and more shuffled beats, and most radically changed by removing the second and fourth bass kick from each bar. 2-step garage became a mainstream success in 1999 with platinum status anthems like 'Sweet Like Chocolate' by Shanks & Bigfoot and 'Re-Rewind' by Artful Dodger.

trajectory and legacy

The genre experienced a decline in the mid-2000s, during which it was largely subsumed into other styles of music production such as dubstep, grime, and bassline. This period also saw the birth of closely related UK funky. The 2010s witnessed a resurgence of the genre, and by the early 2020s, a revival known as 'new UK garage' or 'NUKG'.

key figures and venues

Early promoters of the sound include DJ EZ, Tuff Jam and Dreem Teem. Pirate radio stations such as Freek FM, Magic FM, Upfront FM and London Underground were influential in the promoter's role. Pirate radio stations like Ice FM, Rinse FM, and Flex FM and Déjà Vu also provided continued support. Clubs such as Fabric, Ministry of Sound, The Colosseum, Scala and The Gass Club became associated with garage. Additionally, venues like Heaven in Charing Cross and the Frog & Nightgown were early sites for the the same scene.
