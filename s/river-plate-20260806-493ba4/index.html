The River Plate refers to both a professional sports club based in Buenos Aires, Argentina, and an estuary formed by the confluence of the Uruguay and Paraná rivers. The estuary is located on the southeastern coastline of South America and forms part of the border between Argentina and Uruguay.

club atlético river plate

Club Atlético River Plate (CARP) is a professional sports club founded in 1901 in the Belgrano neighborhood of Buenos Aires. The club is named after the English-language name for the city's estuary, Río de la Plata. It is widely regarded as the most successful professional club among Argentina's traditional 'Big Five' of Argentine football. Its home stadium, the Monumental, is the largest in South America. As a multi-sport club, it is best known for its association football department, which has won the Primera División championship 38 times and holds a record of 54 domestic titles total. Internationally, the club has won 18 titles, including four Copa Libertadores and one Intercontinental Cup. The club maintains a fierce rivalry with Boca Juniors, matches between whom are known as the Superclásico.

río de la plata estuary

The Río de la Plata (or River Plate) is an estuary that empties into the Atlantic Ocean and forms a funnel-shaped indentation on the South American coastline. Depending on the geographer, it may be considered a river, river, a gulf or a marginal sea; if viewed as a river, it is the widest in the world with a maximum width of 220 kilometres. It begins at the confluence of the Uruguay and Paraná rivers at Punta Gorda and flows eastward into the South Atlantic Ocean. The estuary forms part of the border between Argentina and Uruguay, and its coasts are among the most densely populated areas of both countries.

geography and hydrology

The Río de la Plata's drainage basin is one of the largest in the world, covering approximately 3.17 million to 3.18 million square kilometres across southeastern Bolivia, southern and central Brazil, Paraguay, and northern Argentina and Uruguay. The estuary behaves as a salt wedge estuary where denser saltwater penetrates below freshwater from the main tributaries, the Paraná and Uruguay rivers. A submerged shoal called the Barra del Indio divides the river into an inner freshwater riverine portion and an outer brackish estuarine portion.

history of the estuary

The region was inhabited by nomadic aboriginal people for thousands of years before European exploration. The Portuguese first explored the Río de la Plata in 1512–13, followed by the Spanish in 1516 when Juan Díaz de Solís called it the 'Mar Dulce'. Later, explorer Sebastian Cabot gave the river its modern name after acquiring silver trinkets from the Guaraní near Asunción. The English name 'River Plate' was adopted using the archaic term 'plate' meaning silver.
