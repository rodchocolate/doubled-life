Sir George Henry Martin was an English record producer, musician, composer, and conductor who is widely known as the "fifth Beatle" for his extensive involvement in the Beatles' original albums. His career spanned over sixty years across film, television, music, and live performance.

early life and education

Born on 3 January 1926 in North London to Henry and Bertha Beatrice Martin, George Martin grew up in Highbury and Drayton Park. He began taking piano lessons at age eight, though they ended quickly due to a disagreement between his mother and the teacher; however, he continued learning independently through his natural perfect pitch. After attending several Roman Catholic schools and Bromley Grammar School, Martin volunteered for the Fleet Air Arm of the Royal Navy during World War II, where he adopted an upper-class accent and gentlemanly demeanour. Following the war, he used a veteran's grant to study piano, oboe, and music composition and orchestration at the Guildhall School of Music and Drama from 1947 to 1950.

career at emi and parlophone

Martin joined EMI in November 1950 as an assistant to Oscar Preuss, head of the Parlophone label. He eventually took over the label in April 1955 when Preuss retired. As head of Parlophone, Martin transformed the label from a "sad little company" into a highly profitable business by recording classical and Baroque music, jazz, and regional music, as well as original cast recordings. He also capitalized on the 1956 skiffle boom by signing the Vipers Skiffle Group. Additionally, Martin produced numerous comedy and novelty records, working with artists such as Peter Sellers, Spike Milligan, Bernard Cribbins, and Peter Ustinov.

work with the beatles

George Martin's formal musical expertise and interest in novel recording practices helped the Beatles facilitate their desire for new sounds. He wrote most of the group's orchestral and string arrangements and played piano or keyboards on several records. Their collaborations resulted in innovative, highly acclaimed records, such as the 1967 album Sgt. Pepper's Lonely Hearts Club Band. AllMusic has described him as the "world's most famous record producer," and he produced 30 number-one hit singles in the UK and 23 in the US, winning six Grammy Awards.
