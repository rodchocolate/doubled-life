DMX, born Earl Simmons, was an influential American rapper, songwriter, and actor who lived from 1970 to 2021. He is recognized as one of the greats of hip-hop with a music style characterized by aggressive rapping and lyrics focusing on hardcore themes or personal trauma.

early life

Earl Simmons was born in Mount Vernon, New York, to Arnett Simmons and Joe Barker. As the middle child, he experienced physical violence from his mother and was diagnosed with bronchial asthma. He was raised as a Jehovah's Witness but later disassociated himself from the spirituality after an accident involving a driver. At age five, his mother sent him to live with relatives in Yonkers' School Street housing apartments. After being expelled from middle school at ten and spending time in a group home, he returned to Yonkers at fifteen, where he slept in empty storage bins and befriended stray dogs. To survive, he robbed students for food and clothing and eventually began carjacking.

musical career

Simmons' career began in 1985 when he beatboxed for a local rapper named Ready Ron. He later adopted the name 'DMX', inspired by the Oberheim DMX drum machine, which he initially defined as 'Divine Master Of the Unknown' and later as 'Darkman X'. After serving a two-year prison sentence, he began writing lyrics and performing for children at a local recreation center. While imprisoned for carjacking in 1988, he developed a 'Spellbound' style of rapping. He eventually signed with Ruff Ryders Entertainment and Def Jam Recordings. Between 1998 and 2003, DMX released five consecutive number one albums on the Billboard 200, including It's Dark and Hell Is Hot (1998), Flesh of My Flesh, Blood of My Blood (1998), ... And Then There Was X (1999), The Great Depression (2001), and Grand Champ (2003).

acting and other works

DMX acted in several commercially successful films, such as Belly (1998), Romeo Must Die (2000), Exit Wounds (2001), and Cradle 2 the Grave (2003). In 2006, he starred in a reality television series called DMX: Soul of a Man on the BET cable network. He also published an autobiography titled E.A.R.L.: The Autobiography of DMX in 2003.
