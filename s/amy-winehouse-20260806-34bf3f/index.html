Amy Winehouse was a British singer, songwriter, musician, and businesswoman known for her distinctive contralto vocals and an eclectic blend of soul, rhythm and blues, and jazz. Her music, fashion, and highly publicised personal life made her an influential figure in popular culture.

early life and education

Born to a Jewish family in Enfield, London, on 14 September 1983, Winehouse was raised in Southgate and attended Osidge Primary School and later Ashmole School. As a child, she attended a weekly cheder, though she grew up in a지 jazz-influenced household with parents Mitchell "Mitch" Winehouse and Janis Winehouse. Her father, an amateur singer and taxi driver, and her paternal grandmother, Cynthia, who had been a singer and dated jazz saxophonist Ronnie Scott, nurtured her early interest in jazz. In 1992, she attended the Susi Earnshaw Theatre School and later the Sylvia Young Theatre School, and briefly went to the BRIT School in Selhurst, Croydon. She also attended the Mount School in Mill Hill. After experimenting with her brother's guitar, she purchased her own at age 15 and began writing music a year later. Before her professional career, she worked as an entertainment journalist for the World Entertainment News Network and was a vocalist for the National Youth Jazz Orchestra.

musical career

Winehouse's professional career began in 2002 when she signed with Simon Fuller's 19 Management. After being kept as a recording industry secret, she was signed to Island Records in 2002 by A&R representative Darcus Beese. She worked with producer Salaam Remi for her debut studio album, Frank (2003), which received critical acclaim for its mature songwriting and jazz-imbued production. The album earned her an Ivor Novello Award for the lead single "Stronger Than Me".

Inspired by 1960s girl groups and soul music, Winehouse recorded her second studio album, Back to Black (2006), with Remi, Mark Ronson and the Dap-Kings. This album became an international critical and commercial success and one of the best-selling albums of all time, selling over 20 million copies worldwide. later life and legacy

Winehouse's career was often overshadowed by substance abuse, mental illness, and addiction, culminating in her death from alcohol poisoning. Her brother Alex believed bulimia was also a factor. In 2015, the documentary Amy was released to depict her life and career, and in 2024, her story was dramatised in the biopic Back to Black. In 2023, Rolling Stone included her on its list of the 200 Greatest Singers of All Time. Additionally, the album Back to Black was preserved in the National Recording Registry by the Library of Congress in 2025.
