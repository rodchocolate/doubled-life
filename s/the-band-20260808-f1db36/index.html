The Band was a Canadian-American rock band formed in Toronto, Ontario. The group's music combined elements of country, jazz, R&B, folk, and Americana. Their sound influenced artists such as Wilco, Eric Clapton, Elton John, and George Harrison.

early history and the hawks

Between 1958 and 1963, the group were known as the Hawks and served as the backing band for Toronto-based rockabilly singer Ronnie Hawkins. During this time, they developed their instrumental skills through all-night rehearsals. Members included Levon Helm, Robbie Robertson, Rick Danko, Richard Manuel, and Garth Hudson. Stan Szelest was also a member during this period. The group eventually split from Ronnie Hawkins over personal differences and heavy-handed leadership, including fines for smoking marijuana or bringing girlfriends to clubs.

collaboration with bob dylan

In late summer 1965, the group (then known as Levon and the Hawks) were hired by Bob Dylan as his backing band for his first U.S. 'electric' tour in 1965 and world tour in 1966. Following the tour, they moved to Saugerties, New York, with help from Dylan and manager Albert Grossman. There, they made the informal 1967 recordings that became The Basement Tapes. This period of collaboration led to the group adopting the official name 'The Band' starting in 1968.

career and later years

The Band's debut album, Music from Big Pink (1968), and their eponymous 1969 album, The Band, received critical acclaim and commercial success. Their most popular songs include "The Weight" (1968) and "Up on Cripple Creek" (1969). They released several other albums including Stage Fright (1970), Cahoots (1971), Rock of Ages (1972), Moondog Matinee (1973), and Northern Lights – Southern Cross (1975). The group performed a farewell concert on November 25, 1976, which was released as the film The Last Waltz directed by Martin Scorsese. After a reunion tour in 1983 without Robbie Robertson, Danko, Hudson, Helm, and Manuel reunited. The band broke up for good after Rick Danko's death in 1999.

facts

  • Formed in: Toronto, Ontario
  • Members: Rick Danko, Garth Hudson, Richard Manuel, and Robbie Robertson (Canadian), Levon Helm (American)
  • Inductions: Canadian Music Hall of Fame (1989) and Rock and Roll Hall of Fame (1994)
  • Rolling Stone Ranking: 50th on the 100 Greatest Artists of All Time list (2004)
