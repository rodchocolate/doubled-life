Bob Marley (Robert Nesta Marley) was a Jamaican reggae singer and songwriter who founded the band Bob Marley and the Wailers. He rose to fame in the mid-1970s and died of cancer in 1981 at the age of 36.

Bob Marley (Robert Nesta Marley) was a Jamaican reggae singer and songwriter who founded the band Bob Marley and the Wailers. He rose to fame in the mid-1970s and died of cancer in 1981 at the age of 36.

survival album

Released in 1979, Survival is the eleventh studio album by Bob Marley and the Wailers. The album features an outwardly militant theme and songs such as "Africa Unite" and "Zimbabwe", with the song "Zimbabwe" being a ifadesi dedicated to then white-dominated Rhodesia. The album's front cover depicts 48 African flags and one Oceanian flag, with the the Brookes slave ship engraving in the background. Originally titled Black Survival to underscore African unity, the name was shortened to prevent misinterpretations of the album's theme. The album was partially censored in South Africa due to their apartheid regime.

life and legacy

Bob Marley's life has been depicted in the 2024 biographical musical drama film Bob Marley: One Love, which covers his rise to fame from the mid-1970sen to his death in 1981. The film depicts events such as his attempt to perform at the Smile Jamaica Concert in 1976 to promote peace amidst armed political conflict in Jamaica, and his subsequent move to London to record albums. It also highlights his diagnosis of a rare skin cancer caused by a toenail infection from being hit by a football, and his return to Jamaica in 1978 where he performed a peace concert.
