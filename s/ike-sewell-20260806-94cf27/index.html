Ike Sewell was an American entrepreneur and businessman who founded the Uno Chicago Grill restaurant chain. He is best known for founding Pizzeria Uno in 1943, which introduced the first Chicago deep dish pizza.

early life and athletic career

Sewell was born in Wills Point, Texas, and grew up in San Antonio. He attended the University of Texas at Austin, where he played football as a guard on the Longhorn football squad from 1926 to 1929, achieving All-Southwest Conference and All-American honors.

restaurant industry

In 1943, Sewell opened Pizzeria Uno at the corner of Ohio Street and Wabash Avenue in Chicago. Although he had originally intended to open a Mexican restaurant because there were no decent options in the city at the time, he instead offered the first Chicago deep dish pizza. Along with Ric Riccardo, he expanded his business by opening Pizzeria Due in 1955 and later opened Su Casa, an upscale Tex-Mex restaurant, in 1963 (or 1965).

franchising and later years

In 1978, Sewell licensed the name and concept of his restaurants to the Uno Restaurant Holdings Corporation in the Boston area. The chain began franchising in 1980. Following his death on August 20, 1990, at Northwestern Memorial Hospital in Chicago after a lengthy bout with leukemia, he was survived by his wife Florence Davis, children, and grandchildren.
