Theodore "Fats" Navarro was an American jazz trumpet player and a pioneer of the bebop style of jazz improvisation in the 1940s. Born in Key West, Florida, he toured with big bands before achieving fame as a bebop trumpeter in New York City.

early life and education

Navarro was of Cuban, African, and Chinese descent and spoke Spanish as a second language. He began playing piano at age six, though he did not become serious about music until he started playing the trumpet at thirteen. He also mastered the tenor saxophone and played both instruments professionally early in his career. After graduating from Frederick Douglass School in 1941, he moved to Orlando to join Sol Allbright's band, touring the Midwest before taking lessons.

professional career

Navarro joined Snookum Russell's territory band and later played in the big bands of Andy Kirk, Benny Goodman, and Lionel Hampton. He also joined Billy Eckstine's band, which included several bebop musicians. In 1946, he settled in New York City, where his career progressed. He participated in recording sessions with figures such as Kenny Clarke, Bud Powell, and Tadd Dameron. He won the Metronome Jazz Poll in 1948 and joined the Metronome Jazz All-Stars for a studio session. By the late 1940s, some jazz musicians refused to play with him because they feared being overshadowed by his skill.

later years and death

Navarro became ill with tuberculosis and developed a heroin addiction and weight problems. His narcotics use accelerated the progression of the disease. He was hospitalized on July 1, 1950, and died on July 7, 1950, at the age of 26 in New York City. He was survived by his wife Rena and daughter Linda. He was buried in an unmarked grave at Rose Hill Cemetery in Linden, New Jersey.
