Lorraine Gordon (1922–2018) was an American jazz music advocate and author of a memoir on jazz music. She owned and managed the Village Vanguard jazz club in New York City.

early life and career

Gordon grew up in Newark, New Jersey, in a Jewish family and attended Newark Arts High School before graduating from Weequahic High School in 1940. As a teenager, she was an ardent fan of jazz music. In 1942, she married Alfred Lion, co-founder of Blue Note Records. During the 1940s, Gordon and Lion recorded works by artists such as pianist Thelonious Monk and clarinetist Sidney Bechet. In 1949, she married Max Gordon, owner of the owner of the Village Vanguard club in New York City.

jazz advocacy and thelonious monk

Gordon became a public mouthpiece for Thelonious Monk, passionately advocating for his genius to the jazz world. She secured Monk's first gig at the the Village Vanguard in September 1948, though it was not successful as no one attended. She also wrote to Ralph Ingersoll, editor of the newspaper PM, to describe Monk as a 'genius living here in the heart of New York, whom nobody knows'. In her autobiography, she recounted how she fought against record store owners in Harlem who claimed Monk could not play.

village vanguard management

The Village Vanguard, established in 1935 by Max Gordon, was originally a venue for folk music and beat poetry. Under the influence of Lorraine Gordon's artistic direction, it became primarily a jazz music venue in 1957. After Max Gordon's death in 1989, Lorraine assumed ownership and management of the club, maintaining its reputation as a premier jazz club until late 2012. For her contributions to jazz advocacy, she received the NEA Jazz Master Award from the U.S. National Endowment for the Arts in 2013.

memoir and later life

In 2006, Gordon published her autobiographical memoir, Alive at the Village Vanguard: My Life In and Out of Jazz Time, which received the ASCAP Deems Taylor Award for excellence in music print publishing. She died on June 9, 2018, at age 95.
