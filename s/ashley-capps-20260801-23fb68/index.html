Ashley Capps is the founder and head of AC Entertainment, a music promotion company based in Knoxville, Tennessee. He began his career as a music promoter in Knoxville during the 1970s, booking events at off-campus venues and the University of Tennessee.

career history

Capps opened a music club called Ella Guru's in Knoxville in 1988, which he closed in 1990 to focus on forming AC Entertainment. In 1991, he founded the company and also co-founded the alternative weekly newspaper Metro Pulse with Rand Pearson, Margaret Weston, and Ian Blackburn. He is the head of AC Entertainment, which specializes in venue management, event booking, production, marketing, and sponsorships, as well as acting as talent-buyers for several Southeast venues.

festival production

Capps is a co-founder of the Bonnaroo Music and Arts Festival, which he developed following the cancellation of the Hot Summer Nights rock music festival in Knoxville in 1999. Along with Superfly Presents, AC Entertainment produces Bonnaroo, and maintains the Great Stage Park festival ground. The company also produces the Forecastle Festival in Louisville, Kentucky, and the WayHome Music and Arts Festival in partnership with Republic Live. In addition, Capps was the brainchild behind the Big Ears festival in Knoxville, Tennessee.

other ventures

AC Entertainment has produced several other events, including the Sloss Music and Arts Festival from 2015 to 2018, the Mountain Oasis Electronic Music Summit in 2013, and worked on Vegoose in Las Vegas, Nevada, as well as AFROPUNK ATL in Atlanta, Georgia, and Sundown in the City in Knoxville, Tennessee.
