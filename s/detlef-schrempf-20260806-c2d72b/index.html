Detlef Schrempf is a German-American businessman, philanthropist, and former professional basketball player. He played college basketball for the Washington Huskies from 1981 to 1985 and was drafted into the NBA by the Dallas Mavericks in 1985.

basketball career

Schrempf played in the NBA for 16 seasons, with stints at the Indiana Pacers, Seattle SuperSonics, and Portland Trail Blazers. He was a three-time NBA All-Star and an All-NBA Third Team member in 1995. He was also a two-time NBA Sixth Man of the Year. In 1996, he reached the NBA Finals with the SuperSonics, becoming one of only four German-born players to do so. He played for the West German and later German national team in the 1984 and 1992 Summer Olympics and the 1983 and 1985 EuroBasket championships, and was inducted into the FIBA Hall of Fame in 2021.

early life and college

Born in Leverkusen, West Germany, Schrempf attended Centralia High School in Washington state for one year as a senior. He then enrolled at the University of Washington in Seattle, where he played for the Huskies under head coach Marv Harshman. During his college career, he scored 1,449 total points and led the Huskies to Pac-10 regular-season titles in 1984 and 1985. He was inducted into the Husky Hall of Fame in 1995 and named to the University of Washington All-Century Team.

title la own foundation

In 1996, Schrempf and his wife Mari established the Detlef Schrempf Foundation. The non-profit organization supports organizations that provide care and assistance to children and families in the Northwest. Between its founding and 2012, it raised and donated approximately $10 million for children's charities, including Seattle Children's Hospital, Cure Autism Now, and the Seattle Cancer Care Alliance.

post-professional career and popular culture

Since 2010, Schrempf has worked as the Business Development Officer at Coldstream Capital in Seattle. He also served as an assistant coach for the Seattle SuperSonics under Bob Hill in 2006. In popular culture, he has appeared as himself in three episodes of Parks and Recreation and a documentary titled "Mania" (2008), and had a cameo appearance in Married... with Children.
