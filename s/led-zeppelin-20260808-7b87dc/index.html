Led Zeppelin was an English rock band formed in London in 1968. The group consisted of vocalist Robert Plant, guitarist Jimmy Page, drummer John Bonham, and bass-guitarist/keyboardist John Paul Jones. They are among the best-selling music artists of all time, with estimated worldwide record sales of 200 to 300 million.

formation and early history

Led Zeppelin evolved from a previous band called the Yardbirds. Originally named 'the New Yardbirds', they signed a deal with Atlantic Records that granted them artistic freedom. The band's sound combined heavy electric-guitar sounds with elements of folk and blues music, which helped popularize stadium rock and album-oriented rock. They were established as the progenitor of hard rock and heavy metal.

musical career and discography

Over ten years, the band released eight studio albums. Their 1969 debut, Led Zeppelin, was a top-ten album in several countries. Led Zeppelin II (1969) became their first number-one album and is described as their heaviest album. Their untitled fourth album, commonly known as Led Zeppelin IV (1971), is one of the best-selling albums in history with 37 million copies sold. Other notable albums include Led Zeppelin III (1970), Houses of the Holy (1973), Physical Graffiti (1975), Presence (1976), and In Through the Out Door (1979).

writing and recording process

Jimmy Page composed most of the music, while Robert Plant wrote most of the lyrics. John Paul Jones occasionally originated songs with keyboard parts. The recording process for Led Zeppelin II was spontaneous and urgency-driven, as it was recorded in various studios across the UK and North America during tour breaks. In contrast, the untitled fourth album was recorded mostly at Headley Grange, a country house in Hampshire, England, where the relaxed environment allowed the band to try different arrangements and create songs in various styles.

later years and disbandment

During the first half of the 1970s, the band performed record-breaking tours that earned them a reputation for hedonism. However, touring frequency and quality declined in the second half of the 1970s due to Jimmy Page's heroin addiction. The group disbanded after drummer John Bonham died from alcoholism in 1980. The surviving members have only sporadically collaborated for concerts, including the 2007 Ahmet Ertegun Tribute Concert in London.
