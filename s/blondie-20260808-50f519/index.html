Blondie is an American rock band formed in New York City in 1974 by singer Debbie Harry and guitarist Chris Stein. They are considered pioneers of the new wave genre from the mid-1970s CBGB scene, known for an eclectic mixture of musical styles including punk, disco, pop, reggae, funk, and early hip hop.

formation and early career

The band was formed in July 1974 by Debbie Harry and Chris Stein after they parted ways with the Stillettoes. Originally billed as Angel and the Snake for two shows in August 1974, they renamed themselves Blondie and the Banzai Babies before shortening the name to Blondie by October 1974. The name was derived from catcalls directed at Harry, who had bleached her hair blonde. Early members included Billy O'Connor and Fred Smith. Later, drummer Clem Burke and bassist Gary Valentine were recruited. Keyboard player Jimmy Destri was added in November 1975. The group became regular performers at Max's Kansas City and CBGB.

band hiatus and reunion

The band disbanded after the release of their sixth studio album, The Hunter (1982). During this hiatus, Debbie Harry pursued a solo career and acting roles. The group re-formed in 1997 and achieved renewed success with the seventh album No Exit (1999) and the single "Maria" in 1999. They were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2006 and have sold over 40 million records worldwide.
