Otis Redding was an American singer and songwriter known as the "King of Soul." He is considered one of the greatest singers in American popular music history and a seminal artist in rhythm and blues and soul music.

Otis Redding was an American singer and songwriter known as the "King of Soul." He is considered one of the greatest singers in American popular music history and a seminal artist in rhythm and blues and soul music.

early life and influences

Born in Dawson, Georgia, on September 9, 1941, Redding was the first son of Otis Redding Sr. and Fannie Roseman. He sang in the Vineville Baptist Church choir and learned piano and guitar. From age 10, he took singing and drum lessons. His primary musical influences were Sam Cooke and Little Richard, citing Little Richard as his inspiration for entering the music business. At age 15, he left high school to support his family financially because his father had contracted tuberculosis. He worked various jobs including gasoline station attendant and well digger while occasionally playing piano with pianist Gladys Williams' band at the Hillview Springs Social Club.

musical career

Redding's breakthrough occurred in 1958 during a talent contest called "The Teenage Party" hosted by disc jockey Hamp Swain. After winning the contest for 15 consecutive weeks, he was hired by the Upsetters when Little Richard abandoned rock and roll for gospel music. He later toured the Southern United States on the Chitlin' Circuit with Pat T. Cake and the Mighty Panthers. In 1962, an unscheduled appearance at a Stax Records recording session led to a contract and his first hit single, "These Arms of Mine." His debut album, Pain in My Heart, was released two years later. He eventually reached a wider pop audience, performing at venues like the Whisky a Go Go and touring Europe, and performed at the Monterey Pop Festival in June 1967.

otis blue/otis redding sings soul

Released on September 15, 1965, Otis Blue is considered by critics as Redding's first fully realized LP and the definitive soul album of its period. The album was recorded in less than 24 hours across two sessions at the Stax recording studio in Memphis, Tennessee, with backing from Booker T. & the M.G.'s and pianist Isaac Hayes. It features cover versions of contemporary R&B hits, and original material including "Respect," which Redding described as taking a day to write and one take to record. The album was a crossover success, selling more than 250,000 copies and topping the US R&B LPs chart.

death and posthumous legacy

Redding died in a plane crash shortly after recording "(Sittin' On) The Dock of the Bay" with Steve Cropper. Released in January 1968, the song became the first posthumous number-one record on both the R&B and Billboard Hot 100 charts. His album The Dock of the Bay was the first posthumous album to reach number one on the UK Albums Chart. He received many posthumous accolades, including induction into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame, the Songwriters Hall of Fame, and two Grammy Awards. Additionally, his self-penned "Respect" (1965) became widely associated with Aretha Franklin's cover.
