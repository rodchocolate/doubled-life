Mark Ronson is a British and American musician, record producer, songwriter, and DJ. He has won ten Grammy Awards, an Academy Award for Best Original Song, and a Golden Globe.

early life and education

Born on 4 September 1975 in Notting Hill, London, Ronson was raised in a Conservative Jewish household. After his parents' divorce, he moved to New York City at age eight with his mother, Ann, and stepfather, Foreigner guitarist Mick Jones. He attended the private Collegiate School in Manhattan, followed by Vassar College and New York University. In 2008, Ronson obtained American citizenship to vote in the the same year's election.

musical career

Ronson began as a DJ on the local club scene in New York City during the early 1990s. He transitioned to production after being introduced to musicians by Dominique Trenier, manager for Nikka Costa. His debut studio album, Here Comes the Fuzz (2003), was released via Elektra Records but received lukewarm commercial and critical reception. Following this, he co-founded Allido Records in 2004. He gained wider recognition through production work for artists such as Amy Winehouse, Christina Aguilera, and Lily Allen.

major works and achievements

Ronson's second album, Version (2007), peaked at number two on the UK Albums Chart and earned him a Brit Award for British Male Solo Artist. His 2014 single "Uptown Funk" featuring Bruno Mars reached number one on the U.S. Billboard Hot 100 for 14 consecutive weeks and became one of the best-selling singles of all-time. He also co-wrote the song "Shallow" for the film A Star Is Born (2018), which won a Grammy, an Academy Award and a Golden Globe. More recently, he served as lead and executive producer for the soundtrack to the 2023 film Barbie, which won three Grammy Awards and an Academy Award.

family and connections

Ronson was born into one of Britain's wealthiest families in the 1980s, founders of Heron International. He is the nephew of businessman Gerald Ronson. Through his mother, he is distantly related to British Conservative politicians Sir Malcolm Rifkind and Leon Brittan, and Odeon Cinemas founder Oscar Deutsch. He has two younger sisters, twins Charlotte and Samantha, and several half-siblings through his parents' second marriages.
