Talking Heads were an American rock band formed in New York City in 1975. The group consisted of lead vocalist and guitarist David Byrne, drummer Chris Frantz, bassist Tina Weymouth, and guitarist and keyboardist Jerry Harrison. They are described as one of the most acclaimed groups of the 1970s and 1980s and helped pioneer new wave music by blending punk, art rock, funk, and world music.

formation and early years

David Byrne, Chris Frantz, and Tina Weymouth met as freshmen at the Rhode Island School of Design. In 1973, Byrne and Frantz formed a prototype punk band called the Artistics. After the Artistics dissolved in 1974, the trio moved to New York City in 1975 and adopted the name Talking Heads. The name was inspired by a TV Guide issue that described head-and-shoulder shots of people talking as 'all content, no action'. Tina Weymouth took up the role of bassist after the group was unable to find one, learning to play from Suzi Quatro albums. They played their first gig opening for the Ramones at CBGB on June 5, 1975. In November 1976, they signed a recording contract with Sire Records, which allowed them to make music their full-time career.

collaborations and musical evolution

The band's debut album, Talking Heads: 77, was released in 1977 and produced the charting single 'Psycho Killer'. They subsequently collaborated with British producer Brian Eno on the albums More Songs About Buildings and Food (1978), Fear of Music (1979), and Remain in Light (1980). These works blended their art school sensibilities with influences from Fela Kuti and Parliament-Funkadelic, exploring psychedelic funk, Afrobeat and West African polyrhythms. To perform these complex arrangements, the band toured with an expanded group including musicians such as Adrian Belew and Bernie Worrell. From the early 1980s, they included additional recording session musicians like Nona Hendryx and Busta Jones.

commercial peak and later years

Talking Heads reached their commercial peak in 1983 with the U.S. Top 10 hit 'Burning Down the House' from the album Speaking in Tongues. In 1984, they released the concert film Stop Making Sense, directed by Jonathan Demme, and were joined by performances by Alex Weir, Steve Scales, Lynn Mabry and Ednah Holt. Their best-selling album, Little Creatures, was released in 1985. They produced a soundtrack for David Byrne's film True Stories (1986) and their final album, the worldbeat-influenced Naked (1988). David Byrne left the band in 1991, and the remaining members performed as Shrunken Heads before releasing the album No Talking, Just Head (1996) as the Heads. The group eventually disbanded permanently.

critical acclaim

Four of their albums appeared on Rolling Stone's 2003 list of the '500 Greatest Albums of All Time'. Additionally, 'Psycho Killer', 'Life During Wartime', and 'Once in a Lifetime' were included among the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame's 500 Songs that Shaped Rock and Roll. The band was ranked number 64 on VH1's list of the '100 Greatest Artists of All Time' and number 100 on Rolling Stone's 2011 update of the '100 Greatest Artists of All Time'.
