Bud Powell was an American jazz pianist and composer who pioneered the development of bebop. His application of complex phrasing to the piano influenced later pianists such as Barry Harris, Toshiko Akiyoshi, and Walter Davis Jr. He is known for his right-hand-focused approach to the piano.

early life and education

Born in Harlem, New York, in 1924, Powell own a musical family; his father William was a stride pianist and his grandfather Zachary was a flamenco guitarist. He began taking classical piano lessons at age five from William Rawlins, a West Indian man. As an altar boy at the suatu own Catholic church in Harlem, he also learned to play the organ. By age 10, Powell showed interest in swing music, appearing in public for the first time at a rent party where he emulated Fats Waller and Art Tatum. After leaving DeWitt Clinton High School, he shifted toward jazz, mastering James P. Johnson's "Carolina Shout" as his first jazz composition. He also developed an interest in adapting Broadway songs to jazz improvisation.

professional career

Powell's early professional career began as an accompanist for singer Valaida Snow and played with the Sunset Royals. He later joined trumpeter Cootie Williams's band in 1943, where he became the musical director and arranger. While with Williams, Powell requested that the band play Monk's "'Round Midnight", which resulted in the first recording of the tune.

health struggles and institutionalization

In 1945, Powell suffered a severe beating by railroad police in Philadelphia, which led to persistent head wounds and chronic pain. To relieve this pain, he turned to alcohol, developing an addiction that haunted him throughout his life. This resulted in several years of intermittent institutionalizations, including stays at Bellevue Hospital and Creedmoor State Hospital in Queens, New York. He was later released after a jazz aficionado arranged for his medical care to be transferred to someone who allowed him to play piano regularly.

later years and death

In 1959, Powell relocated to Paris, France, to flee racism and barriers to a higher standard of living. He returned to a regular recording schedule and toured Northern and Central Europe. In February 1963, he recorded the studio album 'Bud Powell in Paris' for Reprise, produced by Duke Ellington with financial support from Frank Sinatra. In 1963, he became ill with tuberculosis tuberculosis. After returning to New York, he died in 1966 at age 41 due to ill health and alcohol addiction.
