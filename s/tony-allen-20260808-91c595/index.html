Tony Allen was a Nigerian drummer, composer, and songwriter who co-founded the Afrobeat genre. He served as the musical director of Fela Kuti's band Africa '70 from 1968 to 1979. He is widely regarded by Brian Eno as perhaps the greatest drummer who has ever lived.

early career and influences

Born in Lagos, Nigeria, Allen began playing drums at age 18 while working as a radio station engineer. He was influenced by American jazz, Jùjú music, and the highlife scene in Nigeria and Ghana. To develop his style, he studied recordings of Ghanaian drummer Guy Warren (Kofi Ghanaba) and jazz drummers Max Roach and Art Blakey. Allen's early professional work included playing claves for Sir Victor Olaiya's highlife band, the Cool Cats, before filling the drum set chair. He also played with the Melody Makers, the Nigerian Messengers, and and Agu Norris and the Heatwaves.

collaboration with fela kuti

In 1964, Allen joined Fela Kuti's 'Koola Lobitos' highlife-jazz band. By 1969, he became the musical director of Africa '70, where he and Kuti developed a militant African sound that blended soul, jazz, highlife, and Yoruba polyrhythmic templates. Allen recorded over 30 albums with Fela and Africa '70. He released three solo recordings during this period: Progress (1977), Jealousy (1975), and No Accommodation For Lagos (1979). Allen left the band in 1979 due to tensions regarding royalties, recognition, and the músicasia's surrounding parasites.

later work and global collaborations

After leaving Africa '70, Allen emigrated to London in 1984 and later moved to Paris. His work expanded into genres such as alternative rock, electronic music, dub, Afro-funk, avant-garde hip-hop, and avant-garde hip-hop. He collaborated with Damon Albarn of Blur, joining the project Good, the Bad & the Queen and Rocket Juice & the Moon. He also worked with artists such as Charlotte Gainsbourg, Zap Mama, Flavia Coelho, and Malian singer Oumou Sangaré. In 2017, he released a four-song EP on Blue Note Records titled A Tribute to Art Blakey & the Jazz Messengers. Posthumously, albums such as There Is No End (2021) and The Solution Is Restless were released.
