Béla Fleck is an American virtuoso banjo player known for blending bluegrass with other genres such as jazz. He has won 18 Grammy Awards and has been nominated 41 times. In 2020, he was inducted into the International Bluegrass Music Hall of Fame as a member of New Grass Revival.

Béla Fleck is an American virtuoso banjo player known for blending bluegrass with other genres such as jazz. He has won 18 Grammy Awards and has been nominated 41 times. In 2020, he was inducted into the International Bluegrass Music Hall of Fame as a member of New Grass Revival.

early life and education

Born into a Jewish family in New York City, Fleck was named after three classical composers: Leoñ Janáçek, Anton Webern, and Béla Bartók. He became interested in the banjo after hearing Earl Scruggs play the theme song for The Beverly Hillbillies and 'Dueling Banjos' by Eric Weissberg and Steve Mandell. At age 15, he received his first banjo from his grandfather at a garage sale. While attending the High School of Music & Art in New York City, he played French horn before being transferred to the choir. He studied the book How to Play the Five String Banjo by Pete Seeger and the book Bluegrass Banjo by Pete Wernick, and took lessons from Tony Trischka, Marc Horowitz, and Erik Darling.

musical career

Fleck's early career began with the bluegrass group Tasty Licks and his debut solo album Crossing the Tracks (1979). He later formed Spectrum in 1981 with Jimmy Gaudreau, Glen Lawson, and Mark Schatz. Following this, he performed for nine years with New Grass Revival. He also recorded a solo album, Drive (1987), which was nominated for a Grammy Award for Best Bluegrass Album in 1988. In 1989, he joined the supergroup Strength in Numbers, headed by Sam Bush on mandolin, featuring Jerry Douglas, Mark O'Connor and Edgar Meyer.

béla fleck and the flecktones

Formed in 1988 after an invitation to perform on PBS's The Lonesome Pine Specials, the band combined jazz improvisation with rock, classical, funk, world music traditions and progressive bluegrass. Original members included Béla Fleck, Howard Levy (harmonica and keyboards), Victor Wooten (bass guitar), and Roy 'Future Man' Wooten (Drumitar). The group recorded numerous albums, including Flight of the Cosmic Hippo, which reached number one on the Billboard Top Contemporary Jazz Albums chart. After Levy left in 1992, the band continued as a trio until Jeff Coffin joined in 1997. Coffin left in 2010 to join the Dave Matthews Band, and Levy returned in 2011 for the album Rocket Science. The band announced a hiatus in June 2012.

other projects and collaborations

Fleck has collaborated with various artists, including recording an album of classical music on banjo, Perpetual Motion (2001), with Edgar Meyer. He and Meyer also composed a double concerto for banjo and bass and a trio concerto featuring Zakir Hussain. Fleck premiered the Concerto for Banjo in 2011 with the Nashville Symphony Orchestra. In 2005, he formed the acoustic fusion supergroup Trio! with Stanley Clarke and Jean-Luc Ponty. He is married to banjo player Abigail Washburn, whose debut album Song of the Traveling Daughter was co-produced by Fleck.
