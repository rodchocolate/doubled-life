UK garage (UKG) is an electronic music genre that originated in England during the early to mid-1990s. It is characterized by syncopated hi-hats, cymbals, and snares with percussive, shuffled rhythms. The genre typically features a tempo of around 130 BPM and often incorporates "chopped up," pitch-shifted or time-stretched vocal samples.

origins and influences

UK garage emerged in London during the early 1990s from a blend of New York garage house, R&B, jungle, and Jamaican ragga and dancehall music. It was influenced by rave culture, gospel, Baptist churches, the Black diaspora, and the pirate radio scene. According to MJ Cole, the multicultural nature of London served as a melting pot for young people, which was reflected in the music. In the UK, garage was initially played in second rooms at jungle events, providing a more soulful and sensual sound at 130bpm compared to the harsher techstep influenced sound of jungle. This led to the creation of the 'Sunday Scene,' as promoters could only hire venues on Sunday evenings because Friday and Saturday nights were reserved for faster, more popular jungle and drum and bass events.

speed garage

In 1994, DJ EZ started playing American DJ Todd Edwards' garage house track "The Praise (God in His Hand)" at a speedier 130bpm to align with the tempo of UK hardcore and jungle music. This faster version of garage house became known as speed garage. Speed garage tracks were characterized by rolling snares, reverse-warped basslines, and a sped-up house-style beat. It incorporated elements like sub-bass lines, ragga vocals, and reversed drums. Armand van Helden's 1997 remix of Sneaker Pimps' "Spin Spin Sugar" is credited with breaking speed garage into the mainstream. Todd Edwards' sampling style—using vocal phrases as instruments by reversing or pitch-shifting individual syllables—influenced the emerging genre.

2-step garage and mainstream success

The 2-step garage sound emerged with shuffled beats, contemporary R&B-styled vocals and a different drum pattern where the second and fourth bass kick from each bar were removed. One of the earliest examples bridging speed garage and 2-step was the Kelly G remix of Tina Moore's "Never Gonna Let You Go" in 1997. The genre entered the mainstream in 1999, with tracks like Shanks & Bigfoot's "Sweet Like Chocolate"—the first UK garage track to hit number one in the UK—and Artful Dodger's "Re-Rewind" reaching platinum status. Venues such as Fabric, Ministry of Sound, The Colosseum, Scala and The Gass Club became associated with the genre, and pirate radio stations like Rinse FM, Ice FM, Flex FM and Déjà Vu provided continued support.

evolution and legacy

UK garage encompassed subgenres such as 2-step and speed garage. In the mid-2000s, it was largely subsumed by other styles of music production including dubstep, grime, and bassline. The decline of UK garage in the mid-2000s coincided with the birth of closely related UK funky. UK garage saw a resurgence in the 2010s and a revival in the early 2020s, sometimes referred to as "new UK garage" or "NUKG".
