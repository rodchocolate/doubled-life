The Fania All Stars is a musical group formed in 1968 as a showcase for musicians on Fania Records, the leading salsa music record label of the time.

origins and formation

Fania Records was founded in New York City in 1964 by Jerry Masucci, an Italian-American lawyer, and Johnny Pacheco, a Dominican-born flutist, percussionist, and bandleader. The label's name was inspired by a popular luncheonette in Havana, Cuba, that Masucci had frequented. Fania Records began as a small venture, selling records out of the car trunk of Johnny Pacheco, but grew after the success of Pacheco's recording 'Cañonazo'. This led to the uma expansion of talent base and the acquisition of musicians such as Larry Harlow, Ray Barretto, and Bobby Valentín.

the fania all-stars supergroup

In 1968, Fania Records created a continuously revolving line-up of entertainers known as the Fania All-Stars. Considered some of the best Latin Music performers in the world at that time, the original lineup included band leaders like Johnny Pacheco, Ray Barretto and Willie Colón, singers such as Héctor Lavoe and Ismael Miranda, and special guests including Tito Puente and Eddie Palmieri. The group recorded 'Live at the Red Garter' and later a legendary 1971 performance at New York City's Cheetah Lounge, captured in the documentary 'Our Latin Thing'.

major performances and global reach

The All-Stars performed before more than 40,000 spectators at New York's Yankee Stadium on August 24, 1973. This performance was included in the second set of 50 recordings preserved in the U.S. National Recording Registry. In 1974, they appeared at a music festival in Kinshasa, Zaire, alongside James Brown and others during the heavyweight title fight between Muhammad Ali and George Foreman, which was captured on film as 'Live in Africa'. In 1976, Fania made a deal with Columbia Records for crossover albums, such as 'Delicate and Jumpy', featuring Steve Winwood. In 1979, they participated in the Havana Jam festival in Cuba, cùng với artists like Billy Joel and Weather Report.
