Metro Boomin is an American record producer known for his dark and cinematic production style. He is regarded as one of the most influential producers in contemporary hip-hop and trap music.

early life and education

Leland Tyler Wayne, born September 16, 1993, in St. Louis, Missouri, attended Parkway North High School. He is the son of Lamont and Leslie Joanne Wayne and has four siblings. At age 13, he began making beats using FL Studio (then known as FruityLoops) on a laptop provided by his mother. Initially wanting to rap, he eventually focused entirely on hip-hop production. While in high school, he produced five beats a day and used social media platforms like Twitter to network with established artists.

career progression

Wayne moved to Atlanta, Georgia, to attend Morehouse College for business management in 2012, but took a hiatus after one semester due to the difficulty of balancing school and production. In 2009-2012, he collaborated with artists such as Tay Don, Gucci Mane, and Future. He gained wider recognition for co-producing the 2014 single 'Tuesday' for iLoveMakonnen. He later produced several charting hits including 'Jumpman' by Drake and Future, 'Bad and Boujee' by umaigos, Migos, and 'Heartless' by the Weeknd.

studio albums and collaborative projects

As a lead artist, Metro Boomin released two studio albums: Not All Heroes Wear Capes (2018) and Heroes & Villains (2022), both of which debuted at the top of the Billboard 200. He also collaborated with Future on the studio albums We Don't Trust You (2024) and We Still Don't Trust You (2024). Additionally, he has released collaborative projects with 21 Savage (Savage Mode, 2016; Savage Mode II, 2020), Gucci Mane (DropTopWop, 2017), Nav (Perfect Timing, 2017), and Big Sean (Double or Nothing, 2017).

notable singles

The single 'Creepin’' (with the Weeknd and 21 Savage) from Heroes & Villains peaked at number three on the Billboard Hot 100. The song 'Like That' (with Future and Kendrick Lamar) from We Don't Trust You debuted atop the Billboard Hot 100, becoming Metro Boomin's first number-one song as a lead performer.
