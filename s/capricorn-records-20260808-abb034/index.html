Capricorn Records was an independent record label founded in 1969 in Macon, Georgia, by Phil Walden and Frank Fenter. The label is most recognized for helping launch the careers of Otis Redding and the Allman Brothers Band.

founding and early history

Phil Walden and his brother Alan Walden had managed R&B performers such as Percy Sledge, Al Green, and Sam & Dave in the early 1960s. Following the death of Otis Redding in 1967, Phil Walden sought to establish a recording studio. With funding from Atlantic Records Vice President Jerry Wexler, who suggested adding a record label to the project, the label and studio were named 'Capricorn' after their shared astrological sign. Capricorn Records was established in 1969 with a distribution deal through Atlantic Records.

the 1970s and southern rock

The label became a crucible for the 'Southern rock' subgenre, pioneering and popularizing the genre with acts like The Allman Brothers Band, Wet Willie, Elvin Bishop, and The Marshall Tucker Band. While the Allman Brothers' first album was not a best-seller, their third release, At Fillmore East, became the label's first gold album and is cited by music historians as the impetus for the rise of southern rock. Between 1969 and 1979, Capricorn produced seventeen gold albums, nine platinum albums, and five gold singles.

financial decline and bankruptcy

In 1975, Capricorn entered a new distribution deal with PolyGram after Warner Brothers failed to negotiate a buyout. The label faced financial difficulties during the the 1970s recession, leading to numerous foreclosure and debt charges between July and September 1979. Polygram filed a lawsuit seeking assets as collateral for a $5 million loan from 1977. After an 18-month reorganization process through Chapter 11 bankruptcy proceedings, Phil Walden retained the label name and studio, but Polygram received all master recordings and musical copyrights.

later iterations

In 1983, Walden and Frank Fenter attempted to revive the label, but efforts dissolved following Fenter's death from a heart attack in July 1983. A liquidation sale of assets occurred at the Macon Coliseum in the summer of 1986. The label was relaunched in Nashville, Tennessee, in 1991 via a joint venture with Warner Brothers. This second incarnation signed acts such as Widespread Panic, Gov't Mule, and Kenny Chesney, and eventually moved to Mercury Records.
