Dubstep is a genre of electronic dance music that originated in South London in the early 2000s. Its roots trace back to the Jamaican sound system party scene of the early 1980s and it emerged as an offshoot of UK garage, blending 2-step rhythms and sparse dub production.

characteristics

Dubstep is generally characterized by syncopated rhythmic patterns, prominent basslines, and a dark tone. It is typically instrumental and often uses a minor key or the Phrygian mode. The tempo is usually between 132–142 beats per minute, with a clap or snare on every third beat in a bar; because of this, the genre is sometimes referred to as "140". A notable feature is the 'wobble bass' (or 'wub'), where a low-frequency oscillator is used to manipulate a synthesizer's parameters to create rhythmic variations in volume, filter cutoff, or distortion. Some tracks also incorporate 'bass drops', inherited from drum and bass, where percussion pauses before resuming with more intensity and dominant sub-bass.

history and growth

The sound began to be promoted at the London nightclub Plastic People during the "Forward" night and on the pirate radio station Rinse FM in 2001. The term 'dubstep' first appeared in 2001, and was used by labels Big Apple and Tempa from 2002 to describe remixes distinct from grime and 2-step. BBC Radio 1 DJ John Peel started playing the genre in 2003, and later Mary Anne Hobbs launched her "Dubstep Warz" show in January 2006. Dubstep entered mainstream British popular culture in late 2005 and early 2006, aided by websites like dubstepforum, Barefiles, and blogs such as gutterbreakz, as well as coverage in publications like The Wire and Pitchfork. Towards the end of the 2000s and into the early 2010s, it became more commercially successful in the UK, and its influence was noted in pop artists' work.

variants and offshoots

AllMusic describes the sound as "tightly coiled productions with overwhelming bass lines and reverberant drum patterns, clipped samples, and occasional vocals." Other variants include chillstep, a softer and more melodic offshoot that incorporates ambient and chill out music. The harsher electro-house and heavy metal-influenced variant brostep, led by American producers such as Skrillex, boosted the genre's popularity in the United States. Additionally, post-dubstep is an umbrella term for styles influenced by the same sparse rhythms and heavy sub-bass of the UK dubstep scene, but with a more minimal, mature sound featuring soulful vocals and piano. Post-dubstep was associated with artists like James Blake and Mount Kimbie.
