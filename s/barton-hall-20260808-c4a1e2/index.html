Barton Hall is an on-campus field house at Cornell University in Ithaca, New York. It serves as the site for Cornell Police, ROTC offices and classes, and the indoor track facilities of the university.

building and design

Designed by Lewis Pilcher, the official State Architect of New York, Barton Hall was built in 1914 and 1915 as a drill hall for the Department of Military Science. The building is constructed from local limestone with double trusses spaced 40 feet apart to support the roof. Its original drill shed provided 362 x 228 feet of open floor space, which was capable of accommodating 1,000 men. For a long time, it was considered the largest unpillared room in existence, with an interior covering almost 2 acres.

university functions and history

Originally known as the 'New Armory', the building was named for Col. Frank A. Barton in January 1940. It served as an airplane hangar during World War I and as an armory during World War II. Historically, it hosted Cornell Basketball from 1919 to 1990 and hosted graduation ceremonies for both Cornell University and Ithaca High School until 1974. Until the start of online registration, students would register for classes by placing punched cards into bins in the hall. In the first part of the 20th century, mandatory drill for male students was conducted here. The Cornell Big Red Marching Band and Pep Band were based here until 2013.

current uses

Barton Hall currently houses the three ROTC Detachments (Army, Navy, and Air Force) and an indoor challenge course. It also contains the Wortham Museum, the Hart Memorial Library, basketball courts, and a premier indoor track facility featuring a 200 m Recaflex track with eight 42-inch lanes. The facility includes a throwing cage, high jump areas, and pits for long and triple jump, as well що як an indoor runway for pole vault. In 1995, the H. Hunt Bradley Track Center was constructed under the south bleachers, providing a weight room, library, and Hall of Fame.

concerts and protests

Barton Hall is used as a concert venue for the Cornell Campus via the Cornell Concert Commission. It has hosted various artists such as Bob Dylan, Santana, Ludacris, Death Cab For Cutie, Franz Ferdinand, Daya, Saint Motel, and the Flaming Lips. A May 8, 1977, performance by the Grateful Dead is widely considered one of the band's career highlights and was selected for preservation in the National Recording Registry at the Library of Congress in 2011. The building has also been the site of political protests, including a student takeover in 1969 and anti-war protests in 1972.
