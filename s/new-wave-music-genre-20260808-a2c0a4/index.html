New wave is a music genre encompassing pop-oriented styles that emerged in the United States and United Kingdom in the mid- to late 1970s. It was initially synonymous with punk rock but later branched off into its own distinct style characterized by a lighter, more melodic broadening of punk culture.

characteristics and etymology

The term 'new wave' was popularized by Seymour Stein of Sire Records as a catch-all for styles emerging after punk rock, alluding to the French New Wave film movement. The genre is characterized by a humorous, quirky pop approach, the use of electronic sounds, and a distinctive visual style in fashion and music videos. Musically, it features choppy rhythm guitars with angular riffs, fast tempos, keyboards, synthesizers, and jerky rhythms with stop-start song structures. Vocalists often sound high-pitched, geeky, and suburban, while fans and acts like Talking Heads, Devo, and Elvis Costello adopted a nervous, nerdy persona involving robotic dancing and suits with big glasses.

history and influence

New wave commercially peaked from the late 1970s to mid-1980s, with MTV's launch in 1981 heavily promoting new wave acts in the United States. In the Kingdom, the original movement was considered over by the early 1980s, succeeded by New Romantic and new pop movements. US popularity declined by the late 1980s as genres like alternative rock, dance-pop, and glam metal gained success. The genre influenced later internet microgenres such as synthwave, vaporwave, chillwave, new rave, bloghouse and devocore. It also inspired subgenres like darkwave and minimal wave.

regional scenes and crossovers

New wave developed regional scenes across Europe, including Yugoslav new wave, Germany's Neue Deutsche Welle, the Netherlands' ultra, Spain's La Movida Madrileña, and coldwave in France, Belgium, and Poland. Some acts embraced a crossover of pop and rock with African and African-American styles; for example, Adam and the Ants and Bow Wow Wow used Burundi-style drumming, while Talking Heads' album Remain in Light was marketed as a melding of new wave and African styles.

forerunners

The genre was influenced by the Velvet Underground, David Bowie, Iggy Pop, Brian Eno, and the glam and art rock style of Sparks and Roxy Music. Other influences include experimental rock artists like Frank Zappa, Captain Beefheart and the Residents, underground psychedelic bands such as Lothar and the Hand People, and Germany's kosmische musik scene and krautrock, specifically Kraftwerk. Additionally, the visual aesthetic was influenced by avant-garde art movements like Cubism, Dada and the Bauhaus school.
