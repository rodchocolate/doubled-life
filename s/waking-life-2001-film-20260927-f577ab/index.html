Waking Life is a 2001 American adult animated surrealist drama film written and directed by Richard Linklater. The movie explores philosophical issues such as consciousness, free will, the meaning of life, and the nature of reality and dreams.

plot

The film follows an unnamed young man who experiences a series of dreamlike realities and engages in philosophical discussions with various characters, including scholars, artists, and restaurant-goers. As the protagonist realizes he is living in a perpetual dream broken by false awakenings, he begins to converse more openly with other characters before eventually despairing about being trapped. His final conversation with a character played by Richard Linklater reveals that reality may be only a single instant interpreted as time; that dreams offer a glimpse into the infinite nature of reality; and that freedom from the illusion of life is achieved by accepting God's invitation to become one with the universe. The film concludes with the protagonist levitating into the sky until he disappears.

production

The live-action footage was shot over six weeks starting in August 1999 using a Mini DV camera by Richard Linklater and Palotta. The film's visual effect was created through rotoscoping, an animation technique where animators overlay live-action footage with animation that approximates the original images. This process was primarily produced using Rotoshop, a program designed specifically for the production by Bob Sabiston, on Apple Macintosh computers. Because Linklater employed a variety of artists, the movie's feel continually changes to create a surreal shifting dreamscape. Wiley Wiggins, who plays the protagonist, also served as one of the animators.

reception

Waking Life received critical acclaim and won several awards, including the National Society of Film Critics award for Best Experimental Film and the New York Film Critics Circle award for the Best Animated Film. On Rotten Tomatoes, it has an approval rating of 81% based on 145 reviews. While Roger Ebert gave the film four stars and included it on his list of 'Great Movies', some critics like J. Hoberman and Frank Lovece called the film a 'bull session' or 'pedantic navel-gazing'. In 2018, Richard Linklater Linklater addressed the inclusion of Alex Jones in the film, stating he thought he was funny at the time.
