30 for 30 is a term associated with a documentary series on ESPN, a song by SZA and Kendrick Lamar, and a .30-30 Winchester cartridge. The ESPN series focuses on sports history, while the song is a collaboration between the two American artists, and the cartridge is a small-bore sporting rifle cartridge.

espn documentary series

30 for 30 is a series of documentary films airing on ESPN, its sister networks, and online that highlights interesting people and events in sports history. The idea began in 2007 from ESPN.com columnist Bill Simmons and ESPN's Connor Schell. The series was originally designed as 30 one-hour films by 30 filmmakers to celebrate ESPN's 30th anniversary in 2009. Volume I premiered in October 2009 and chronicled stories from the "ESPN era," starting with the network's founding in 1979. The series has since expanded to include audio podcasts, 30 for 30 Shorts, and Soccer Stories. The entire archive of films and shorts became available on ESPN+ on April 12, 2018.

sza and kendrick lamar song

Released on January 7, 2025, "30 for 30" is a single from SZA's reissue album, Lana. The song was written by SZA, Kendrick Lamar, Bobby DeBarge, Greg Williams, Anthony Jermaine White, and Michael Uzowuru. It features a spoken-word sample from the song "I Call Your Name" by the R&B/funk band Switch. The track debuted at the top of the US Hot R&B Songs chart, breaking the record for the most number-ones by a woman on that chart for SZA. It reached number 10 on the Billboard Hot 100 following a joint performance by the artists at the 2025 Super Bowl LIX halftime show.

winchester cartridge

The .30-30 Winchester, also known as .30 WCF or 7.62×52mmR, was first marketed in 1895 for the Winchester Model 1894 lever-action rifle. It was one of the United States' first small-bore sporting rifle cartridges designed for smokeless powder. The cartridge is most common in lever action rifles and is often used for hunting big game in North America, such as whitetail, mule deer, pronghorn, caribou, elk, moose, and black bear. Due to the majorité of rifles chambered in .30-30 being lever-action with tubular magazines, most cartridges are loaded with round-nose or flat-nose bullets for safety to prevent the primer of the cartridge ahead of it from being set off during recoil.
