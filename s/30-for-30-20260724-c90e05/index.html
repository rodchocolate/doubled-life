30 for 30 is a sports documentary film series airing on ESPN, its sister networks, and online. The series highlights interesting people and events in sports history.

background and origins

The series was conceived in 2007 by Connor Schell and Bill Simmons, an ESPN.com columnist and founder of Grantland.com. The title '30 for 30' was chosen because the series began as 30 one-hour films by 30 filmmakers to celebrate ESPN's 30th anniversary in 2009. Volume I premiered in October 2009 and focused on stories from the 'ESPN era', starting with the network's founding in 1979. Subsequent volumes and online shorts expanded the series' scope beyond this era.

series structure and content

The series consists of 157 episodes across four volumes, each containing 30 episodes, along with a 13-episode series under the ESPN Films Presents title (2011-2012) and a series of 30 for 30 Shorts. It has also expanded to include audio podcasts and Soccer Stories, which aired before the 2014 FIFA World Cup. Since April 12, 2018, the entire archive of films and shorts has been available on the direct-to-consumer platform ESPN+.

reception and accolades

While the series had a slow start with the first film, Kings Ransom, receiving poor ratings, viewership grew as critical acclaim increased. For example, the seventh episode, The U, reached over 2 million viewers. The series has received various accolades, including a 2010 Peabody Award and a 2014 Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Short-Format Nonfiction Program. Additionally, the film O.J.: Made in America won an Academy Award for Best Documentary Feature in 2016.

sponsorship

The series is presented by sponsors Cadillac and Levi's. Cadillac's name appears on the logo, and Levi's 'go forth' slogan is displayed during directors' interstitial comments. Commercials for both companies are shown during intermissions, with Levi's guaranteed a 60-second commercial slot at the start of the third act. Cadillac replaced Honda as a primary sponsor.

facts

  • Production Company: ESPN Films
  • Original Release Date: October 6, 2009
  • Network: ESPN, ESPN2, ABC, ESPN Deportes, and ESPN+
