The Grateful Dead was an American rock band formed in Palo Alto, California, in 1965. They are known for their eclectic style fusing elements of rock, blues, jazz, folk, country, bluegrass, rock and roll, gospel, reggae, and world music with psychedelia.

formation and early years

The band began as the Warlocks in early 1965, emerging from remnants of a Palo Alto jug band called Mother McCree's Uptown Jug Champions and members of the Wildwood Boys. Their first show as the Warlocks was on May 5, 1965, at Magoo's Pizza Parlor in Menlo Park. They later changed their name to Grateful Dead—a phrase found in a dictionary—after discovering another band used the name Warlocks. Their first performance under this new name took place on December 4, 1965, at one of Ken Kesey's Acid Tests. The group's participation in these tests was considered crucial to their improvisational vocabulary and bonding as a band.

musical style and performance

The Grateful Dead are considered the pioneering godfathers of the jam band world due to their range of influences and the structure of their live performances. They are famous for improvisation during live shows, which they encouraged fans to tape. The band's devoted fan base is known as "Deadheads". Despite having only one Top-40 single, "Touch of Grey" (1987), they remained among the highest-grossing American touring acts for decades.

title: discography and recordings

The band's discography includes more than 200 albums, most of which were recorded live. Between 1965 and 1995, they released thirteen studio albums and nine contemporary live albums. Their debut studio album, titled The Grateful Dead, was released by Warner Bros. Records on March 17, 1967, and was primarily recorded in Los Angeles at RCA's Studio A. In later years, the band began releasing retrospective live albums through series such as Dick's Picks, Road Trips, and Dave's Picks.

members

The founding members were Jerry Garcia, Bob Weir, Phil Lesh, Bill Kreutzmann, and Ron "Pigpen" McKernan. With the exception of Pigpen, who died in 1973, the remaining founding members stayed with the band until their 1995 split. Subsequent members included Mickey Hart, Robert Hunter, Tom Constanten, Keith Godchaux, Donna Godchaux, Brent Mydland, and Vince Welnick.
