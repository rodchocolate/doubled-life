Dr. Dre is an American record producer, rapper, and record executive. He is a key figure in the crafting and popularization of West Coast G-funk, a subgenre of hip-hop characterized by slow, heavy production and a synthesizer foundation.

signature sound and techniques

Dr. Dre's production style evolved from the generic electro-hop of the World Class Wreckin' Cru to the development of G-funk. His later work, specifically on the album 2001, expanded on this sound with sparse beats and a reduced use of samples. His recording process involves starting with a drum machine in a room of trusted musicians who are on call; he programs a beat and then has musicians play along, isolating and refining the sound when he hears something he likes.

landmark records

The Chronic (1992) was Death Row's first major project and established Dre's solo career, becoming one of the best-selling American music artists of 1993. Doggystyle (1993) was the debut album of Snoop Dogg, which Dre produced. The album 2001 (1999) was a follow-up to The Chronic and expanded on the G-funk sound, going six times platinum in the US and being acclaimed by critics for its production.

collaborators and lineage

Dre has mentored producers such as Warren G and Daz Dillinger. He has signed and produced for artists such as Eminem, 50 Cent, The Game, Kendrick Lamar, Anderson .Paak, and Busta Rhymes. He has worked with co-producers like Mel-Man and musicians such as Scott Storch on keyboards and Mike Elizondo on bass guitar.

canonical status

Dr. Dre is considered canonical due to his influence on the West Coast hip-hop scene and his role in the popularity of gangsta rap through N.W.A. He has won seven Grammy Awards, including Producer of the Year, Non-Classical, and Rolling Stone ranked him number 56 on the list of 100 Greatest Artists of All Time.
