Sean Paul Ryan Francis Henriques is a Jamaican rapper, singer and songwriter. He gained international fame with his second album, Dutty Rock, released in 2002.

early life and education

Born in Kingston on 9 January 1973, Paul was raised as a Catholic but attended the Jewish private school Hillel Academy. His mother, Frances, is of English and Chinese Jamaican descent, and his father, Garth Henriques, has Afro-Jamaican ancestry and descendants from Portuguese Sephardic Jews. He trained in commerce at the College of Arts, Science, and Technology (now University of Technology) with the goal of pursuing a swimming career. Before entering music, he worked as a bank teller and enrolled in a hotel-management program to learn French cuisine basics.

musical career

Paul's musical journey began in 1994 with an appearance in the 'Nice Time' music video by Carrot Jetter. He collaborated with manager and producer Jeremy Harding, recording the song 'Baby Girl' over a rhythm track. After gaining local attention, Paul released his debut album Stage One in 2000 through VP Records. In 2001, the single 'Gimme the Light' became a worldwide smash hit, leading to a record deal with Atlantic Records in a joint venture with VP Records. His second album, Dutty Rock (2002), was a commercial breakthrough and the first dancehall song to top the Billboard Hot 100 with 'Get Busy'.

chart success and discography

Paul has achieved multiple US number-one singles, including 'Get Busy' and 'Temperature', which is widely regarded as his signature song. His third album, The Trinity (2005), debuted at number 5 on the Billboard 200 and went Platinum in the United States. He has also released albums such as Imperial Blaze (2009), Tomahawk Technique (2012) and Full Frequency (2014). titles and awards

Most of his albums have been nominated for Grammy Awards for Best Reggae Album, with Dutty Rock winning the award. He also won an American Music Award for '(When You Gonna) Give It Up to Me', beating Kanye West and Nick Lachey.
