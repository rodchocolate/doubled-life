Max Gordon is a name shared by several individuals, including an American theater and film producer, a professional racing driver, and founders of jazz clubs or trade union leadership. Other notable figures with this name include a Lithuanian nihilist and anarchist, and a South African Trotskyist activist.

american theater and film producer

Max Gordon (1892–1978) was born Mechel Salpeter and adopted the stage name of his brother, Cliff. After forming a vaudeville agency with Albert Lewis, he became one of New York's most successful producers from the Roaring Twenties through the Eisenhower years. His notable productions include The Jazz Singer (1925–1926), Born Yesterday (1946), and My Sister Eileen. Several of his works were adapted into popular films, such as The Solid Gold Cadillac (1956) and Born Yesterday (1950). He is also mentioned in Cole Porter's 1934 musical song 'Anything Goes'.

professional racing driver

Max Gordon (born 2008) is an American professional racing driver nicknamed 'Mad Max'. The son of Robby Gordon, he began driving at age three and started karting in 2014. He competes primarily in off-road racing, including the Baja 1000, the Mint 400, and Stadium Super Trucks (SST). In 2017, he became the youngest person to compete in the Baja 1000 at age nine, though his finish was officially classified as a retirement due to age restrictions. He has achieved wins in the SST series, including victories at Long Beach and Mid-Ohio Sports Car Course.
