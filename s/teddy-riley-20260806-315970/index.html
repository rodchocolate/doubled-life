Teddy Riley is an American record producer and songwriter credited with the creation of new jack swing, a fusion genre of hip-hop and R&B. He is the founder of musical groups Guy and Blackstreet, and has worked with numerous high-profile artists including Michael Jackson and Bobby Brown.

early life and musical beginnings

Riley was raised in St. Nicholas Houses in Harlem, New York City, where he began playing instruments in church from the age of five. He spent much of his time growing up in a studio built by his uncle, who owned The Rooftop club in Harlem. By age 15, he had begun working with artists such as Doug E. Fresh. Under the guidance of producer Gene Griffin, Riley formed the short-lived group Kids at Work, and at age 18, he produced Kool Moe Dee's crossover hit " kind of a crossover hit, reaching number 89 on the Billboard Hot 100 in 1986.

career and group foundations

In 1987, Riley formed the R&B group Guy with Aaron Hall and Timmy Gatling. Guy is credited as a pioneer of new jack swing, a sound developed and mainstreamed by Riley. The group released two platinum albums, Guy and The Future, before splitting up in 1991. In late 1991, Riley formed Blackstreet, which produced several major hits including the number one single "No Diggity". Following the group's various disbandments and reformations, as of 2019, the lineup consists of Riley, Dave Hollister, Sherman "J-Stylz" Tisdale, and Rodney Poe under the name "Blackstreet 2".

production work and influence

Riley's influence on contemporary R&B production introduced further usage of rapping segments, sound effects, and samples. He is also credited with popularizing the modern use of the talk box vocoder and discovering Pharrell Williams in 1991. His production credits include four Billboard Hot 100 number one singles: "I Like the Way (The Kissing Game)" by Hi-Five, "Stutter" by Joe, "My Prerogative" by Bobby Brown, and "No Diggity" by Blackstreet. He also co-produced half of Michael Jackson's album Dangerous, which is considered the most successful new jack swing album, and produced songs for Lady Gaga, and artists in the Eastern pop market including Exo, Girls' Generation, and Super Junior.

personal and professional challenges

In 2008, Riley became bankrupt after falling victim to a Ponzi scheme. That same year, in June, a fire destroyed his Virginia Beach recording studio, which was insured for $336,000. In 2010, while working on the posthumous album Michael by Michael Jackson, the music he produced for the project was questioned by some; Riley insisted all songs were sung by Jackson, though later admitted there were some elements of his own voice in the music.
