Public Image Ltd (PiL) is an English post-punk band formed in June 1978 by lead vocalist John Lydon, guitarist Keith Levene, bassist Jah Wobble, and drummer Jim Walker. While the group's line-up has changed frequently over the years, Lydon has remained the sole constant member.

formation and early career

Following his departure from the Sex Pistols in January 1978, John Lydon sought an experimental 'anti-rock' project. He recruited Jah Wobble, a school friend from Hackney who was a natural talent on bass guitar, and guitarist Keith Levene, whom Lydon had met while touring with the Clash. Jim Walker, a Canadian student, joined as the drummer after answering a Melody Maker advertisement in May 1978. The band began rehearsing in mid-May 1978 and was officially named 'Public Image' (with 'Ltd' added upon company incorporation in July 1978) after a Muriel Spark novel. Their debut single, 'Public Image', reached number 9 on the UK Singles Chart.

musical evolution and discography

PiL released their debut studio album First Issue (1978), which featured an abrasive, bass-heavy sound influenced by dub, noise, progressive rock, and disco. Their second album, Metal Box (1979), is regarded as one of the most important albums of the post-punk era, featuring hypnotic dub reggae bass lines and bleak vocals. The third album, The Flowers of Romance, is also cited as an influential post-punk record. By 1984, the band had transitioned into a solo vehicle for Lydon, moving toward a more accessible sound with commercially successful albums such as This Is What You Want... This Is What You Get (1984) and Album (1986).

the 'album' project

Released on 27 January 1986, the studio album Album was recorded in New York in late 1985. In a departure from previous releases, Lydon used an all-star cast of session musicians including Steve Vai, Ginger Baker, and Ryuichi Sakamoto. The album's artwork was deliberately generic, a pastiche of generic brand products, with the release being named after its format (e.g., 'Compact Disc', 'Cassette'). This approach was later used on other releases such as the 7" single 'Single' and the 12"-inch single '12-inch Single'. Lydon described the album as being 'almost like a solo album'.
