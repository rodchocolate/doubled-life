Aretha Franklin's recordings for Atlantic Records began in 1967 following her departure from Columbia Records. These recordings were characterized by a shift toward blues and gospel-inflected R&B music, known as soul music, under the direction of producer Jerry Wexler.

Aretha Franklin's recordings for Atlantic Records began in 1967 following her departure from Columbia Records. These recordings were characterized by a shift toward blues and gospel-inflected R&B music, known as soul music, under the direction of producer Jerry Wexler.

recording process and production

Franklin's first Atlantic recording sessions took place in January 1967 at FAME Studios in Alabama, where she recorded 'I Never Loved a Man (The Way I Love You)' with the Muscle Shoals Rhythm Section, also known as the 'Swampers'. Due to a physical altercation between her manager Ted White and the studio owner, recording of the B-side 'Do Right Woman, Do Right Man' was canceled. The track was eventually completed at Atlantic Recording Studios in New York with the Muscle Shoals musicians and Franklin's sisters, Erma and Carolyn. Following this, on February 14, 1967, Franklin recorded 'Respect', a cover of an Otis Redding song, which she rearranged and reshaped to provide a woman's perspective. She played piano for the number, and the overall arrangement was by co-producer Arif Mardin based on the ideas Franklin brought in.

the hit single 'respect'

Released on April 10, 1967, as the second single from her debut Atlantic album I Never Loved a Man the Way I Love You, 'Respect' became an immediate hit. It reached number one on the Billboard Hot 100 for two weeks and won Franklin two Grammy Awards at the 1968 ceremony. It has since been heralded as Franklin's signature song and a protest anthem connected to the civil rights movement of the 1960s and the second-wave feminist movement of the 1970s. The song was inducted into the Grammy Hall of Fame in 1987 and the Library of Congress' National Recording Registry in 2002.

atlantic era successes

Franklin's breakthrough hit 'I Never Loved a Man' reached number nine on the Billboard Hot 100 and peaked at number one on the Top Selling R&B Singles chart, which was her first of 20 number one hits on that chart. The compilation album Aretha's Greatest Hits, released in September 1971, featured tracks such as 'Chain of Fools', 'I Say a Little Prayer', '(You Make Me Feel Like) A Natural Woman', and 'Do Right Woman – Do Right Man'.
