André Cymone is an American record producer, songwriter and bassist born in Minneapolis, Minnesota. He is regarded as an unsung architect of Minneapolis' identity as a music capital and was a bass guitarist for Prince's touring band before launching a solo career.

early life and musical beginnings

Cymone was the youngest of six children born to Fred Anderson, a musician, and Bernadette Anderson, a social worker. In 1975, he was a member of the funk band 94 East with Prince. He also played in the band Grand Central, which later changed its name to Champagne due to confusion with Graham Central Station, and included members Morris Day and Cymone's sister Linda.

solo career and production work

Cymone released three early solo albums: Livin' in the New Wave (1982), Survivin' in the 80's (1983), and A.C. (1985). These albums explored a fusion of new wave and funk, producing half a dozen hit R&B singles. His most successful single, "The Dance Electric," was written by Prince and reached #10 on the R&B charts in 1985. After stepping away from the spotlight in the late 1980s to raise his children, he returned to music with a digital single titled "America" in 2012 and released the albums The Stone (2014) and 1969 (2017).

production and collaborations

Cymone became a producer for several artists, including his then wife Jody Watley, producing hit songs like "Looking for a New Love" and "Real Love". He also produced and wrote songs for Pebbles, Jermaine Stewart, Evelyn "Champagne" King, The Girls, Pretty Poison, and Adam Ant. For Adam Ant's 1990 album Manners & Physique, Cymone provided production and a Minneapolis sound. Additionally, he wrote songs featured on the Beverly Hills Cop II soundtrack in 1987, performed by James Ingram and Pebbles. In 2024, he provided engineering and arrangement for track 12 of Vanessa Williams' album Survivor.
