Jake Holmes is an American singer-songwriter and jingle writer born on December 28, 1939. He began his recording career in the 1960s and is known for writing the song "Dazed and Confused".

musical career

Holmes' first musical foray was with his wife Katherine in the folk pop parody duo, Allen & Grier. Following military service, he recorded two well-regarded albums for EMI's Tower Records label: "The Above Ground Sound" of Jake Holmes and A Letter to Katherine December. He later signed with Polydor, recording an album called Jake Holmes and his most successful solo album, So Close, So Very Far to Go (1970), which reached No. 135 on the Billboard album chart. He also recorded the album How Much Time for Columbia Records.

collaborations

Holmes collaborated with Bob Gaudio of The Four Seasons on the 1969 Genuine Imitation Life Gazette album and Frank Sinatra's 1970 Watertown album. He also worked with Harry Belafonte, who recorded Holmes' songs on several albums including The Warm Touch (1971), Play Me (1973), Loving You Is Where I Belong (1981), and the full album Paradise in Gazankulu (1988). Additionally, Nina Simone recorded cover versions of "For a While" from the Watertown album on her albums Nina's Back and Live And Kickin' in 1985.

advertising jingles

In the late 1970s, Holmes moved into writing advertising jingles for HEA Productions. He earned the nickname "Jingle Jake" for his work on campaigns such as "Be All That You Can Be" for the US Army, "Aren't You Hungry for Burger King Now?" (1981), and "We'll Take More Care Of You" for British Airways. He also co-wrote the "Most Original Soft Drink Ever" jingle for Dr Pepper with Randy Newman. Other clients included Chevrolet, Pan American World Airways, and IBM.

dazed and confused

Holmes wrote the song "Dazed and Confused" in 1967, which appeared on his debut album. The song was reworked by the Yardbirds after Holmes opened for them at a Greenwich Village gig in August 1967. It was later recorded by Led Zeppelin on their debut album without attribution. This led to multiple copyright infringement lawsuits filed by Holmes against guitarist Jimmy Page starting in 2010, which were settled out of court in January 2012 and again in August 2025.
