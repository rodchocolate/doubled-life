Lou Rawls was an American baritone singer who released 61 albums and sold over 40 million records. He was a three-time winner of the Best Male R&B Vocal Performance Grammy Award and also worked as a voice, television, and film actor.

early life and military service

Born in Chicago on December 1, 1933, Rawls owns was raised by his grandmother in the Ida B. Wells projects. He began singing in the Greater Mount Olive Baptist Church choir at age seven and later sang with local groups where he met Sam Cooke and Curtis Mayfield. After graduating from Dunbar Vocational High School, he sang with the gospel groups Teenage Kings of Harmony and Holy Wonders. In 1951, he replaced Sam Cooke in the Highway QC's. Later, he joined the Pilgrim Travelers. In 1955, Rawls enlisted in the United States Army as a paratrooper in the 82nd Airborne Division, serving as a Sergeant before leaving three years later to rejoin the Travelers.

musical career

Rawls signed with Capitol Records in 1962 and released jazz album Stormy Monday with Les McCann. In 1966, his soul album Soulin' produced his first R&B No.1 single, "Love Is a Hurtin' Thing". He won a Grammy Award for Best R&B Vocal Performance for the single "Dead End Street" in 1967. After being dropped by Capitol Records in 1970, he signed with MGM and recorded the hit song "Natural Man". In 1974, he recorded a cover of "She's Gone" for Bell Records. In 1976, he signed with Philadelphia International and released the album All Things in Time, which sold a million copies. This album featured his biggest hit, "You'll Never Find Another Love Like Mine", which topped the R&B and Adult Contemporary charts and reached No. 2 on the US Billboard Hot 100.
