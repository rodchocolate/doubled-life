Robert Alexander "Bumps" Blackwell (May 23, 1918 – March 9, 1985) was an American record producer, songwriter, arranger, and bandleader. He is most recognized for his work with Little Richard during the early stages of his career.

early career and specialty records

Blackwell began his career leading a jazz group in the late 1940s that included Quincy Jones and Ray Charles. After moving to Hollywood, California, he took a job as an arranger and producer at Art Rupe's Specialty Records. While there, he produced work for Guitar Slim, Larry Williams, Lloyd Price, and Sam Cooke. He also oversaw the production of Little Richard's rise to stardom in 1955 and 1956.

work with little richard

Blackwell produced Little Richard's breakthrough hit "Tutti Frutti" after hearing him sing the song in the studio. He also co-wrote and produced several other mid-1950s hits, including "Rip It Up", "Ready Teddy", "Long Tall Sally", and "Good Golly Miss Molly". These songs became rock and roll standards that were later covered by artists such as Creedence Clearwater Revival, the Beatles, and Elvis Presley. Additionally, Blackwell produced the twelve tracks on Little Richard's debut studio album, Here's Little Richard, which was released in 1957.

later career and management

From 1959 to 1963, Blackwell served as the West Coast A&R director for Mercury Records, where he produced gospel recordings for Little Richard. During this time, Little Richard toured Europe with the Beatles acting as his opening act. Blackwell eventually became Little Richard's manager and continued working with him into the 1970s. In 1981, he produced songs for Bob Dylan's album Shot of Love, including the title track. In 1985, he issued a single called "Give it Up - Pay Little Richard".
