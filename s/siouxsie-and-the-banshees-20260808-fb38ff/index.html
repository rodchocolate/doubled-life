Siouxsie and the Banshees were a British rock band formed in London in 1976 by vocalist Siouxsie Sioux and bassist Steven Severin. They are described as post-punk pioneers who evolved from the punk scene to create music characterized by daring rhythmic and sonic experimentation. The group released 11 studio albums and 30 singles during their career before disbanding in 1996.

formation and early years

Siouxsie Sioux and Steven Severin met in September 1975 and became part of a group of teenagers known as the 'Bromley Contingent' who followed the Sex Pistols. In September 1976, they performed an improvised 20-minute set based on 'The Lord's Prayer' at the 100 Club Punk Festival. After recruiting drummer Kenny Morris and guitarist Peter Fenton, the band's first television appearance was in November 1977 on Tony Wilson's show So It Goes. Following the firing of Peter Fenton, John McKay took his place as guitarist in July 1977.

musical evolution and success

The band signed with Polydor in June 1978 and released their first single 'Hong Kong Garden', which reached the top 10 in the UK. Their debut album, The Scream, was met with widespread critical acclaim. Following membership changes, including the addition of John McGeoch and Budgie, they became one of the most successful alternative pop groups of the 1980s. Their third album Kaleidoscope (1980) peaked at number 5 on the UK Albums Chart, while Juju (1981) reached the top 10 and influenced the emerging gothic scene.

later career and north american breakthrough

The band achieved a breakthrough in North America with the album Peepshow (1988), which received critical praise. They also achieved a mainstream hit in the US in 1991 with the pop single 'Kiss Them for Me'. Throughout their career, they released 11 studio albums and 30 singles, with Siouxsie and Severin remaining the only constant members. The band disbanded in 1996.
