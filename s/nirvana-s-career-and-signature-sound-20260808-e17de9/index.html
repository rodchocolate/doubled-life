Nirvana was an American grunge band co-founded by Kurt Cobain and Krist Novoselic in 1987. The group became a worldwide phenomenon following the release of their 1991 album Nevermind, which brought mainstream attention to the music of Seattle.

career overview

Nirvana established themselves as part of the Seattle grunge scene in the late 1980s, releasing three albums between 1989 and 1993. The band's first album, Bleach, was released in 1989. Following a period of variety in their lineup, they signed with DGC Records and recorded Nevermind at Sound City Studios in Los Angeles. This album became a worldwide commercial success and launched the band as a phenomenon up through their hit single "Smells Like Teen Spirit". The group's career abruptly ended in 1994 following the death of Kurt Cobain.

signature sound and genre

Nirvana is associated with grunge, a genre that fuses elements of punk rock and heavy metal, featuring distorted electric guitar sound, bass guitar, drums, and vocals. Grunge incorporates influences from indie rock bands such as Dinosaur Jr., Pixies, and Sonic Youth. Lyrics are typically introspective and angst-filled, often addressing themes of social alienation, addiction, psychological trauma, and a desire for freedom. While some musicians, including Kurt Cobain, loathed the word "grunge", others like Jeff Stetson noted that prominent bands in the genre, including Nirvana, all sound different.
