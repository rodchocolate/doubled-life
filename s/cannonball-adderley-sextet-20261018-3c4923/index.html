The Cannonball Adderley Sextet was a jazz ensemble led by alto saxophonist Julian "Cannonball" Adderley. The group evolved from an earlier, more successful second quintet featuring his brother, Nat Adderley, on cornet.

The Cannonball Adderley Sextet was a jazz ensemble led by alto saxophonist Julian "Cannonball" Adderley. The group evolved from an earlier, more successful second quintet featuring his brother, Nat Adderley, on cornet.

overview and personnel

The Cannonball Adderley Sextet included noted musicians such as saxophonists Charles Lloyd and Yusef Lateef, pianists Bobby Timmons, Barry Harris, Victor Feldman, Joe Zawinul, Sérgio Mendes, Hal Galper, Michael Wolff, and George Duke, bassists Ray Brown, Sam Jones, and Walter Booker, and drummers Louis Hayes and Roy McCurdy. The group's sound was characterized by inventive and melodic music, and Cannonball Adderley became known for his wryly humorous comments between songs to maintain a close rapport with the audience.

the cannonball adderley sextet in new york

The group's recording debut was the live album The Cannonball Adderley Sextet in New York, recorded at the Village Vanguard on January 12 and 14, 1962. This album featured performances by Cannonball Adderley, Nat Adderley, Yusef Lateef, Joe Zawinul, Sam Jones, and Louis Hayes. Critics have described the recording as indispensable, and it served as a springboard to prominence for pianist Joe Zawinul.

The group's personnel changes over time reflected the broader jazz scene. Yusef Lateef joined the band only three weeks prior to the recording of their New York live album, and Joe Zawinul's mastery of bop and the blues fused with the rhythm section of Sam Jones and Louis Hayes.
