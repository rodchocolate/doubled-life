Donna Summer (1948–2012) was an American singer and songwriter known as the "Queen of Disco". She gained global prominence during the 1970s disco era and is recognized as one of the world's leading female singers.

early life and career

Born in Boston to Andrew and Mary Gaines, Summer was the third of seven children. She began her performance career at age ten at a church, later performing in school musicals at Jeremiah E. Burke High School. In 1967, she joined the blues rock band Crow, but the group dissolved after a record label passed on signing the same group because they were only interested in Summer. In 1968, she moved to Munich, Germany, to play the role of Sheila in the musical Hair, and eventually became fluent in German. She also participated in the musicals Show Boat, Godspell, and Ich bin ich. After moving to Vienna, Austria, joined the Vienna Volksoper.

rise to fame and disco era

In Munich, Summer met producers Giorgio Moroder and Pete Bellotte. In 1974, she released her first album, Lady of the Night, for the European market. Following the recording of "Love to Love You Baby", she signed with Casablanca Records in 1975. Her first three Casablanca albums—Love to Love You Baby, A Love Trilogy and Four Seasons of Love—all went gold in the US. She became known as the "First Lady of Love" and later achieved a number one hit with "I Feel Love", which is hailed as one of the most important records in pop music history. Summer's first six albums were recorded in Munich with Moroder and Bellotte before she relocated to the United States.

later career and label transitions

Summer's success was interrupted by a social backlash against disco in the late 1970s. In 1980, she left Casablanca Records for Geffen Records, where she recorded rock, new wave, and inspirational music inspired by her Christian faith. She later returned to the top of the pop charts in 1983 with "She Works Hard for the Money" on Mercury Records. After a series of label changes including Atlantic and Warner Bros., she remained signed to those labels until the early 1990s. Her final album was released by Sony's Burgundy Records in 2008.

legacy and honors

Summer's work on "I Feel Love" is described as the start of electronic dance music. She has been inducted into the Hollywood Walk of Fame, the Dance Music Hall of Fame, and the National Rhythm & Blues Hall of Fame. In 2013, she was posthumously inducted into the the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame, and in 2025, she was inducted into the the Songwriters Hall of Fame.

facts

  • Global Record Sales: Over 100 million records sold worldwide
  • Billboard 200 Achievements: First and only artist to have three number one double albums
  • Chart Success: First female artist to record three number one singles in one calendar year (1979)
  • Cause of Death: Lung cancer
  • label associations: Casablanca Records, Geffen Records, Mercury Records, Atlantic Records, Warner Bros. Records, Epic Records, Burgundy Records
