Dancehall is a genre of Jamaican popular music that originated in the late 1970s. It refers to both a music and dance style, and was not officially named until the 1980s. The genre is characterized by its use of Jamaican Patois and a focus on track instrumentals known as riddims.

Dancehall is a genre of Jamaican popular music that originated in the late 1970s. It refers to both a music and dance style, and was not officially named until the 1980s. The genre is characterized by its use of Jamaican Patois and a focus on track instrumentals known as riddims.

origins and development

Dancehall emerged in the late 1970s among lower and working-class people from Kingston's inner city who were unable to participate in uptown dances. It was originally called Bashment music and grew out of political turbulence and social upheaval. In contrast to roots reggae, which sought international recognition, dancehall focused on the day-to-day realities of average Jamaicans, often observing society in a provocative, gritty, and vulgar manner. Themes of social injustice and Rastafari movement themes were replaced by lyrics about sexuality, violence, and dancing. Because radio was conservative and failed to the play 'people's music', sound systems filled this gap, providing accessibility to music for those who could not afford radios.

musical characteristics and evolution

Initially a more sparse version of reggae, dancehall evolved with the prevalence of digital instrumentation in the 1980s. This led to the creation of 'ragga', which is characterized by artistically faster rhythms and deejays Toasting over digital (electrical) rhythms. Early dancehall used prerecorded rhythm tracks or 'dub' from reggae songs of the 1960s and 1970s. The genre also pioneered the concept of remixing. Production level and sound system quality were critical to the industry, as sound systems allowed people to listen to music without having to buy a radio.

title la own

The term 'Dancehall' originally referred to physical open-air venues where DJs and Toasters performed original mixes and songs via sound systems. These venues allowed performers to to come to the people in inner city communities. The culture grew as technology and sound systems improved, and women were able toconfront gender ideologies to enact change through the use of video light. The genre saw initial mainstream success in Jamaica in the 1980s, became popular in diaspora communities in the 1990s, and experienced worldwide mainstream in the 2000s. By the 2010s, it began to heavily influence Western artists and producers.

notes

Prominent figures in the own music scene include Vybz Kartel (known as 'King of Dancehall') and Spice (known as the 'Queen of Dancehall'). Vybz Kartel is known for his provocative lyrics and has been an inspiration for Western artists like Drake. Vybz Kartel and Spice collaborated on the controversial 2009 single 'Romping Shop', which was banned by the Jamaica Broadcasting Corporation due to its explicit lyrical content.
