The concept of Germany as a distinct region can be traced back to Julius Caesar's time. Germanic tribes' victory in the Battle of the Teutoburg Forest prevented Roman annexation, although parts of the Rhine were under Roman control. Following the fall of the Western Roman Empire, the Franks conquered other West Germanic tribes, and after the division of the Frankish Empire in 843, East Francia became the Kingdom of Germany. The Holy Roman Empire was established in 962 with Otto I as its first emperor.

During the High Middle Ages, the Hanseatic League dominated German port cities along the Baltic and North Seas. The Investiture Controversy saw German emperors resist Church authority. Martin Luther led the Protestant Reformation in 1517, dividing Germany into Protestant and Catholic states. The Thirty Years' War (1618-48) brought significant destruction to the Holy Roman Empire, leading to the Peace of Westphalia where regional dukes gained autonomy.

The Napoleonic Wars led to the dissolution of the Holy Roman Empire in 1806 and the establishment of the German Confederation. The Industrial Revolution modernized Germany's economy, leading to urban growth and the socialist movement. Prussia unified Germany into the German Empire in 1871. World War I resulted in Germany's defeat and loss of territories. The Weimar Republic was established after the war but fell under Nazi rule in 1933, leading to World War II and the Holocaust. Post-war division led to East and West Germany until reunification in 1990.

Germany's prehistory includes evidence of early human presence dating back over a million years. Significant finds include the oldest complete set of hunting weapons from Schöningen, wooden spears from Bilzingsleben, and the oldest uncontested figurative art in Europe, the Löwenmensch figurine. The Neolithic period saw the Linear Pottery culture and the use of wheels and wagons around 3400 BC.
