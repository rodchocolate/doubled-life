lavenue

['Lavenue was a cookery school in London that played a significant role in the culinary education and career of Agnes Marshall, an influential English chef.', "Marshall renamed Lavenue to the Marshall's School of Cookery after acquiring it in 1883, transforming it into one of the leading culinary schools in London."]

Lavenue was originally a cookery school founded by Felix Lavenue (the pseudonym of Charles Shepperd) in 1864. It was renamed Marshall's School of Cookery after Agnes Marshall and her husband Alfred acquired it in 1883. The school became renowned for teaching high-end English and French cuisine.

The original Lavenue cookery school, located at 67 Mortimer Street, was managed by Agnes Mary Lavenue until its sale in 1883. After the purchase, the Marshalls moved the school to 30 Mortimer Street and expanded its offerings. The school's curriculum included specialized classes taught by experts from various culinary backgrounds.

Under Agnes Marshall's leadership, Marshall's School of Cookery grew rapidly in popularity and became one of the most prestigious cookery schools in London. It played a crucial role in Marshall's career development and contributed to her reputation as an influential culinary entrepreneur.
