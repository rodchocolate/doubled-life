lavenue

Lavenue was a cookery school located at 67 Mortimer Street in Marylebone, London. It was founded by Felix Lavenue, the pseudonym of Englishman Charles Shepperd, who died in 1864.

After Felix Lavenue's death in 1864, the school was run by his daughter, Agnes Mary Lavenue. The Marylebone rate books show that 67 Mortimer Street was scheduled for demolition in 1883. A few months after selling the school, Agnes Mary Lavenue died in Tunbridge Wells.

In January 1883, Agnes and Alfred Marshall acquired the Lavenue cookery school. Although the original records of the transaction have not survived, later evidence suggests that the couple purchased the school together. This was unusual at the time, as women had only very recently earned the legal right to purchase property through the Married Women's Property Act 1882.

Soon after the purchase, the Marshalls moved the school down the road to 30 Mortimer Street and renamed it Marshall's School of Cookery. Agnes's half-brother John was employed as the school's manager, and her half-sister Ada worked for a time as the housekeeper.
