toyota 4runner

The Toyota 4Runner is an SUV manufactured by the Japanese automaker Toyota and has been marketed globally since 1984 across six generations. Originally, it was a compact SUV that resembled a Toyota Hilux pickup truck with a fiberglass shell over the bed. The name '4Runner' was created as a play on the term 'forerunner,' where the '4' indicated the vehicle's four-wheel drive system and 'Runner' referenced its all-terrain capabilities.

The 4Runner is sold primarily in North America and South America as of 2021. In markets that do not offer the 4Runner, such as Europe, Asia, and the Middle East, Toyota provides the Land Cruiser Prado instead. For some markets, the 4Runner was branded the Hilux Surf, which was later replaced by the Fortuner, a lower-cost alternative based on the Hilux platform.

The original 4Runner concept drew inspiration from vehicles like the second generation Ford Bronco and the Chevrolet K5 Blazer. The first generation (N60; 1983) was developed by modifying an existing short-bed Toyota Hilux, adding removable fiberglass shells over the rear sections and bench seats. This modification process allowed the vehicle to be more than simply a conventional pickup truck with a shell added.

The concept of third-party recreational vehicle conversions predates the 4Runner; for example, in 1981–1983, Winnebago Industries built the Trekker conversion on the short-bed Toyota Hilux. The discontinuation of the Trekker occurred when Toyota began producing and selling the 4Runner SUV, suggesting that these third-party conversions served as marketing test vehicles.

The 4Runner has demonstrated longevity in use; a 2019 study by iSeeCars.com found that 3.94% of 4Runners had accumulated mileage exceeding 200,000 miles (320,000 km). The vehicle's name was created by copywriter Robert Nathan for the Saatchi & Saatchi advertising company.
