music in film on screen

A film score is original music specifically composed to accompany a film or television program, enhancing the dramatic narrative and emotional impact of scenes. This music consists of cues—orchestral, instrumental, or choral pieces—that are timed to specific moments during the film's runtime. Scores can encompass diverse styles, ranging from Western classical music to jazz, rock, pop, blues, ambient, and various world music genres.

Film scores are written by composers under the guidance of or in collaboration with the film's director or producer. While many scores are orchestral works rooted in Western classical music, they can also incorporate influences from jazz, rock, pop, blues, new-age, ambient, and various ethnic styles. Since the 1950s, electronic elements have become more common, leading to modern scores often featuring a hybrid of orchestral and electronic instruments. Although songs like pop or rock tracks are usually considered part of the film's soundtrack rather than the score, some musicals may feature songs based on thematic ideas from the score.

The creation process involves several stages, beginning with 'spotting,' where the composer watches an unpolished 'rough cut' and discusses required styles and tones with the director. During spotting, precise timing notes are taken to determine how long each cue must be and when it should coincide with specific moments in a scene. Alternatively, composers may write music based only on scripts or storyboards without seeing the film itself. Another key process is 'syncing,' which involves aligning dramatic events on screen with musical events using methods like SMPTE timecode or free timing.

A film score is known by several names, including background score, background music, film soundtrack, original soundtrack (OST), film music, screen composition, and incidental music. The term 'soundtrack' is generally reserved for media like live theatre, television, radio programs, or video games; for these, the music is typically called the soundtrack or incidental music.
