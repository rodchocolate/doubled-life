quincy jones

Quincy Jones (1933–2024) was an American record producer, composer, and arranger whose seven-decade career bridged jazz, pop, film scoring, and television. He is best known as the production genius behind Michael Jackson's most successful albums—Off the Wall (1979), Thriller (1982), and Bad (1987)—and for producing the global charity single 'We Are the World' (1985).

Jones came to prominence in the 1950s as a jazz arranger and conductor before transitioning into pop production. He studied under Nadia Boulanger in Paris, who advised him to 'deal with your roots,' grounding his sophisticated arrangements in jazz and R&B traditions. As a producer, he served as the creative architect behind Michael Jackson's metamorphosis from teen idol to global phenomenon, beginning with Off the Wall (1979). His work spanned swing through soul, bebop through hip-hop, reflecting a deep versatility across American popular music.

Jones produced three of Michael Jackson's most successful albums: Off the Wall (1979), which began Jackson's transformation; Thriller (1982), which became the highest-selling album of all time; and Bad (1987), whose first-week sales exceeded the rest of the Top 30 combined. In 1985, he produced and conducted 'We Are the World,' raising funds for famine victims in Ethiopia. He also produced pop hits for Lesley Gore in the early 1960s, including 'It's My Party.'

Jones backed jazz legends including Billie Holiday, Ella Fitzgerald, Duke Ellington, Count Basie (whom he called 'daddy'), Thelonius Monk, Miles Davis, and Charlie Parker. He served as musical director for Frank Sinatra in the mid-1960s and arranged for Dizzy Gillespie and Ray Charles, producing Charles's first album for Atlantic Records. His collaborations extended to Chaka Khan and Donna Summer.

Jones received 28 Grammy Awards, a Primetime Emmy Award, and a Tony Award, with nominations for seven Academy Awards and four Golden Globe Awards. He composed film scores for The Pawnbroker (1965), In the Heat of the Night (1967), In Cold Blood (1967), The Italian Job (1969), The Wiz (1978), and The Color Purple (1985). He won the Emmy for Roots (1977) and a Tony for producing the 2016 revival of The Color Purple. Named one of the most influential jazz musicians of the 20th century by Time, he received posthumous honors including the Academy Honorary Award in 2025.
