me at the zoo

A zoo is a facility where animals are kept within enclosures for exhibition and often bred for conservation purposes. The term 'zoological garden' refers to zoology, the study of animals, and the abbreviation 'zoo' was first used in relation to the London Zoological Gardens. Historically, zoos have evolved from ancient menageries into modern scientific institutions.

The term for a zoo is derived from the Ancient Greek ζῷον, zōion, meaning 'animal,' combined with the suffix -λογία, '-logia,' meaning 'study of.' The abbreviation 'zoo' first appeared in print in the United Kingdom around 1847. While initially known as menageries or 'zoological forests,' newer terms such as 'conservation park' or 'bio park' have been adopted by some professionals to distance institutions from older, criticized concepts.

The concept of keeping exotic animals is ancient; the oldest known zoological collection dates back to c. 3500 BCE in Hierakonpolis, Egypt. Throughout history, zoos served various purposes—from symbolizing royal power (such as King Louis XIV's menagerie at Versailles) to providing educational exhibits. The modern zoo emerged in the 19th century when major European cities began establishing public zoological gardens aimed at educating the general population using scientific methods.

Today, zoos are facilities that exhibit animals and often breed them for conservation purposes. Despite their role in conservation efforts, animal welfare organizations have expressed criticism regarding housing conditions and the impact of visitor behavior. In response to these concerns, efforts have been made over recent decades to improve conditions within zoological institutions.

Separately, 'morning zoo' is a format for English-language radio shows characterized by its spontaneous, playful, and irreverent interactions among hosts. This concept was first conceived in 1981 by Scott Shannon and Cleveland Wheeler at WRBQ-FM in Tampa, Florida, and quickly spread across the United States.
