german history

German history spans over two millennia, from prehistoric settlements to modern-day Europe's most populous nation. This timeline explores key events, figures, and cultural developments that have shaped Germany.

The earliest human presence in Germany dates back over a million years ago with the discovery of Homo heidelbergensis. The first distinct German culture emerged during the Iron Age, around 500 BC, with the arrival of Celtic tribes. Germanic tribes later dominated the region, eventually forming the basis for modern German identity.

The Frankish Empire's division in 843 led to the establishment of East Francia, which became the Kingdom of Germany in 911. Otto I was crowned Holy Roman Emperor in 962, marking the beginning of the Holy Roman Empire. This period saw significant cultural developments, including the rise of the Hanseatic League and the Northern Crusades.

The Protestant Reformation, sparked by Martin Luther's Ninety-Five Theses in 1517, divided Germany religiously. The Thirty Years' War (1618-48) devastated the Holy Roman Empire, but ultimately led to the Peace of Westphalia, which established modern sovereignty and diplomacy.

Prussia's rise in power culminated in the unification of Germany under Otto von Bismarck in 1871. Germany's expansionist policies led to its involvement in World War I, resulting in defeat and significant territorial losses. The interwar period saw the rise of Adolf Hitler and the Nazi Party, leading to World War II and the Holocaust.

Divided during the Cold War, Germany was reunited in 1990 following the fall of communism. Today, it is a major economic powerhouse and a key player in European politics.
