me at the zoo

A zoo, short for zoological garden, is a facility where animals are kept within enclosures for exhibition and often bred for conservation purposes. The term derives from the Ancient Greek words for animal and study of. The abbreviation 'zoo' was first used in reference to the London Zoological Gardens, which opened for scientific study in 1828.

The term zoological garden refers to zoology, the study of animals. The word is derived from the Ancient Greek ζῷον (zōion), meaning 'animal', and the suffix -λογία (-logia), meaning 'study of'. The abbreviation 'zoo' first appeared in print in the United Kingdom around 1847 when it was used for the Clifton Zoo. It became popular approximately twenty years later through the rhyming song 'Walking in the Zoo' by music-hall artist Alfred Vance. Relatively new terms that emerged in the late 20th century include 'conservation park' and 'bio park', with the latter being first coined by the National Zoo in Washington D.C. in the late 1980s.

The predecessor of the zoological garden is the menagerie, which has a long history from ancient times to the modern era. The oldest known zoological collection was revealed during excavations at Hierakonpolis, Egypt in 2009, dating to approximately 3500 BCE. By the 19th century, major European cities established public zoological gardens, transitioning from princely menageries designed to entertain high society into facilities focused on educating the general population along modern scientific lines. The oldest zoo still in existence is the Schönbrunn Zoo in Vienna, Austria, constructed in 1752.

The first modern zoo was the Tierpark Hagenbeck by Carl Hagenbeck in Germany. In the United States alone, zoos are visited by over 181 million people annually. However, animal welfare organizations have expressed criticism of zoos and the conditions in which animals are kept, raising concerns about housing conditions and the effects of visitor behavior on animal welfare. In recent decades, efforts have been made to improve conditions in zoos, with some institutions adopting new names like 'wildlife conservation parks' to distance themselves from the criticized concept of 19th-century zoos.
