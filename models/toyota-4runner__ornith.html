toyota 4runner

The Toyota 4Runner is a four-wheel-drive SUV manufactured by the Japanese automaker Toyota and marketed globally since 1984. Originally derived from the Toyota Hilux pickup truck with an added fiberglass shell, it has evolved into a standalone vehicle positioned between compact and mid-size SUVs. The name "4Runner" was created as a play on "forerunner," referencing its four-wheel drive system and all-terrain capabilities.

The 4Runner evolved from third-party recreational vehicle conversions of the Toyota Hilux. In 1981, Winnebago Industries produced a conversion called the Trekker, which removed the rear wall of the short-bed Hilux to add a bench seat and fiberglass shell. Approximately 1,200 to 1,500 Trekkers were built before being discontinued in 1984 when Toyota began producing the 4Runner. The first-generation 4Runner (N60), introduced in 1983, was mechanically nearly identical to the Hilux 4x4, featuring a removable fiberglass canopy and rear seats added behind the B-pillar.

The name "4Runner" was coined by copywriter Robert Nathan for Saatchi & Saatchi advertising. Toyota states that the "4" refers to the four-wheel drive system, while "Runner" highlights its all-terrain capabilities. As of 2021, the 4Runner is sold primarily in North and South America. In markets where it is not offered, such as Europe and Asia, consumers receive the Land Cruiser Prado instead.

All 4Runners are built in Japan at Toyota's Tahara plant or the Hino Motors plant in Hamura. In a 2019 study by iSeeCars.com ranking the longest-lasting vehicles in the US, the 4Runner placed fifth, with 3.94% of units reaching over 200,000 miles.
