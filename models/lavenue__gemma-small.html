lavenue

Lavenue was the name of a cookery school that Agnes Marshall and her husband acquired in 1883. The original school, which was situated at 67 Mortimer Street, was founded by Felix Lavenue, who used this name as a pseudonym for Englishman Charles Shepperd. In 1883, the couple purchased the school from his daughter, Agnes Mary Lavenue.

The Marshall's School of Cookery, which operated at 67 Mortimer Street and later moved to 30 Mortimer St., was a major culinary institution. It taught a mixture of high-end English and French cuisine and became one of only two major cookery schools in the city, alongside The National Training School Of Cookery. Agnes Marshall's husband, Alfred, also operated other businesses through the school, including an agency that supplied domestic staff, and a food shop selling flavorings, spices, and syrups.

Agnes Bertha Marshall acquired the Lavenue cookery school in January 1883. The original founder was Felix Lavenue, who used this name as a pseudonym for Charles Shepperd, and he died in 1864. In 1883, the school was being run by his daughter, Agnes Mary Lavenue. Following the purchase, the Marshalls moved the school down the road to 30 Mortimer St. The couple purchased the school together, which was notable as women had only recently gained the legal right to purchase property through the Married Women's Property Act 1882.

Marshall published her first book, *The Book of Ices*, in 1885. This publication contained 177 different ice cream and dessert recipes and was self-published through the cookery school. The book also promoted Marshall's inventions, including the Marshall's Patent Freezer. Other innovations included an "ice-breaking machine," an "ice cave" (an insulated box for storing ice cream), and several kitchen appliances and food ingredients.
